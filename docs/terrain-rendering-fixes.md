# Terrain and cloud fixes

This records the September 2026 bug pass for Certified runtime-generation Terrain3D worlds. It supplements the [runtime-generation checklist](terrain3d-runtime-generation-checklist.md); it does not close the remaining M6 performance and human visual acceptance items.

## Changes

- **Near terrain appearance:** the old shader made steep dry ground as smooth as roughness 0.55. Gameplay now defaults to dry roughness 0.92, with a small slope adjustment, and wet roughness 0.35. Local rain wetness drives darkening, roughness, and specular response. Snow does not accumulate liquid wetness. The grave-key Terrain3D panel persists dry roughness, wet roughness, and rain wetness strength. Active, staged, and reused native terrain owners receive the same values. Color-map alpha continues to represent alpine biome weight.
- **LOD transitions:** runtime proxy triangles and parent interpolation use the surface lattice's positive diagonal. Their custom vertex payload morphs to the parent's full 3D triangle, including curvature and differing underlay offsets. Water and retained terrain keep their existing topology. Proxy coverage accounts for the inner Terrain3D discard region and keeps the full transition disk covered by uploaded terrain. The pinned Windows Terrain3D addon now uses the matching local diagonal for its standard grids; debug and release binaries are rebuilt. The near shader also evaluates small ring angles with a stable polynomial: native GPU sine precision was shifting vertices on steep surfaces. See [native patch provenance](../third_party/Terrain3D/PROVENANCE.md).
- **Coasts:** new recipes use `ringworld_surface_coastal_v2`, with a steeper nearshore profile and a compressed shelf-to-basin transition. The preview exposes beach slope, approximate shelf width, shelf depth, and continental slope width. The coast derivative is evaluated symmetrically over a landform-scale interval; a small interval amplified floating-point noise into metre-scale steps and was rejected during walking validation.
- **Cloud noise:** normalized blue-noise time avoids repeating the same texture slice at 60 FPS with motion set to 60. Gameplay additionally varies samples by render callback. Fractional color reprojection and coherent depth metadata allow the selected history weight to accumulate noisy samples while retaining depth and offscreen rejection.
- **Far terrain occlusion:** a GPU compositor reads resolved opaque depth from the scaled far viewport, reconstructs physical ray distance, and supplies it to the cloud compute and post-composition passes. Sky has zero/invalid depth. No CPU readback occurs during gameplay. Resize and shutdown retire descriptors and textures safely.
- **Window requests:** supported stationary players retain the active terrain anchor. This avoids requesting the previous window immediately after a handoff. Directional prefetch, reversal, and recovery from unsupported positions remain active.
- **Structural wall streaming:** predicted wrapped sector indices are placed on the nearest periodic copy beside the current sector before constructing the wall strip. Previously, a prediction across coordinate zero could span almost the full ring, causing enormous worker allocations and expensive mesh installation. Wall detail and physical geometry remain at their configured settings.
- **Global terrain rebuilds:** one background worker samples each segment using an independent sampler and captured geometry/edit state. Mesh construction and publication remain on the main thread. Canceled work leaves its queue entry intact; edits and origin changes invalidate stale preparations. Existing terrain stays visible during replacement, including partition changes. Unchanged quality settings no longer rebuild the ring, and an across-detail change preserves the structural hull and occluders.

## Coast scale and compatibility

There is no universal distance at which coastal water becomes deep. USGS profiles report roughly 6 m depth at 342 m offshore of the +1 m contour at Myrtle Beach, versus 849 m at Waites Island. The chosen game profile favors a quicker descent rather than copying either beach. [USGS beach profiles](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1206/html/change3.html).

The default one-dimensional profile reaches 2 m depth at 90 m and 6 m at 280 m, then 150 m at an estimated 12 km shelf edge. It approaches the default 4,500 m basin over the next 30 km. These are profile distances, not an exact signed-distance field: coastal curvature, continental noise, and domain warping affect measured transects. The 12 km shelf is a deliberate gameplay compression; a NOAA-hosted coastal-ocean review describes a worldwide average shelf width of about 75 km. [Coastal-ocean review, page 3](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/39955/noaa_39955_DS1.pdf).

Frozen v1 recipes retain their original field shape, math, hash, saves, and terrain edits. V2 has four additional required fields and a distinct generator identity. The normal preview save path is now `user://terrain_generation_preview/recipe_v2.tres`; existing worlds do not adopt a new recipe on load. Runtime world generator contract v17 and surface-only height limits remain unchanged.

## Validation

The native terrain GPU test checks shader parameters on active, staged, and reused owners as well as transformed physical support. Headless runs explicitly skip only renderer-dependent parameter checks. Settings tests cover persistence, reset, and clamping.

The proxy seam contract exercises the actual runtime parent-chord helper with fractional nonplanar edits, different parent/fine underlays, both triangle halves, and floating-origin transforms. Separate radial morph and surface-definition tests cover retained topology and the runtime lattice.

The global-preparation contract drives the production worker and mesh publisher. It verifies deferred publication, cancellation without losing queue progress, replacement coverage across repeated partition changes, unchanged quality and hull/occluder reuse, and detached-worker shutdown. A Certified v2 fixture compares sampled vertices, colors, and water classification under transformed, anchored placement; fractional edits must reach the actual published mesh. A separate late-ring edit checks the 128-segment mapping and wrapped neighbors, including the integer-overflow regression. These checks pass with a clean .NET build.

The rendered diagonal fixture compares native GPU depth to actual physics rays on an 8 m spike over a 2 m lattice. The initial center mismatch was about 2.98 m; correcting the diagonal left a 0.22 m curvature error. With both fixes, all 15 sampled rays agree within 2.55 mm. This is a deliberately steep diagnostic, not a claim that every terrain seam has been visually accepted. The native diagonal patch currently ships for Windows x64 only; other platforms require their own rebuild. Restart the editor after replacing its loaded native addon.

The far-depth GPU fixture measures a known box face at 9,500.27 m, then 9,500.17 m after resize, with zero for sky. The cloud consumer fixture supplies a split depth field: the occluded half has zero cloud alpha and matches a no-cloud final render, while clouds remain visible on the unobstructed half. Gameplay history and the existing menu history fixtures compare captures against accumulated references; high history materially reduces noise but does not make finite-step ray marching noise-free.

In the gameplay fixture, stationary alpha error falls from 0.1033 without history to 0.0533 with high history; moving alpha error falls from 0.1075 to 0.0524. Color errors fall from 0.0526 to 0.0241 and from 0.0550 to 0.0244 respectively. These measurements verify accumulation and approximately halve the measured noise; residual grain remains.

Coast tests check strict recipe identity, hash sensitivity, monotone profile depths, eight shoreline-normal transects, deep-water reaches, and smooth adjacent 2 m samples around spawn. Legacy reference/golden fixtures remain pinned to v1. Reports and captures are generated under `.godot/terrain-generation-tests/`, `.godot/menu_space/`, and `.godot/sunshine_far_depth_gpu/`.

## Walking and remaining frame stalls

A sequential 1920 x 1080 Forward+ run on the RTX 4070 traversed 512 m out and back at 12 m/s through World 1's saved area using an isolated copy of its seed and frozen v1 recipe. All 5,170 support rays hit terrain; there were no walking coverage waits. Its frame intervals were 55.12 ms median, 106.90 ms at the 95th percentile, and 123.93 ms maximum. Slow frames also occurred while terrain jobs were idle, with main-viewport GPU timings around 122 ms and physics around 1 ms. These measurements do not establish that terrain generation explains the remaining frame stalls.

A separate half-resolution cloud diagnostic followed the same route: 5,171 successful support probes, zero walking coverage waits, and 53.85 ms median / 108.04 ms 95th-percentile intervals. Lowering cloud resolution alone did not resolve the expensive main rendering pass. The captured run's first recorded interval included screenshot encoding (582 ms); excluding that measurement artifact gives a 124.60 ms maximum. The fixture now resets the timing baseline after capture. Reports are `m6-4508142/travel-benchmark.json` and `m6-4350183/travel-benchmark.json` beneath `.godot/terrain-generation-tests/`.

The isolated controller regression additionally covers 1,420 m at 60 m/s across handoffs, reversals, diagonal travel, and an immediate post-handoff pause, with no support misses or coverage waits. Its step checks found no unexpected positional jumps; the boundary pause had zero drift. This and the normal-speed route narrow the remaining investigation to frame timing rather than establishing a complete fix for every reported stop. Full-scene performance and human visual acceptance remain open. The existing full-scene shutdown resource warnings also remain; the focused far-depth resize/cleanup fixture passes.

An earlier v2 run (`m6-4246784/travel-benchmark.json`) covers 256 m out and back from the configured origin at 12 m/s, including the circumference wrap on return: 2,601 support probes, zero misses, and zero coverage waits. The report verifies that both the bridge and compositor use the requested half resolution. Its median is 38.63 ms, 95th percentile 96.80 ms, and maximum 436.46 ms. Unlike the earlier capture artifact, the long 290/436 ms intervals occur during travel and correlate with process time while physics remains below 1 ms, reported GPU time is 14–20 ms, and Terrain3D preparation is already ready. The later traces below identify two contributors; reducing cloud resolution alone is not a complete remedy.

A subsequent instrumented route (`m6-4289086`) recorded a 292.60 ms frame containing a 199.03 ms structural-wall completion, with no GC suspension during that interval. Near the zero-coordinate crossing, the old wrapped-index range calculation requested almost a full-ring wall strip. The route allocated 38.01 GB cumulatively, including a roughly 20 GB allocation jump near the seam. After correcting the range (`m6-4282630`), the largest retained structural-completion event was 0.87 ms, the largest requested strip was 176 around cells, and route allocations fell to 14.07 GB. The complete frame maximum fell to 195.20 ms, but p95 remained 97.86 ms; this was one identified cause, not the entire slowdown. Both runs had 2,601 successful support probes and zero coverage waits.

A GPU-timing repeat (`m6-4246735`) measured cloud compute at 0.82 ms mean / 1.31 ms p95 and FFT water compute at 7.32 ms mean / 9.91 ms p95 in retained samples. GPU samples carry delayed source-frame IDs and cannot be summed with a current CPU frame. A separate global-terrain trace (`m6-4339884`) attributed the sustained slow period to the 128-segment quality rebuild: sampling averaged 59.52 ms per segment, mesh construction 7.10 ms, and scene installation 0.068 ms. The old 2 ms service budget was only checked between whole segments. Both comparison configurations allowed the same automatic terrain fallback; these are isolated short-route measurements, not a broad FPS guarantee.

Moving global sampling to its own worker and avoiding redundant structural rebuilds reduced the next 256 m out-and-back run (`m6-4258630`) to 33.91 ms median, 53.83 ms p95, 61.68 ms p99, and 77.10 ms maximum. All 2,601 support probes passed, with zero coverage waits. In retained telemetry, sampling averaged 63.29 ms on the worker, while main-thread mesh construction averaged 7.05 ms. The timing budget cannot interrupt an individual mesh commit: the largest initial-segment service observed by the per-frame counter was 24.95 ms at the higher detail setting.

These timings include progressive distant-terrain preparation. This run began with 15 of 128 global segments installed, completed that initial set during travel, and retained all 128 visible segments when adaptive quality requested replacements. At route end, 45 replacements remained queued. Thus the result demonstrates shorter frame stalls while background work proceeds; it is not a benchmark of an entirely settled scene. Managed allocations and GC also remain substantial (15.90 GB cumulative allocation and 1.29 s cumulative GC pause over the measured 43.31 s).

A longer final run (`m6-4287968`) traveled 512 m outward and back at 12 m/s with the same 1080p, half-resolution-cloud configuration. Its 3,015 measured intervals had 24.53 ms median, 48.74 ms p95, 58.69 ms p99, and 73.28 ms maximum. All 5,161 support rays hit, with zero coverage waits or ungrounded time. The initial 128-segment build finished 23.99 s into measurement. Adaptive quality requested replacements at 34.36 s; all completed at 48.07 s, leaving no global preparation work pending at the end. The remaining 1,607 intervals after this completion had 22.49 ms median / 31.93 ms p95 / 50.25 ms maximum. These later intervals also use the lower adaptive detail setting and a different route portion, so they are not an isolated quality-setting comparison.

That longer run ended with one active and one idle native Terrain3D owner, zero pending sample jobs, a two-worker high-water mark, and 1.97 MB of cached canonical samples under the 8 MiB limit. The final diagnostics snapshot reported about 2.88 GB working set and 112.70 MB managed heap. Cumulative route allocation was 17.76 GB and GC pause time 2.29 s over 85.93 s of measurement. This establishes progress and bounded queues on this route; it does not establish long-session memory stability or the 60 FPS target. Existing full-scene startup/shutdown warnings remain separate from the passing focused contracts.

This fresh-world origin is underwater. Both the existing v1 and new v2 runtime launch paths use fixed horizontal coordinates and settle vertically onto the terrain; neither searches for dry land. The ground-support fixture disables swimming, so this route verifies terrain traversal and wrapping, not swimming or dry-land spawn selection. The comparison through World 1's saved area above provides the separate dry-land route. Player world files were not modified by these fixtures.

## Reproduction

Build the .NET project first. Run the following scripts with Godot's `--path . --script` arguments. Use `--headless` for CPU contracts; native material and cloud tests require Forward+ rendering.

| Script | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `tools/terrain_generation_coastal_test.gd` | V2 profile, transects, strict identity, and local smoothness |
| `tools/terrain_generation_shared_math_test.gd` | Frozen v1 golden comparisons |
| `tools/runtime_surface_definition_test.gd` | Runtime field, bounds, edits, identity, and collision lattice |
| `tools/proxy_seam_geometry_contract_test.gd` | Parent triangles, wrapped placement, and coverage |
| `tools/structural_wall_periodic_range_contract_test.gd` | Bounded wall requests across the ring seam and generated wall geometry |
| `tools/global_terrain_preparation_contract_test.gd` | Worker publication, cancellation, edit revisions, quality changes, and retained coverage |
| `tools/lod_radial_morph_contract_test.gd` | Retained radial morph contracts |
| `tools/terrain3d_gameplay_native_test.gd` | Native publication, physical support, and GPU appearance |
| `tools/terrain3d_diagonal_gpu_test.gd` | Native rendered triangle heights against curved collision rays |
| `tools/surface_terrain_settings_smoke.gd` | Appearance and streaming preferences |
| `tools/terrain3d_gameplay_stream_test.gd` | Window requests, cancellation, caching, and stationary stability |
| `tools/terrain3d_gameplay_movement_test.gd -- --speed=60 --distance=256` | Controller steps, handoffs, reversal, support, and stationary stability |
| `tools/sunshine_far_depth_gpu_test.gd` | Physical far depth, sky, resize, and resource cleanup |
| `tools/menu_cloud_history_gpu_test.gd -- --gameplay-history` | Gameplay history, phase sampling, and far-depth consumption |
| `tools/menu_cloud_history_gpu_test.gd` | Existing menu history regression |
| `tools/terrain3d_gameplay_travel_benchmark.gd -- --speed=12 --distance=512` | Full scene, normal controller, configured spawn, frame times and ground support |

The travel fixture creates an isolated world and preferences under `.godot`. It uses the configured spawn unless explicit `--around` and `--across` coordinates are supplied. `--reference-v1` retains the runtime backend while selecting the older recipe for comparison. `--cloud-resolution=1` applies a test-only half-resolution override, and `--capture` writes start/end screenshots outside the measured interval. `--telemetry` exports CPU events and adds per-frame allocation/GC counters on the same monotonic clock; see [runtime diagnostics](performance_diagnostics.md#correlating-a-walking-hitch). Run performance comparisons sequentially without another game or GPU fixture competing for resources. A passing short route does not establish the 60 FPS target or complete the longer M6 acceptance route.
