## Phase rules

The "Phase rules" of T2.md apply to every order here, in addition to TEMPLATE.md: Claude Code executors (`implementer`, `claude-lead`; `claude-deep` only with the primary's approval), production first, sampling with tolerances (99.5 % and a hard limit), a STOP RULE on every order, no new requirements from inside, the reduced testing cadence, at most five evidence links per report. Added for T3:

- **T3 does not move rivers.** No order changes a centreline, a level profile, a lake level or a lake cell. T3R-3 re-measures the straight-run criterion to show it.
- **A class is a measurement.** The class of a span comes from its danger speed, and a test compares that with the flow field.
- **Commit points** (the primary runs `-Suite gate` and commits): **C4** after T3R-3 (done, `ad9530d`), **C5** after T3R-5 and T3R-7, **C6** at T3R-12.
- **Pictures.** **D1** the danger map (T3R-1, seen by the user). **D2** a swim down a fierce stretch (T3R-5). **D3** visible flow and sound (T3R-8, T3R-10). Then gate G2.
- **Constants** marked **(tune at review)** in the Spec are starting values, not measurements of production code.

Added on 2026-11-13:

- **No version moves.** No open order changes `HydrologyVersions` (`DrainageAlgorithm` 9, `CarveOperator` 10, `GraphFormat` 4) or `LodSectorCacheRiverVersion` (13). T3 from here on reads the graph and adds render-time and runtime data. An order that finds it must change one of them stops and reports. The one exception would be a retune after T3R-3c, which gets its own order (T3R-3d) if the lead decides on it.
- **The realism rule** (user, 2026-11-13) applies to every formula here: no floor, minimum, widening or boost. Where a rule would show or do more than the water would, it is a question for the user first ("Questions for the user (2026-11-13)" under Review notes).
- **Worlds with `water.rivers` and without `water.current` do not change**: their pixels, physics and sound are the T2 state the user passed on 2026-11-13. Every T3 consumer is behind `water.current`.
- **One working tree, one Debug assembly.** At most two code-writing executors at a time, on the disjoint files the table below names. An executor builds only when its own edits compile, and never while another executor has said it is mid-edit in a file of the same assembly that does not compile. Reviews, audio sourcing, captures and report runs can go alongside.
- **The user plays on this PC.** `tools/play.ps1` puts an optimized assembly into the Debug slot until the game exits (H36). Before any build or test: if `.godot\mono\temp\bin\DebugPlaySaved\RingworldTechDemo.dll` exists, or a Godot process is running that is not yours and not in `tools/gpu_queue.ps1 -Status`, do not build and do not start Godot; work on code or text and tell the primary. Never kill a Godot process you did not start.
- **One GPU.** Every GPU test, report-tool run and capture goes through the queue (`tools/run_tests.ps1` takes it itself; `tools/gpu_queue.ps1 -Wrap` for tools). GPU tests of two orders are never started at the same time; the headless ones of one order can run while another order waits for the GPU.
- **Poses above the ground.** Since T2R-18 the fine levels are released by altitude (H35). A GPU test or capture that looks down from more than about 400 m either switches the rule off for the pose (the coordinator's `CurrentAltitudeErrorFactor` set to 0.01 and restored, as `inland_water_render_gpu_test` does), or waits 6 s at the pose and states which levels were drawn.

## Spec

### E1. Spans and records

- **Span**: a stretch of one reach. A reach of k channel cells has `ceil(k / 2)` spans of equal cell count (the last takes the remainder). Span boundaries are the arc lengths of those cells' nodes on the stem.
- **Stored** (`RiverSpan`, 24 B, sorted by reach then `SStart`): `Reach` (int32), `SStart`, `SEnd` (float), `Q` (float, discharge at the span's last cell), `Sh` (float), `Landing` (float: 1 = none, above 1 = the factor of E4).
- `Sh = max((W(s_m - x0) - W(s_m + x1)) / (x0 + x1), 1e-4)`, W = `RiverLevel.Stored`, `s_m` the span's middle, `x0 = min(2500, s_m)`, `x1 = min(2500, s_end - s_m)`. Computed after `RiverLevel.Build`.
- `Sr = max(0, (W(SStart) - W(SEnd)) / (SEnd - SStart))`, derived at load.
- **Derived at load** per span (floats): half width, bank width, depth, edge depth, floodplain width, valley slope, `Vp`, `eta`, `Vd`, whiteness, class. Per reach: `SpanStart`, `SpanCount`, and the **path section** (today's `RiverHydraulics.Section` of the reach's `Q` and `S`), which keeps feeding `MeanderAmplitude`, `MeanderWavelength`, `WanderAmplitude` and the bank-line station offset.
- **Blend**: channel and valley values blend from the upstream span over the first 200 m of a span (`smoothstep`), also across reach boundaries on a stem. The first span of a stem does not blend. Q and the class do not blend.
- Floodplain and valley slope use S6's formulas with the span's w and `Sh`. A landing, and the first calm or swift span after a dangerous one, limit the valley slope to 0.6.
- **Versions**: at T3R-3, `GraphFormat` 4 (payload gains spans after reaches), `DrainageAlgorithm` 4, `CarveOperator` 3. **On 2026-11-13: 4, 9 and 10** (the T2 rules batch and play-test 2; H23). No open T3 order changes them.
- **Bytes**: at T3R-3, 61,758 spans and 30.3 MiB resident. On 2026-11-13, 85,869 spans (2.06 MB stored, 6.2 MiB resident with the derived arrays) and a resident graph of about 36.2 MiB on seed 1337; the gate became a tripwire at 44 MiB in T2R-22.
- **In-lake spans** (since T2R-8b). A stem that starts inside a lake carries spans along its drowned channel. They are flat, so calm, and wide and slow by the law of E2. They are not rivers to classify: `RiverNetwork.InLakeEnd` marks them, and every share, band and window is counted without them (T3R-3c makes the last report line do so).
- **Heads** (since T2R-21). `RiverNetwork.ChannelAt` tapers width and depth over the first 1,500 m of a source stem (H25). The span records and the derived span arrays are not tapered; a reader that needs the local section goes through `ChannelAt`, and E5 applies the matching speed factor.
- **Checked by** `river_danger_test` and the graph report.

### E2. Hydraulics

Per span, in `RiverDanger.Channel`:
```
d   = channel_depth * Kd * Q^0.4                                 Kd = 0.34
n   = L * clamp(Np * (Sh / Sa)^Gn, Np, Nm) / fer                 Np = 0.05, Nm = 0.18, Sa = 0.0003, Gn = 0.4; L = the span's Landing
U0  = min(d^(2/3) * sqrt(Sh) / n, Vpool / 1.5 * fer)             Vpool = 9
w   = min(Q / (U0 * d), Wmax)                                    Wmax = 400; if Q / (U0 * d) > Wmax then d = Q / (U0 * Wmax)
U   = Q / (w * d)                                                Q <= 0 gives d = w = U = 0
```
**No floors since T2R-20** (user decision of 2026-11-13; T2-rules.md RS3). The block above is the law as built today. As written for T3R-3 it had `d = max(1.2, ...)` and `w = clamp(..., 8, Wmax)`. On a source stem the section tapers over the first 1,500 m (T2-rules.md RS4, H25).
**Fixed by T3R-2 (2026-11-12); Nm re-chosen in the T3R-3 escalation (2026-11-12).** Kd 0.34, Np 0.05, Nm 0.18 (was 0.20), Sa 0.0003, Gn 0.4, Vpool 9, Wmax 400. Nm and Kd are chosen from the grid (E3), now evaluated on the production spans. Np, Sa, Gn, Vpool and Wmax were not in the grid and are kept on purpose.
`river_ferocity`: recipe field, default 1, range 0.6 to 1.5, frozen with the world. Bank width, edge depth, freeboard and incision are S6's.
**Checked by** `river_danger_test`: `Q = U w d` within 1e-6; widths in (0, 400] and depths above 0; monotone in Sh and in Q.

### E3. Danger speed and classes

```
Vp  = 1.5 * U
c   = min(1, Lc * Sr)                                            Lc = 10 (RiverLevel.ChuteMeters)
h   = max(1, Ld * Sr)                                            Ld = 30
eta = (E0 / L) * clamp(d / Dref, 0.5, 1.6)                       E0 = 0.80, Dref = 2.2
Vc  = max(Vp, min(sqrt(Vp^2 + 2 g eta h), Vchute * fer))         Vchute = 12
Vd  = (1 - c) * Vp + c * Vc
```
Class: calm `Vd < 1.5`, swift `< 4`, fierce `< 7`, torrent otherwise. Whiteness of a span: `wbar = smoothstep(2.5, 7, Vd)`.
**Fixed by T3R-2 (2026-11-12); E0 re-chosen in the T3R-3 escalation (2026-11-12).** E0 0.80 (was 0.65; chosen from the grid), Dref 2.2, Ld 30, Lc 10, Vchute 12 (not in the grid; kept on purpose). The class thresholds are the plan's and were never tunable.

### E5. `RingWaterFlow`

```
public readonly record struct WaterFlowSample(InlandWaterKind Kind, double Surface, double Bed, double VelocityAround, double VelocityAcross,
    double Speed, int Span, RiverClass Class, double DangerSpeed, double Whitewater, double ChuteHead);
public interface IWaterFlowSource { WaterFlowSample Sample(double around, double across, double height); }
public sealed class RingWaterFlow : IWaterFlowSource   // RingWaterFlow(RiverGraph graph, RiverCarveOperator carve); double CurrentStrength { get; set; }
```
`height` NaN means the surface. Kind None gives NaN levels and zero velocity. Thread-safe, allocation-free. Cost: "Budgets"; the `Near` bit exits first away from water.

**Rewritten on 2026-11-13.** Against the first version: the surface is the located level; source stems use the head factor of T2-rules.md RS4 in place of a 200 m fade-in; water held in a lake is still apart from inflow jets and the draw at the outlet (the first version faded an outlet stem in over its whole held length, which is now up to 10 km of lake, and drew the jet in open lake water); a stem that ends on a lake cell fades out; the class in the sample follows the local speed at heads and in lakes. Overflow outlets, straits and lake-end joins (T2R-22) need no rule of their own.

**Location.** `RiverCarveOperator.Locate(around, across, 0, ...)` gives the owner core with its stem, reach, s, t, tangent, a, b, d, d_e and drawn level. Where no core owns the point, the lake query (T3R-4) gives the lake and its level where the membership P is above 0. The field has no ground: it does not know islands, the dry ring of a lake or the sea. A caller compares `Surface` with its own ground, and the controller samples only where the sampler reports inland water.

**In a core.**
- `Surface` = the located level (`RiverLocation.Level`: the displayed level with the draw-down at confluences), which is what the sampler shows. `Bed = CoreProfile(|t|, level, a, b, d, d_e)`. Direction = the displaced centreline's tangent, downstream.
- Chute head: `sigma(s) = clamp(L(s - 5) - L(s + 5), 0, 1)`, `B(s) = sigma(s) * max(1, L(s - Ld) - L(s))`, L = `RiverLevel.AtStem`, arguments clamped to the stem. Where the profile is steeper than 10 % the level is a ramp (T2R-7c) and B is the fall over the last 30 m. Just above a lake-end join (T2R-22 item 8f) B reaches tens of metres; the cap below holds the speed.
- Centre speed of a river: `vc(s) = H(s) * min(sqrt(Vp^2 + 2 g eta B(s)), Vchute * fer)`, with Vp and eta of the span, blended as E1.
- Head factor: `H(s) = (s / T)^(4/9)` on a source stem for `s < T = min(1,500, stem length)`, else 1. With the tapered width and depth of `ChannelAt` the discharge then grows as `(s / T)^(5/3)` and `Q = U w d` holds along the taper (RS4).
- Across: `vc * (1 - (t / a)^2)` for `|t| < a`, else 0; a is the located half width, so it is tapered at a head. With depth: times `lerp(0.6, 1, clamp((height - Bed) / (Surface - Bed), 0, 1))`.
- End fades: times `smoothstep(0, 500, s_end - s)` on a stem that ends in the sea or Dead; times `smoothstep(0, 3 w, s_end - s)` on a stem that ends on a lake cell with no drowned channel to carry it on (196 stems on seed 1337). No fade before a confluence.

**Water held in a lake.** A stem i that starts in a lake (`OutletLake[i] >= 0`) is that lake's water for `s <= HeldEnd[i]`: a drowned channel, an in-lake branch, a strait, and the outlet stem up to its outlet point (H29). A lake does not flow like a river. Its water is still, apart from where a river runs in and where the outlet draws. In held water the centre speed is, in place of `vc`:
```
v_held(s, t) = max( draw(s) * (1 - (t / a)^2),  max over inflows j of jet_j(s, t) )
draw(s)      = vc_i(HeldEnd) * exp(-(HeldEnd - s) / (3 w_o))        only where i is the lake's outlet stem; vc_i is the river formula above at HeldEnd, w_o the width there
jet_j(s, t)  = vc_j(end of j) * exp(-x / (3 w_j)) * exp(-(t / (a_j + 0.2 x))^2)        x = s - JunctionS_j >= 0
```
- An inflow j of i is a stem that ends as a Confluence on i inside i's held part and is not itself lake water at its end: a river that arrives from outside the lake. `vc_j`, `w_j` and `a_j` are taken at its end. The inflows of each stem are tabled once per graph. A sample uses at most the four nearest inflows upstream of s and ignores `x > 12 w_j`.
- Both terms are 0 outside the core (`|t| >= a`). There is no chute boost; the level is flat. A jet does not pass from one in-lake stem to the next.
- Continuity: at `s = HeldEnd` the draw equals the river's `vc`, and at a junction the jet starts at the inflow's end speed. Where the span at the end of a hold is the lake's wide, slow one (E1), the draw is gentle and the river picks up speed over the 200 m blend into its first span outside the lake.
- An outlet stem that is lake water to its end and joins a passing river there (T2R-22 (c')) draws toward that junction by the same formula; the step to the receiver's speed is a confluence shear line.
- A strait, an in-lake branch and the in-lake stem of a closed lake have no draw. Without an inflow they are still.
- The lengths `3 w` and the spread 0.2 **(tune at review)**. Still water over most of a lake is the realistic case and the default. A slow current along the drowned channel through the whole lake would show more flow than a lake has; it is question 2 of 2026-11-13 and is not built without the user's yes.

**Open lake water** (no core, P above 0): `Surface` = the lake level, `Bed = NaN`, velocity 0.

**Kinds.** River: a core owns the point and the point is not held. Lake: held water in a core, or open lake water. None otherwise. This is a rule on the arc length. The sampler's rule since PT2-2 is on the membership and the level, so the two can differ within about a cell of a lake's edge (the last metres of an inflow, the first of an outlet). The look follows the sampler and the force follows the field; the field is continuous across the difference.

**Derived values.**
- `Whitewater = max(smoothstep(2.5, 7, Speed / CurrentStrength), min(1, B))` in a river; `smoothstep(2.5, 7, Speed / CurrentStrength)` in held water; 0 in open lake water, and 0 when the strength is 0. `ChuteHead = B`, 0 in held water.
- `Span`, `Class` and `DangerSpeed` are the span's. Inside a head taper `DangerSpeed = Vd * H(s)` and `Class` is the class of that speed (the span arrays are not tapered, H25). In held water `DangerSpeed` is the local centre speed at strength 1 and `Class` its class.
- Every velocity is multiplied by `CurrentStrength`.
- **Checked by** `river_flow_field_test`.

### E6. Coupling

**Rewritten on 2026-11-13** for the swim depths of T2R-20 (entry 1.15 m, exit 0.9 m, float 1.15 m; H28). The swimming rule is unchanged. The wading rule is new. The first one added `6 * (depth / entry)^2 * (waterVelocity - planarVelocity)` per second to the walking step; against a walking acceleration of 45 m/s^2 it could not move a standing player in any river, and its depth scale was the old entry depth of 0.55 m.

- **Sample.** One per physics frame, only when the player's column is inland water and the player is at or under the water level plus 0.5 m. None in noclip, in flight, in the ocean or without `water.current`. Below, `depth` is `CurrentWaterDepthMeters` (the surface minus the feet) and the water velocity is the sample's at the player's height.
- **Swimming** (`ProcessSwimming`; depth above 1.15 m, or above 0.9 m once swimming): target = input velocity + (VelocityAround, VelocityAcross) on canonical X and Z, approached at `SwimAcceleration`. Float target = `Surface - SwimSurfaceFloatDepthMeters - min(0.6, 0.3 * ChuteHead)` **(tune at G2)**. Where a river is shallower than the float depth the swimmer's feet are on the bed, as today.
- **Wading** (the walking block; depth above 0, not swimming): footing `f = smoothstep(0.6, 1.2, depth * |waterVelocity|)`, depth in m and speed in m/s. The walking target becomes `input velocity + f * waterVelocity`, approached with the existing ground or air acceleration.
  - Under a depth times speed of 0.6 m^2/s the player stands and walks as on land. Above 1.2 m^2/s the player is carried at the water's speed and still walks relative to it.
  - The two numbers are where flood-safety studies put the start and the end of an adult's footing in moving water (about 0.6 to 1.3 m^2/s). They are not chosen for effect **(tune at G2, inside that range only)**.
  - Examples: 0.3 m at 2 m/s holds; 0.3 m at 4 m/s sweeps; 1.0 m at 0.5 m/s holds; 1.0 m at 1.2 m/s sweeps; ankle-deep water never does.
- **Between the two.** At the swim entry a wader in water faster than about 1 m/s is already carried at the water's speed, so entering the swim state changes nothing there. In slower water the swimmer picks up the drift on entering, at most about 0.6 m/s.
- **Never pinned.** The flow has no component across the channel, the input always adds, and the speed is 0 at the channel edge.
- **Wading speed** (question 3 of 2026-11-13; not built without the user's yes). Today the player walks at the full 8 m/s in water up to 1.15 m deep. That is faster than swimming (5.5 m/s), so a wader can outwalk a current that a swimmer cannot hold. If wanted: the input velocity of a wader is multiplied by `lerp(1, 0.5, smoothstep(0.3, 1.15, depth))`, in inland water and at the sea shore alike.
- **Checked by** `river_current_swim_test`.

### E7. Vertex flow and shader

**Rewritten on 2026-11-13**: the carrier (H33), Lake-kind water (H29), the drift that the flow replaces (H32), and nothing in the cache record.

- **Carrier.** `CUSTOM1` as four half floats (8 bytes per vertex). x and y: the velocity along canonical around and across in m/s at strength 1, which are the axes of the water pattern's coordinates. z: whiteness. w: 0, free. Written for River and Lake vertices from `RingWaterFlow.Sample` at the vertex, at the surface, in the mesh path and in the compact path. Where terrain and water share one vertex array, terrain vertices hold 0 (T3R-9 uses z there). The ocean's `WaterGrid` path, sectors without inland water and worlds without `water.current` carry nothing. `CUSTOM0` stays the parent chord.
- **Not cached.** The values are a pure function of position and the graph and are computed when the mesh is built, as the tint coverage is. The sector cache record and `LodSectorCacheRiverVersion` (13) do not change.
- **Shader**, for inland water (kind above 1.5) when the material's flow switch is on; `RingWaterModel` sets it for worlds with `water.current`. Two phases `p_i = fract(TIME / T + i / 2)`, `T = 3 s` **(tune at review)**. The pattern coordinates of the height, gradient and foam lookups are shifted by `-velocity * ringworld_current_strength * p_i * T` metres, and the two results are blended by `1 - abs(2 p_i - 1)`.
  - River kind: the shift replaces the drift along +x. The pattern's slow change in place stays, so a river at current strength 0 shimmers and does not drift. **This is the ripple drift direction the user left to T3**: the ripples go where the water goes, at its speed.
  - Lake kind: the shift is added to the Lake pattern. Where the lake is still it is 0 and both phases are the same picture.
  - Without the switch the existing path runs unchanged, the +x drift included (legacy rivers, and worlds with `water.rivers` alone).
- **Foam.** Coverage = the vertex whiteness times `min(1, ringworld_current_strength)`, through the existing foam patch pattern, added to the existing foam term ahead of the inland composition (H32), so foam hides the bed as it does at sea. Inland water still has no shore foam.
- **Footprint.** The animation fades between pixel footprints of 1 m and 4 m; foam coverage stays. Proxy levels of 16 m and coarser write `smoothstep(2.5, 7, DangerSpeed)` of the sample in place of the local whiteness, so chutes do not alias.
- **What a level can show.** A level holds a river as water only where `w >= 2 * spacing` (T2.md S13.2), and the vertex flow exists only there. A narrower river is a tint on that level (E8).
- **Checked by** `river_flow_visual_parity_test`.

### E8. Roar and whitewater on the tint

**Rewritten on 2026-11-13**: the roar has no floors, the river layers read the index (H37), and the whiteness also rides on the proxy tint (H34).

- **Roar of a span**: `roar = wbar * min(1.5, sqrt(w / 40))`, with w the local width (tapered at a head) and wbar the whiteness of the local danger speed. Audible radius `R = 500 * min(1, roar)` m. Level at distance x from the nearest point of the span's channel: `min(1, roar) * (1 - smoothstep(0.3 R, R, x))`, and 0 where R is 0. The controller takes the maximum over spans within 640 m and publishes `river_roar` and `river_roar_distance`.
  - The first version had `clamp(sqrt(w / 40), 0.5, 1.5)` and `R = 100 + 400 * min(1, roar)`: a floor of a half and a base of 100 m. With no minimum river they would make a trickle roar like a 10 m river, so they are gone (the realism rule). A 40 m torrent is heard at 500 m as before, a 4 m cascade at about 160 m, the start of a head not at all.
- **Calm layer**, with `x` the distance to the nearest river channel edge from the index: on land, proximity `1 - clamp(x / 640, 0, 1)` and shore proximity `1 - clamp(x / 256, 0, 1)`; in the water, proximity 1 and shore proximity `1 - clamp(x / 320, 0, 1)`. These are today's formulas with the true distance in place of the probe radius. The layer fades out as the roar rises above 0.5. Water held in a lake is the lake's, for both layers.
- **White share of the tint.** Where a level shows a river as a tint, it also carries the white part of that area: `c_w(v) = sum over samples of area * bilinear weight * wbar / spacing^2`, from the same splat as the coverage `c(v)` of T2.md S11b. The shader mixes the tint from the river colour toward foam white (0.92) by `c_w / c`. It is the true white area; there is no minimum.
  - Slot levels (8, 16, 32 m): `attr.w` of terrain lattice texels holds c and c_w as two 16-bit fractions, in place of the float bits of c.
  - Mesh levels (64 m and coarser): c stays in `UV2.y`; `c_w / c` goes into `CUSTOM1.z` of terrain lattice vertices (E7's array, added on sectors with river coverage).
  - Far tier (global LOD, far viewport): per texel in the alpha of the surface tile normal (reserved at 255 by T2R-12), per vertex in the channel V9 finds. The single `river_whitewater` uniform goes.
  - Only with `water.current`. Without it every carrier holds what it holds today.
- **Checked by** `river_ambience_test` and `river_far_water_fraction_test`.

### F2. `RingBuoyancy`

State: canonical position (around, across, height), velocity, yaw. One step of `dt` with a flow sample and the ground height:
- Vertical: a critically damped spring toward `Surface - draft`, natural period 1.2 s **(tune at review)**. Never below `ground + 0.05`.
- Horizontal: `v += (waterVelocity - v) * (1 - exp(-dt * drag))`, `drag` default `1 / 1.5` per second.
- Stranding: with `depth = Surface - ground` under `draft`, a deceleration of `3 * (1 - depth / draft)` m/s^2 against the horizontal velocity. With `may_strand` off, a push of 0.5 m/s toward deeper water instead (as built in T3R-6: the downhill direction of the ground callback, because the flow sample carries no channel offset).
- Out of water: velocity to 0 over 0.5 s, resting on the ground.
- Yaw turns toward the flow direction at up to 0.5 rad/s when the water speed is above 0.3 m/s. Pitch follows the surface slope along the flow.
- Steps longer than 1/30 s are split.

### Questions for the user (2026-11-13)

Each has a default, and work proceeds on the defaults. None of the defaults adds a floor, a minimum or a boost.

1. **The danger mix after today's rule changes.** At the danger map you aimed at about calm 39 %, swift 51 %, fierce 9 %, torrent 1.3 %. The rule changes since then (no minimum river, overflow rivers, rivers removed at sites) moved the rivers. Counted on rivers alone they now seem to be at about 40 / 51 / 8 / 1.0 % on the first seed and 42 / 50 / 7 / 1.0 % on the second: inside your bands, with a little less fierce and torrent water than you saw. T3R-3c measures it exactly. Keep the constants, or have them chosen again to bring fierce and torrent back up? Choosing again changes river widths a little and means your world is built once more. *Default: keep, unless the measurement shows a seed under 6 % fierce.*
2. **Current in lakes.** The realistic picture: a lake is still. Where a river runs in there is a jet that dies away within a few hundred metres, and the outlet draws water in over a similar distance. A swimmer or a floating thing that drifts into a lake stops there. The alternative is a slow current, about 0.3 to 0.7 m/s, along the drowned channel through the whole lake, so that floating things travel on to the outlet. That is more flow than a lake has, and since play-test 2 the drowned channel looks like the lake, so the current would have no visible sign. *Default: the still lake.*
3. **Walking speed in water.** Since the swim entry went up to 1.15 m, the player walks at the full 8 m/s through water up to chest depth. That is faster than swimming (5.5 m/s), so a wader can outwalk a current that a swimmer cannot hold. Slowing the walk with depth would be the realistic rule (proposed: full speed up to 0.3 m of water, falling to half at 1.15 m), and it would apply at every shore, the sea included. The lead recommends it. *Default if unanswered: not built; the walking speed stays as it is and is judged at D2.*
4. **Your play world.** For the swim milestone (D2) the world must be created again with the current switched on (review feature `water.current`). Which slot? And may it be done together with the recreation at C2c, so that it happens once? *Default: together with C2c, in the slot you use now, after your yes.*

Reported, not asked:
- **Wading.** A wader keeps their footing until the water's depth times its speed passes about 0.6 m^2/s and is carried from about 1.2 m^2/s: knee-deep water has to be fast to take you, waist-deep water needs little. The numbers come from flood-safety studies, not from tuning. Dangerous water still displaces and never harms.
- **Ripple drift** (your play-test 2 note). With the current on, the ripples of a river go where its water goes, at its speed. With current strength 0 they shimmer in place.
