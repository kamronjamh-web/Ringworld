## Phase rules

The "Phase rules" of T2.md apply to every order here, in addition to TEMPLATE.md: Claude Code executors (`implementer`, `claude-lead`; `claude-deep` only with the primary's approval), production first, sampling with tolerances (99.5 % and a hard limit), a STOP RULE on every order, no new requirements from inside, the reduced testing cadence, at most five evidence links per report. Added for T3:

- **T3 does not move rivers.** No order changes a centreline, a level profile, a lake level or a lake cell. T3R-3 re-measured the straight-run criterion to show it.
- **A class is a measurement.** The class of a span comes from its danger speed. The test that compares it with the flow field belongs to T9i.
- **Commit points** (the primary runs `-Suite gate` and commits): **C4** after T3R-3 (done, `ad9530d`). CW1 and CW2 moved to the sound project with T3R-10e to T3R-10g. C5 (the current and the floating system) went to T9i with its orders. **C6** at T3R-12, if the freeze steps are kept.

Added on 2026-11-13:

- **No version moves.** No open order changes `HydrologyVersions` (`DrainageAlgorithm` 9, `CarveOperator` 10, `GraphFormat` 4) or `LodSectorCacheRiverVersion` (13). T3 from here on reads the graph and adds runtime data only. An order that finds it must change one of them stops and reports. A retune of the danger constants is T9i's (T3R-3d there).
- **The realism rule** (user, 2026-11-13) applies to every formula here: no floor, minimum, widening or boost. For sound: no water is louder, or heard from farther or from higher, than its size and its distance give. Where a rule would let the player hear more than the water would give, it is a question for the user first.
- **Sound is not world data.** T3R-10a to T3R-10g change no pixel, no physics and no fingerprint in any world, and sit behind no feature. Rivers, chutes and falls sound where a world has a graph (`water.rivers`); the sea, lakes and the water under the surface sound in every world. This is an exception to TEMPLATE.md's "new worlds only" and needs the user's yes before T3R-10d is accepted (question 1 of "Questions for the user (2026-11-13, water sound and the freeze)"). T3R-10a to T3R-10c do not depend on the answer.
- **Sound is checked by numbers and judged by ear.** An agent cannot hear. A sound order proves levels with numbers (the field test, the capture test, the loop checks) and sends the user something to listen to. No report says that something "sounds right".
- **One working tree, one Debug assembly.** At most two code-writing executors at a time, on the disjoint files the table below names. An executor builds only when its own edits compile, and never while another executor has said it is mid-edit in a file of the same assembly that does not compile. Reviews, the loops (T3R-10c: Python and ffmpeg, no build), captures and report runs can go alongside.

Before any build or test: if `.godot\mono\temp\bin\DebugPlaySaved\RingworldTechDemo.dll` exists, or a Godot process is running that is not yours and not in `tools/gpu_queue.ps1 -Status`, do not build and do not start Godot; work on code or text and tell the primary. Never kill a Godot process you did not start.
- **One GPU.** Every GPU test, report-tool run and capture goes through the queue (`tools/run_tests.ps1` takes it itself; `tools/gpu_queue.ps1 -Wrap` for tools). GPU tests of two orders are never started at the same time; the headless ones of one order can run while another order waits for the GPU.

- H39. **The mix.** scripts/water_ambience.gd (104 lines) owns four plain `AudioStreamPlayer` nodes, none positional: Shore, OpenWater, River and Underwater (scenes/VoxelRingIntegrationTest.tscn:234-255), on the buses `WaterAbove` and `WaterUnderwater` (default_bus_layout.tres; the second has a 1,450 Hz low-pass). It reads three metas of the player, kind, proximity and shore proximity, and sets the gains from them alone (:58-74): shore = shore x proximity; sea: open = proximity x (1 - 0.55 shore); river: river = proximity; lake: shore x 0.45 and open = 0.22 x proximity. A 2 s fade (:11, :84-91). Trims -6, -10, -7 and -12 dB (:12-15).
- H40. **Fault 1, heard at any height.** The metas come from `UpdateWaterAmbienceMetadata(sample, around, across)` (scripts/Voxel/Integration/RingVoxelExplorerController.cs:1266-1341), called from `UpdateWaterState` (:1163) every physics frame, in noclip and flight as well, with the probes of H8 every 0.4 s (:1275). **It is given the column and never the height.** Where the column under the player holds water the proximity is 1 (:1322-1324), at any altitude above that water and at any depth under its bed. Elsewhere the proximity is `1 - d / 640` with d one of the four probe radii (:1291, :1332): a straight-line gain, 6 dB down at 320 m and audible to 640 m, the same for a lake and for surf. The players are not positional, so the engine attenuates nothing either.
- H41. **Fault 2, a splash on loop.** One recording plays on all four layers: `shore_wave_cc0.ogg` (scene :66, :239, :243, :248, :253) at pitch 1 (Shore), 0.82 (OpenWater), 1.22 (River) and 0.62 (Underwater). It was made from one beach wave "repeated to 16 seconds" (resources/audio/water/LICENSE.md). Measured on 2026-11-13 with ffmpeg `astats` over half-second windows: the 16 s are one 4.0 s wave four times. In each, the level rises from -38.6 to -23.7 dBFS at 1.5 to 2.0 s, falls to about -34 dBFS, and the last half second is silence (-74 dBFS). So there is one splash every 4.0 s on the shore, every 4.9 s on open water, every 3.3 s on a river and every 6.5 s under water, and nothing else.

### T3R-10. Water sound (T3R-10a to T3R-10g)

Written on 2026-11-13. It replaces the single order "T3R-10. River ambience". The user asked for a better sound for water as a whole and named two faults: water is heard at any height, and the water sound is a splash on loop. Both are confirmed in the code (H40, H41). The design is Spec W1 to W9.

### T3R-10a. The water sound field: one loudness law, distance in three dimensions

ROLE: implementer
SIZE: M
DEPENDS ON: none. Can start at once. Serialise builds with T3R-10b.

OBJECTIVE
`WaterSoundField` is the arithmetic of water sound and nothing else: from a source (its kind, power, half size, distance, wave height, discharge and drop) it gives the level at the listener, the gain, the low-pass cutoff and the loop weights, and from the weather it gives the masker. No node, no asset, no world. A headless test proves Spec W1 to W3, W5 and W6, and that height counts as distance.

CONTEXT / ROOT CAUSE
- H40: today's rule is given the column and never the height, and its gain is a straight line over 640 m for every kind of water. H39: the layer rules are four hand-set factors.
- The pattern is `RainSoundField` (H44): arithmetic that a test can run without a scene.

### E1. Spans and records

- **Span**: a stretch of one reach. A reach of k channel cells has `ceil(k / 2)` spans of equal cell count (the last takes the remainder). Span boundaries are the arc lengths of those cells' nodes on the stem.
- **Stored** (`RiverSpan`, 24 B, sorted by reach then `SStart`): `Reach` (int32), `SStart`, `SEnd` (float), `Q` (float, discharge at the span's last cell), `Sh` (float), `Landing` (float: 1 = none, above 1 = the factor of E4).
- `Sh = max((W(s_m - x0) - W(s_m + x1)) / (x0 + x1), 1e-4)`, W = `RiverLevel.Stored`, `s_m` the span's middle, `x0 = min(2500, s_m)`, `x1 = min(2500, s_end - s_m)`. Computed after `RiverLevel.Build`.
- `Sr = max(0, (W(SStart) - W(SEnd)) / (SEnd - SStart))`, derived at load.
- **Derived at load** per span (floats): half width, bank width, depth, edge depth, floodplain width, valley slope, `Vp`, `eta`, `Vd`, whiteness, class. Per reach: `SpanStart`, `SpanCount`, and the **path section** (today's `RiverHydraulics.Section` of the reach's `Q` and `S`), which keeps feeding `MeanderAmplitude`, `MeanderWavelength`, `WanderAmplitude` and the bank-line station offset.
- **Blend**: channel and valley values blend from the upstream span over the first 200 m of a span (`smoothstep`), also across reach boundaries on a stem. The first span of a stem does not blend. Q and the class do not blend.
- Floodplain and valley slope use S6's formulas with the span's w and `Sh`. A landing, and the first calm or swift span after a dangerous one, limit the valley slope to 0.6.
- **Versions**: at T3R-3, `GraphFormat` 4 (payload gains spans after reaches), `DrainageAlgorithm` 4, `CarveOperator` 3. **On 2026-11-13: 4, 9 and 10** (the T2 rules batch and play-test 2; H23). No open T3 order changes them.
- **Bytes**: at T3R-3, 61,758 spans and 30.3 MiB resident. On 2026-11-13, 85,869 spans (2.06 MB stored, 6.2 MiB resident with the derived arrays) and a resident graph of about 36.2 MiB on seed 1337; the gate became a tripwire at 44 MiB in T2R-22.
- **In-lake spans** (since T2R-8b). A stem that starts inside a lake carries spans along its drowned channel. They are flat, so calm, and wide and slow by the law of E2. They are not rivers to classify: `RiverNetwork.InLakeEnd` marks them, and every share, band and window is counted without them (T3R-3c makes the last report line do so).
- **Heads** (since T2R-21). `RiverNetwork.ChannelAt` tapers width and depth over the first 1,500 m of a source stem (H25). The span records and the derived span arrays are not tapered; a reader that needs the local section goes through `ChannelAt`, and E5 applies the matching speed factor.
- **Checked by** `river_danger_test` and the graph report.

### E2. Hydraulics

Per span, in `RiverDanger.Channel`:
```
d   = channel_depth * Kd * Q^0.4                                 Kd = 0.34
n   = L * clamp(Np * (Sh / Sa)^Gn, Np, Nm) / fer                 Np = 0.05, Nm = 0.18, Sa = 0.0003, Gn = 0.4; L = the span's Landing
U0  = min(d^(2/3) * sqrt(Sh) / n, Vpool / 1.5 * fer)             Vpool = 9
w   = min(Q / (U0 * d), Wmax)                                    Wmax = 400; if Q / (U0 * d) > Wmax then d = Q / (U0 * Wmax)
U   = Q / (w * d)                                                Q <= 0 gives d = w = U = 0
```
**No floors since T2R-20** (user decision of 2026-11-13; T2-rules.md RS3). The block above is the law as built today. As written for T3R-3 it had `d = max(1.2, ...)` and `w = clamp(..., 8, Wmax)`. On a source stem the section tapers over the first 1,500 m (T2-rules.md RS4, H25).
**Fixed by T3R-2 (2026-11-12); Nm re-chosen in the T3R-3 escalation (2026-11-12).** Kd 0.34, Np 0.05, Nm 0.18 (was 0.20), Sa 0.0003, Gn 0.4, Vpool 9, Wmax 400. Nm and Kd are chosen from the grid (E3), now evaluated on the production spans. Np, Sa, Gn, Vpool and Wmax were not in the grid and are kept on purpose.
`river_ferocity`: recipe field, default 1, range 0.6 to 1.5, frozen with the world. Bank width, edge depth, freeboard and incision are S6's.
**Checked by** `river_danger_test`: `Q = U w d` within 1e-6; widths in (0, 400] and depths above 0; monotone in Sh and in Q.

### E3. Danger speed and classes

```
Vp  = 1.5 * U
c   = min(1, Lc * Sr)                                            Lc = 10 (RiverLevel.ChuteMeters)
h   = max(1, Ld * Sr)                                            Ld = 30
eta = (E0 / L) * clamp(d / Dref, 0.5, 1.6)                       E0 = 0.80, Dref = 2.2
Vc  = max(Vp, min(sqrt(Vp^2 + 2 g eta h), Vchute * fer))         Vchute = 12
Vd  = (1 - c) * Vp + c * Vc
```
Class: calm `Vd < 1.5`, swift `< 4`, fierce `< 7`, torrent otherwise. Whiteness of a span: `wbar = smoothstep(2.5, 7, Vd)`.
**Fixed by T3R-2 (2026-11-12); E0 re-chosen in the T3R-3 escalation (2026-11-12).** E0 0.80 (was 0.65; chosen from the grid), Dref 2.2, Ld 30, Lc 10, Vchute 12 (not in the grid; kept on purpose). The class thresholds are the plan's and were never tunable.

### W1. Listener, distance and cadence

Written on 2026-11-13 for T3R-10a to T3R-10g. W1 to W9 are the design of the water sound.

- **The listener is the camera**, as a canonical position (around, vertical, across), found as `RingWeatherAmbience.ResolveCameraListener` finds it (H44).
- **Distance.** For every source, `x` is the straight distance in metres from the listener to the nearest point of that source's water surface: `x = sqrt(h^2 + v^2)`, with h the horizontal distance to that point (0 in or over the water) and v the listener's height above the water level there, or depth under it. **Height is distance.** There is no separate altitude rule, and no layer leaves the height out.
- **Cadence.** The sources are found in slices of a bounded cost (W4). Levels are computed every frame from the kept source points for the listener's present position, so a moving listener hears no steps.

### W2. One law for loudness

Water makes sound where it breaks and where it falls. Every source has a power, a half size `a` in metres, and a level at the listener in dB above the background of a quiet day, so that 0 dB is "just heard":

```
a line of water (a river, a shore, open water):   L = 10 log10(P / P0) - 15 log10(a + x) - A x      P in watts per metre
a point (a chute, a fall):                        L = 10 log10(W / P1) - 20 log10(a + x) - A x      W in watts
```

`P0 = 0.011 W/m`, `P1 = 0.03 W`, `A = 0.02 dB/m` **(all three: tune at review, by ear and against the table of W3)**. `a + x` in metres.

- The 15 and the 20 are the spreading: sound from a line of sources thins more slowly than sound from a point. `A` is what air and ground take.
- **Heard** while `L > M`, the masker of W6 (0 on a quiet day): `u = smoothstep(M, M + 6, L)`.
- **Gain** of the source's players: `min(0, L - 45)` dB plus the volume rows, times u. 45 dB is a wide torrent at its bank; anything louder plays at full scale. One dB of level is one dB of gain.
- **Low-pass** with distance: `fc = clamp(18000 * sqrt(10 / x), 800, 20000)` Hz for x above 10 m, open below. Air takes the high frequencies first: about 5.7 kHz at 100 m and 1.8 kHz at 1 km.
- **No floor.** A power of 0 is silence. No source has a minimum level, radius or proximity, and nothing is raised to be heard.

### W3. Sources and their power

`rho g = 9,810`. Every input is data the world already has.

- **River**, at a point of a channel: `P = rho g Q Sr * min(1, Fr^2) * (0.15 + 0.85 wbar)`, `Fr = U / sqrt(g d)`.
  - Q, U, d and the half width are the channel's there, tapered on a source stem as T2-rules.md RS4 gives them (Q by `x^(5/3)`, U by `x^(4/9)`, d by `x^(2/3)`, the width by `ChannelAt`); Sr and wbar are the span's own gradient and whiteness (H46). `a` = the half width.
  - `rho g Q Sr` is the power the river loses per metre. `Fr^2` is the share that goes into a broken surface: a deep slow river spends its fall on its bed, in silence. The last factor says unbroken riffles sound about a seventh as strong as white water **(the 0.15: tune at review)**. It multiplies a power; it is not a minimum.
  - The in-lake part of a stem (`InLakeEnd`) is the lake's water and has no river source.
- **Chute or fall**, where the displayed level drops (W4): `W = rho g Q h`, h the drop in metres. `a` = the half width there.
- **Shore** of the sea or of a lake: `P = (1/8) rho g Hs^2 * sqrt(g Hs / 0.8)`, the power that waves of height Hs bring to a metre of shore. `a = 2 + 20 Hs`.
- **Open water**, when the listener's own column is sea or lake: `P = 0.001 rho g Hs^2` **(the 0.001: tune at review)**, `a = 3`, x = the height above the water.
- **Hs** is the significant height of the waves the surface there shows (V10): the sea's; on a lake the sea's times 0.24, the Lake pattern's height scale (H49). The sound follows the picture. There is no wave height setting for sound.
- **Under the surface** (the camera under water): the underwater loop at `lerp(0.36, 1, clamp(depth / 4, 0, 1))`, as today; every other layer is silent.
- **River-kind water without a graph** (legacy worlds): a shore with the sea's Hs times 0.11, the River pattern's scale. Nothing is known of its flow, so none is invented.

### W4. Finding the sources

```
public enum WaterSoundKind { River, Drop, SeaShore, LakeShore, OpenSea, OpenLake }
public readonly record struct WaterSoundSource(WaterSoundKind Kind, double Power, double HalfSize,
    double Around, double Vertical, double Across,   // the nearest point of the source's water surface, canonical
    double Distance,                                 // W1, for the listener's present position
    double WaveHeight, double Discharge, double Drop, double Shield, int Stem);
```

Nothing reads a mesh. The finder runs on the main thread in slices and allocates nothing in steady state.

- **Shores** (sea and lakes; in every world), from the sampler. 16 directions from the listener's column. Along each: radii 4, 8, 16 and so on, doubling up to 2,048 m, or less when no shore that far could be heard at the present wave height; at the first change between wet and dry, five bisection steps. The found point is kept as a canonical position with its kind and water level. A budget of sampler calls per frame, set from V11, spreads a whole sweep over about a second. Between sweeps the distances follow the listener from the kept points. The nearest sea shore point and the nearest lake shore point are the two shore sources.
- **Open water.** The listener's own column is sea or lake: one source, x = the height above its water level, 0 when in it.
- **Rivers** (worlds with a graph), from the graph. One new public query on the operator, built on `LocatePiece`:
  `int ChannelsNear(double around, double across, double radiusMeters, Span<RiverChannelNear> output)` with `RiverChannelNear(int Stem, double S, double T, double HalfWidth, double Level, double TangentAround, double TangentAcross)`: for each piece, the closest point of its displaced centreline within the radius.
  - It must be complete to the radius. One bucket of the index is complete to 300 m (H47), so the query merges the pieces of the buckets at the corners of the square of half side `radius - 300` m around the listener (four at most).
  - Radius 2,000 m, the reach of the loudest fall of W3's table, or less when the masker hides everything that far.
  - Per hit: the span through `ChannelAt(stem, s)`, the power by W3, `x` from `|t|` less the half width (0 inside) and the level. Per stem the loudest hit is kept; the two loudest stems are the river sources.
- **Chutes and falls**, from the level profile. One new public method:
  `int RiverLevel.DropsNear(RiverNetwork net, int stem, double s0, double s1, Span<RiverDrop> output)` with `RiverDrop(double S, double Height)`: walking `RiverLevel.AtStem` in 5 m steps, a drop is a run of at most 60 m of channel along which the level falls by at least 0.5 m in every 10 m; it is one drop, at its downstream end, with the whole fall of the run as its height. So a 1 m chute is a drop of 1 m and a short steep fall of 12 m is one drop of 12 m. A longer steep run is a steep river, not a fall, and stays with the river law, which already has its gradient **(the 60 m: tune at review)**.
  - The river law's gradient includes the chutes of its span, so a chute's fall is counted in both. The share is small (the river law takes `Fr^2` of it and the chute all of it) and is left in.
  - For each stem among the river hits, over the part of it within reach. The list is kept until the listener has moved 50 m or the stems change. The three loudest drops are the drop sources.
  - A drop's sound follows the level the water falls through, whatever the picture shows there: no waterfall mesh exists (T9c). The lake-end falls above 25 m are on T2R-25's list.
- **No work when nothing can be heard**: when the masker, or the listener's height above the ground, puts every possible source out of reach.

### W6. Masking and ducking

- **The masker** `M`, in dB on the scale of W2, is what else the listener hears: `M = max(Mrain, Mwind, Mspeed)`.
  - `Mrain = 35 * sqrt(rain)`, rain the precipitation intensity from 0 to 1 at the listener.
  - `Mwind = 40 log10(max(1, wind / 4))`, wind in m/s at the listener.
  - `Mspeed = 40 log10(max(1, v / 20))`, v the listener's own speed in m/s: the rush of air in flight. It is 0 on foot (sprint is 14 m/s) and about 43 dB at 240 m/s, where no water but a great fall close by is heard.
  - All three **(tune at review)**. Masking only removes; it never raises a level.
- **Rain on water** is the rain system's and stays as it is (H43). Water does not duck rain.
- **The wind bed** of scripts/outside_ambience.gd is ducked by water as today, through the same meta, which now carries the loudest water gain as a linear value from 0 to 1. Over water from the air it is 0, so the wind bed is no longer ducked there (H42).

### W7. Placement and voices

- Shore, river and drop voices are positional players with the engine's attenuation off (H44); the gain is W2's. Each is placed every frame in the direction of its source's nearest point, at `min(x, 40)` m from the listener, from canonical positions, so an origin shift needs no handler. Its panning strength is `smoothstep(0, 2 a, h)`: in or over the water the sound is all around, from the bank it has a direction.
- Open water and under water are plain stereo players.
- Voices: shore 2 (the sea's and a lake's), river 2 (the two loudest stems), drop 3, open water 1, under water 1. A shore, river or drop voice holds two players and plays the two loops of largest weight, renormalised; a player changes its loop only while it is silent. At most 16 players exist and at most 8 sound at once.
- A player silent for 5 s stops; it starts again at a random place in its loop. Gains slew over 0.3 s. A source that takes over a voice fades in over 1 s.

### F2. `RingBuoyancy`

State: canonical position (around, across, height), velocity, yaw. One step of `dt` with a flow sample and the ground height:
- Vertical: a critically damped spring toward `Surface - draft`, natural period 1.2 s **(tune at review)**. Never below `ground + 0.05`.
- Horizontal: `v += (waterVelocity - v) * (1 - exp(-dt * drag))`, `drag` default `1 / 1.5` per second.
- Stranding: with `depth = Surface - ground` under `draft`, a deceleration of `3 * (1 - depth / draft)` m/s^2 against the horizontal velocity. With `may_strand` off, a push of 0.5 m/s toward deeper water instead (as built in T3R-6: the downhill direction of the ground callback, because the flow sample carries no channel offset).
- Out of water: velocity to 0 over 0.5 s, resting on the ground.
- Yaw turns toward the flow direction at up to 0.5 rad/s when the water speed is above 0.3 m/s. Pitch follows the surface slope along the flow.
- Steps longer than 1/30 s are split.
**Checked by** `floating_body_test`.

### F3. `RingFloatingBody`
