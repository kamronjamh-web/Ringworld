## What T7 delivers

Today's lowlands have almost no relief between 2 m and 300 m (plains: 0.04 m RMS at 2-30 m, 0.74 m at 30-300 m). Standing on a plain, nothing between the grass at your feet and the hills on the horizon gives depth.

T7 adds walk-scale shape in the three characters the user chose (plan 13.1): **rolling ground with hummocks and ponds, buttes near plateau edges, and coastal cliffs, coves and strand dunes.** Up close it looks natural. At map scale the builders' hand shows, because butte tops sit on the ring-wide bench levels.

What the player should get from it:

- **Near (10-300 m):** the ground hides and reveals. A swell every couple of hundred metres puts a skyline between the player and the horizon, so trees, rocks and water appear over rises instead of all at once.
- **Middle (0.3-3 km):** hollows hold wet meadow or a pond, knolls carry dry ground and pines. The vegetation rules already do this from wetness and convexity (T6 A3, A5); T7 only supplies the shape.
- **Far (1-30 km):** buttes and sea cliffs are landmarks on the scale ladder of the vastness plan, readable through haze.
- **At the coast:** the shoreline stops being one smooth line. Headlands stand out, and coves cut in between them, with cliff walls where the coast is cliffed and a beach where it is low.

Out of scope: tors, dry pits, desert-climate dunes (assumed; the user did not answer on dunes separately), bays at river mouths and any sea arm larger than a cove (question 7), badlands (T9d), the GLSL river carve (T9b), small streams (T9a).

## Design decisions made by this plan

T7-1 turns these into formulas and constants. A later WO that finds one unworkable stops and reports.

- **P1. Meso relief is a stage of its own, never part of the natural height.** Order: natural height, river carve, **meso stage**, floor rules, sites. The natural model does not contain it, so `CpuDrainageHeightSource` and `Gen17NaturalHeightSource` cannot see it. On the GPU it is a separate function, `hm_meso`, in its own include, called by the proxy kernels after `hm_sample` and never by the drainage shader (F7). The drainage rule then holds by construction on both paths.
- **P2. River fade.** Every term is multiplied by `1 - CorridorWeight` (F6) and is zero inside site discs with a margin. Near inland water no term may lower ground below the water level beside it (target S8). T7-1 decides whether that needs a read-only accessor for the lake rim weight; adding one must leave `river_carve_invariants_test` and the graph hash unchanged.
- **P3. GPU sectors need no river lookup.** A GPU-routable sector does not touch river influence (F15). T7-10 proves `CorridorWeight` is 0 everywhere in such sectors, so `hm_meso` runs with fade 1.
- **P4. Recipe fields** are `RecipeFieldSpec` rows (F9), all Float, appended after the existing seven. No GDScript exports (F10).
- **P5. Buffer.** T7's words are a new block starting at **320**, named `HM_P_MESO_*` so the host parser accepts them (F13), with their own noise table (the base table is full, F12). No existing index moves. The feature bits come from the word at 317.
- **P6. Band-limiting in two tiers.**
  - **Tier 1, in T7:** every T7 term takes the footprint and fades to its mean as the footprint approaches a quarter to a half of its wavelength. New terms are written this way from the start, which is cheap.
  - **Tier 2, contingent (group T7-B):** per-octave band-limiting of the existing v7 terms (`landform.band_limited_sampling`, bit 12). It was planned to close edge gaps above the 8 m skirt in hill country. Resident coverage (F16) has since removed most of what the player could see of them. T7-2 measures what is left; T7-B runs only if that baseline fails its trigger.
- **P7. Ponds are the last group before the review and can be left out of the freeze.** Hollows are built pond-ready in T7-5 (rim, level, containment by construction) with no water. T7-11 adds the water as Lake kind through the existing classification (F19), routes pond sectors to the CPU builder as lake sectors are (F15), and reports the GPU share that costs. If a gate fails, landforms freeze with dry hollows, which the vegetation already dresses as wet meadow, and pond water follows as its own feature on the next free bit. The user is asked only if that happens.
- **P8. Dunes need sand.** A 32 degree slip face falls inside the scree band and above the grass limit (F17), so untreated dunes would be drawn as scree with cobbles. T7-8 carries a dune weight into the biome weights (the sand layer exists in every tier) and suppresses scree and rock classes there, behind bit 11. The same carrier gives cove beaches their sand (P10).
- **P9. Hummock height is tied to spacing.** With a slope ceiling s, a knoll of height H on spacing L has peak slope about pi x H / L. With the user's 14 degree ceiling for hummock country that is a height of about one thirteenth of the spacing at most (15 m on 190 m; est.); T7-1 fixes the ratio.
- **P10. Coves cut land, by construction without touching rivers, sites or inland water** (user, 2026-11-21).
  - **Shape.** Each coastal cell has a few hashed candidate centres. A candidate becomes a cove only if its centre lies on the natural shoreline and a probe seaward of it is open sea. The shoreline test uses the signed shore distance `continental / gradient`, the estimate the ocean profile already uses (`TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs:203, 274`). Inside the cove's **water radius** the height becomes `min(height, -depth)`, strictly below 0. From the water radius to the **outer radius** it becomes `min(height, ramp)`, with the ramp strictly above 0. Beyond the outer radius nothing changes.
  - **Order.** Cliffs and headlands raise first; the cove cut comes after. A cove in a cliffed coast therefore gets cliff walls, and one in a low coast gets a beach.
  - **No new inland water.** Every point T7 pushes below 0 lies inside a water disc. The whole disc is below 0 and contains open-sea points, so it is one body with the sea. Outside the discs no term may push land to 0 or below (S7 as before).
  - **Rivers, lakes and sites.** A candidate is rejected, once per cell and cached, if a channel, a lake, a dry basin or a site disc lies within its outer radius plus 300 m (the keep-out distance the rivers already use). The corridor fade and the water rule S8 therefore never meet a cove. The drainage source never sees it (P1). Consequence: there are no bays at river mouths.
  - **Sea ownership.** Ocean is "presentation height below 0" in the sampler (F19); proxy ocean geometry and the FFT ocean coverage atlas are built from the sectors' own heights. Cove water is therefore ocean wherever height decides. T7-1 lists every consumer and checks it. One known exception reads the natural continental field instead: the beach sand weight (`RingTerrainSampler.cs:839`), which is why cove beaches take their sand from the T7-8 carrier.
  - **Coarse levels.** With a footprint the cove's radii shrink (never below zero) instead of its height fading, so the "whole disc below 0" rule holds at every level. Small coves vanish on far levels and the far shoreline is the natural one. The pyramid's minimum is padded by the largest cove depth in coastal cells.
  - **GPU.** A sector within reach of any cove candidate is built on the CPU, as river and pond sectors are. `hm_meso` holds no cove code, because the keep-out needs the river graph, which the GPU does not have. Coves are sparse, so the GPU share lost is small (reported, C8).

### What the slope ceiling buys (est.)

Generation 1 plains already reach 7.71 degrees at p95 from kilometre-scale rolling. Treating old and new slopes as independent, the relief T7 can add under a ceiling is roughly:

| Plains ceiling (p95 at 4 m) | Added relief possible | Reaching 2 m RMS (target R2) needs swells of |
|---|---|---|
| 10 degrees | about 1.0 m RMS per 100 m of wavelength | about 190 m or longer |
| 12 degrees | about 1.5 m RMS per 100 m of wavelength | about 130 m or longer |

Under either ceiling the upper end of R1 (0.60 m at 2-30 m) cannot be reached; T7-1 will narrow R1.

### D3. Terms

Common rules for every term: pure functions of `(around, across, f)` and the recipe; seam-periodic (lattices with an integer cell count around the ring, as `EnhancedCellGullyField`, `TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs:470-517`); C0; zero mean or an explicit mean at large footprint; zero outside the eligibility masks; exit early when their weight is 0.

**Meso noise.** All T7 bands use one new noise, `MesoNoise`: a 2D gradient (Perlin-style) noise on an integer lattice, around-count `round(C / period)`, across period `period`, quintic fade, gradients from the `CellJitter` integer hash (`TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs:575`) with a distinct "octave" code per band, two octaves (period, period / 2; gain 0.5), normalised to unit RMS by a constant measured once (T7-4). Reasons: it is seam-periodic by construction, about 70 ns per octave in C# (est.) against about 150 ns for a 3D FastNoiseLite simplex sample on the cylinder, and it ports to GLSL bit-for-bit in integer arithmetic plus df64 cell indices (D7), which the FastNoiseLite port does not. Lattice and hash codes: ripple 32, swell 33, style 34, coast style 35, cliff height 36, cliff notch 37, dune crest 38, dune wind 39, cliff retreat 43; cell lattices: hummock 40, butte 41, cove 42. The prototype used FastNoiseLite simplex with measured normalisation (RMS 0.311 per unit amplitude, proto); the spectra are close enough for calibration to carry over, and T7-4 re-measures.

**Seed offsets** not in F4 (used only to derive per-feature seeds, `seed + offset`, for the hash): 1601 style, 1607 ripple, 1609 swell, 1613 coast style, 1619 cliff height, 1621 cliff notch, 1623 cliff retreat, 1627 dune crest, 1631 dune wind, 1637 hummock cells, 1657 butte cells, 1663 cove cells.

**Eligibility.** `E = 1 - S(0.02, 0.2, AlpineWeight)`. Not `LowlandWeight`: that is `land * (1 - belt)` with `land = S(0, 0.12, c)`, so it is near 0 for the first 5-10 km inland and the prototype's first run left the whole coastal plain flat (proto v1). Land and sea are handled by the shore distance and the floor rule instead.

**Land-side shore distance `d_s`.** For `0 < c <= 0.06`: `d_s = c / g`, with `g` the gradient magnitude of the continental field in the warped frame, computed as at `TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs:268-273` (central differences at `_continentalDerivativeEpsilon`, floor `1/(32 * continental_scale_m)`). For `c <= 0` it is `-(the ocean-side distance)` and for `c > 0.06` it is `+infinity`. This is the estimate the ocean profile already uses, so `d_s = 0` exactly on the natural shoreline (`c = 0`), where the natural height is 0. Cost control: `g` is read from a per-worker cache on a 256 m lattice (bilinear over the 4 corners, each corner's 4 continental samples computed once), so a coastal sample costs one continental sample plus the interpolation (est. 0.1 us amortised). The interpolated `g` is continuous, so `d_s` is C0. The stage needs the warped coordinates: `SampleLandformNaturalDiagnostics` appends `WarpedAround` and `WarpedAcross` to `Diagnostics` (new fields with defaults; no arithmetic changes).

**Shore fade.** `F_shore = S(0, 150, d_s)` (0 at and below the shoreline). Ripple, swell, hummocks, buttes and dunes are multiplied by it; cliffs and coves have their own shoreline rules.

**Style partition.** Style value `u = U(MesoNoise_style(period meso_style_scale_m))`.
- Hummock weight `w_h = S(t_h, t_h + 0.08, u) * (1 - S(0.35, 0.7, Ruggedness)) * S(150, 400, d_s) * E`, with `t_h` from `hummock_share` (D2 table). Hummock country sits on plains and rolling ground, never within 150 m of the shore. Proto: threshold 0.58 gives 22.3 % of eligible lowland land with `w_h >= 0.5` (S1 target 15-30 %).
- Butte weight `w_b = S(t_p - 0.06, t_p - 0.03, v) * (1 - S(t_p - 0.01, t_p + 0.01, v)) * E`, where `v = U(plateau noise)` (appended diagnostics field `PlateauRaw`) and `t_p = plateau_threshold`: a band a few kilometres wide just outside the plateau edge (plateau noise period 64 km).
- Coastal weight `w_c = 1 - S(800, 1500, d_s)` for `d_s > 0`, else 0. Cliff style inside it: `w_cl = S(t_c, t_c + 0.06, U(MesoNoise_coast(24 km)))`, `t_c` from `coastal_cliff_share` by the same CDF-table method; dune style is `w_d = w_c * (1 - w_cl)`.
- The weights are not normalised against each other: hummocks and buttes have disjoint masks (ruggedness and plateau band versus the 400 m shore margin and the coast band they can meet only at the shore margin), and coastal terms act only in the first 1.5 km. Their sum is at most 1 wherever two of them overlap, because `S(150, 400, d_s)` and `w_c` are both partial there; T7-4's `invariants` asserts `w_h + w_b + w_c <= 1 + 1e-9`.

**Baseline roughness** (everywhere eligible, plains to hill country, the coastal plain included):
`delta_base = F * F_shore * (A_r * N_r * fade(f, L_r) + (1 - 0.6 w_h) * A_s * N_s * fade(f, L_s))`
with `A_r = meso_roughness_m`, `L_r = meso_roughness_scale_m`, `A_s = meso_swell_m`, `L_s = meso_swell_scale_m`, and `F` the river and site fade of D4. Mean 0. In hummock country the swell is cut by 60 % so hummocks, not swell, spend the slope budget. Slope arithmetic against R3: see D10 (the prototype shows the ripple costs more slope per metre of RMS than anything else).

**Hummocks and knolls.** Square cell lattice of period `L = hummock_spacing_m` (integer count around). Cell `(i,k)` has a centre at `CellJitter` offsets (0.3-0.7 of the cell) and hashes `h1..h6`.
- Type: knoll if `h1 < 0.55`; hollow if `h1 > 1 - pond_abundance`; else none.
- Radius `R = L (0.45 + 0.25 h2)`; ellipse axis ratio `0.7 + 0.3 h3`, orientation `pi h4`; knoll height `H = min(hummock_height_m, L/13) (0.6 + 0.4 h5)`; hollow depth `0.8 H`.
- Kernel `k(q) = (1 - q^2)^3` for `q < 1` (q the elliptical normalised radius), C2 at the edge; peak slope about `1.72 H / R`.
- `delta_h = F * w_h * fade(f, L) * sum over the 3x3 neighbour cells (+H k for knolls, -0.8 H k for hollows)`. Mean: the knoll/hollow mix makes the cell mean about `+0.03 H` (est.); the fade goes to 0, so at large footprints hummock country sits that much lower than its fine-level average, well inside the coarse-level tolerance (C3).
- Ratio (P9): `H/L = 1/15` by default, at most `1/13`. Proto: L = 120, 180 and 260 m with H/L = 1/15 all give hummock-country p95 slope 13.4-13.9 degrees at 4 m with every term on (ceiling 14).
- Per-worker cache: the 3x3 cells' parameters keyed by the base cell, refilled only when the base cell changes (pattern `RefillGullyStencil`, `TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs:559`).

**Buttes.** Cell lattice of period 4 km (code 41). A cell holds a butte when `hash < butte_frequency`, `w_b >= 0.5` and `LandformGate >= 0.9` at its jittered centre, and neither the site mask nor a corridor weight above 0 lies within `R_t + riser + 300 m` (checked once per cell, cached). Top level `T = plateau_step_m * ceil((B_c + 60) / plateau_step_m)`, plus one more step with chance 0.35 (`B_c` = the height after carve at the centre), so every top is a ring-wide bench level, one or two steps (160-320 m) above its foot. Top radius `R_t = 250 + 450 h` m with an angular modulation of up to 15 % (harmonics 2-4). Profile with `x = r - R_t(theta)`: `T` for `x <= 0`, then a riser of 60 degrees, then a talus of 32 degrees down to the ground: `P_b = T - max(0, x) * tan 60` above the talus line, `P_b = T - x_t tan 60 - (x - x_t) tan 32` below it (`x_t = 0.45 (T - B_c) / tan 60`). Result `smax(h, P_b, 8 m)` with the compact polynomial smooth maximum, so the top is exactly `T` wherever `T - h >= 8 m` (flat within 0.5 m over 80 % of the top: T7-6 check). Footprint: the riser run is `max((T - B_c)/tan 60, 0.5 f)`, so buttes widen instead of fading and stay landmarks to 256 m spacing.

**Cliffs and headlands (raise only).** For `d_s > 0` and `h_c > 0`:
`delta_cl = F * w_cl * H_c * S(0, W_face, d_s) * (1 - S(400, 1600, d_s))`, with `H_c = coastal_cliff_height_m * (0.6 + 0.4 U(N_cliffheight, 6 km)) * (0.75 + 0.25 U(N_notch, 200 m))` and `W_face = max(0.7 H_c, f)`. The face reaches about 60-65 degrees (ground material: rock above 38-46 degrees, F17), the top is level for about 400 m and returns to the coastal plain over 1.2 km (about 1.3 degrees). It is never negative and is exactly 0 at `d_s <= 0`, so ocean heights are unchanged. Headlands are not a separate raise: they are the cliffed stretches left standing between coves (cove acceptance is higher in cliff style, below), which is how real headlands read. `LandformGate` and every natural diagnostic keep their meaning; the new weights are new fields (`CoastalWeight`, `CliffWeight`).

**Cliff retreat (small bites in a cliff line).** The natural shoreline is smooth below about 10 km (the continental field's shortest octave is 37.5 km), so a raised cliff alone traces a smooth curve, which the v2 pictures show as a ruled line. In cliff style the cliff line therefore steps back from the natural shore by `r = cliff_retreat_m * w_cl * U(N_retreat(500 m))^1.5 * F_ret` (default 120 m, a new D2 row `cliff_retreat_m`, range 0-250, slider), with `F_ret` rising from 0 at 300 m to 1 at 450 m from any channel (the carve's hits reach 300 m from the centreline) and 0 in the site mask. Profile on `d_s`: `P_r = -2 S(0, 20, d_s) S(0, 20, r - d_s)` for `0 < d_s < r` (a shallow rock platform, strictly below 0 where `r > 0`), then `P_r = tan(60) (d_s - r)` (the foot of the face); applied as `h = min(h, P_r)` after the cliff raise and before the coves; the cliff face uses `d' = d_s - r` in its face smoothstep. Every point it pushes below 0 lies between the natural shoreline and the retreated foot on the same shore normal, so it is connected to the sea (S7b); S7 counts these bands with the cove discs; S11 extends to them through `F_ret`. Footprint: `r_f = max(0, r - 0.5 f)`. Proto: v3 (see the pictures section).

**Coves (P10).** Cell lattice of period 6 km (code 42), one candidate per cell. Decided once per cell and cached per worker (3x3 cells around the sample):
1. Candidate `Q` at the cell's jittered point. Skip unless `|c(Q)| <= 0.06`.
2. Project to the natural shoreline: two Newton steps `O = Q - c grad c / |grad c|^2` (gradient as for `d_s`, warped frame). Accept the position only if `|c(O)/g(O)| < 20 m` and `|O - Q| <= 3 km` (so the 3x3 lookup always sees every cove that can reach a sample).
3. Inland normal `n = grad c / |grad c|` at `O`.
4. Size from the hash stream: water radius `Rw = 100 * (cove_width_max_m / 200)^h2` (log-uniform, 200 m to the maximum width); inset `i = 0.35 + 0.5 h3`; disc centre `O' = O + n i Rw`; outline `rho(theta) = Rw max(0.35, 1 + sum_{k=2..7} (0.45 a_k / k) cos(k theta + 2 pi b_k))` (star-shaped about `O'`); depth `D = cove_depth_m clamp(sqrt(Rw / 750), 0.4, 1.5)`; beach width `W_b = (30 + 50 h8) (1 - 0.7 w_cl(O))`; beach slope `m_b = 0.06`; wall angle `alpha = 50 + 20 h9` degrees.
5. Acceptance: `hash < cove_frequency (0.5 + 0.5 w_cl(O))`; **open-sea probe**: the natural height at `O - n (Rw + 300)` is below -1 m and is not inland water; **keep-out**: no channel within `Ro + 300` (`RiverCarveOperator.ChannelsNear(O, min(Ro + 300, 2300))`, F6), no lake or dry basin (`RiverGraph.LakeAt`, `DryBasinAt`) and no site influence (the site mask of D4, which covers the weather lung too) on a 100 m probe grid over the disc of radius `Ro + 300`. `Ro = rho_max + W_b + 200 / tan(alpha)` is the outer radius. Every rejection is counted by cause (T7-7b test output). Because `Ro + 300 <= 2300 m` for the largest cove, `ChannelsNear` is complete (its documented limit).
6. Profile, with `r = |x - O'|`, `rho = rho(theta)`, `e = r - rho`: water `P = -D (1 - (r/rho)^2)` for `e < 0`; beach `P = m_b e` for `0 <= e < W_b`; wall `P = m_b W_b + tan(alpha) (e - W_b)` beyond. `P` is continuous, strictly below 0 inside the outline and strictly above 0 outside it, and exceeds 200 m at `Ro`, above any coastal land within reach (candidates whose natural height within the disc exceeds 150 m are rejected), so `min(h, P) = h` at `Ro` and the cut is C0.
7. Applied after every other term: `h = min(h, P)` for each accepted cove whose `Ro` covers the point. In a cliffed coast the wall of `P` cuts the raised cliff top and becomes the cove's cliff; in a low coast the beach ramp meets the plain before the wall starts, so the cove gets a beach. Heights already below 0 can only go deeper.
8. Connectivity (S7b) by construction: the outline is star-shaped about `O'`, the whole interior is below 0, and it contains the open-sea side of `O` (the inset is at most 0.85, so the outline crosses the shoreline and the probe direction), so every point T7 pushes below 0 is connected to the sea through the disc.
9. Footprint: `rho_f = rho - 0.5 f`, `Rw_f = Rw - 0.5 f`; the cove is absent when `Rw_f < max(f, 50 m)`; `W_b` and `alpha` unchanged. Small coves therefore vanish on far levels and the far coastline is the natural one; S7b holds at every footprint because the shrunken outline is still star-shaped and its interior still below 0.
- Proto (seed 1337, around 85-385 km): 2,397 cells, 46 accepted; rejections by cause: no shore band 1,928, not on the shore 200, projection too far 123, frequency 90, river channel 7, site 3; 6.2 coves per 100 km of natural coastline in the review window (S3b target 3-10).

**Strand dunes** (no prototype pictures; formulas only). In dune style (`w_d`) between `d_s = 60` and `900 m`: a transverse dune train. Wind direction `phi = angle(-n) + 0.5 (N_wind(20 km) - 0.5)` radians (onshore, swinging up to about 15 degrees), from the recipe seed and position. Along the wind, phase `s = (x . w) / dune_spacing_m + 0.6 N_crest(3 dune spacings)` (crest lines wander); profile per period `p = frac(s)`: windward rise `p / 0.9` for `p < 0.9`, slip face `(1 - p) / 0.1` beyond, smoothed at crest and toe with a 4 m rounding, height `dune_height_m (0.6 + 0.4 U(N_crest))`. Slopes at 6 m on 90 m: windward `atan(H / (0.9 spacing))` = 4 degrees, slip face `atan(H / (0.1 spacing))` = 34 degrees before rounding; T7-8 calibrates the slip share (0.08-0.12) so the slip faces land at 28-34 degrees at p90. Weight `F * w_d * S(60, 160, d_s) * (1 - S(600, 900, d_s))`; footprint fade `fade(f, dune_spacing_m)`; mean `0.5 H`, so the fade goes to `0.5 H` (not 0).

**Footprint fade.** `fade(f, L) = 1 - S(0.25 L, 0.5 L, f)` for every band term, with its mean where stated (P6 tier 1).
### D4. Stage, fades and floors

**Insertion points** (`TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs`, lines of 2026-11-21). One private method `MesoStage(around, across, footprint, in Diagnostics natural, in RiverOperatorResult? river)` returns the post-meso height and its diagnostics. It is called:
- `CalculateHeight(around, across, footprint)` (132-137): between the carve (135) and the sites (136).
- `SampleDiagnostics(around, across, footprint)` (159-170): after the river block (162-166), before the sites (167).
- `CalculateHeight(around, across)` (125-130): today it returns the natural height directly when the model has no river operator and no sites. With `HasMesoRelief` it must route through the 3-argument form with footprint 0 (the D8a sweep found this branch; missing it would leave a river-less shaped model without relief).
- **Not** `SampleNaturalDiagnostics` or `SampleLandformNaturalDiagnostics` (172, 248). `CpuDrainageHeightSource` and `Gen17NaturalHeightSource` build models with `applyEmitterSites = false` and read only the natural path, so they cannot see the stage (P1). The stage runs only on shaped models (`applyEmitterSites = true`).
- Order: natural, carve, meso (base, hummocks, buttes, cliffs, dunes, then coves), floor rules, sites.

**River fade.** `F_river = 1 - CorridorWeight` (F6: 1 on bed and bank, 0 beyond bank + 60 m, lake weight included). It is computed from the hits the carve already collected: `MesoStage` receives the operator's result, and the operator gains one read-only output (next item), so no second `Collect` runs.

**Water floor for S8.** `RiverCarveOperator` gains `EvaluateWithNearWater(around, across, natural, footprint, out double nearLevel)`, sharing `Evaluate`'s code path: `nearLevel` is the highest displayed level among the hits whose `|t| <= halfWidth + bankWidth + 150 m`, and the lake level where the lake indicator `P > -1` (any of the four stencil corners is a lake or ring cell, so 150 m from a rim is covered while the drainage cell is at least 150 m), else NaN. `Evaluate` and `CorridorWeight` are unchanged and the graph is untouched, so `river_carve_invariants_test` and the graph hash stay identical; `nearLevel` reuses data the hits already hold (est. under 20 ns). **So a lake-rim accessor is needed, but internal: the lake membership is read inside the operator and never exposed.** Hits reach 300 m from the centreline (`FadeEndMeters`), enough for half-widths up to 150 m.

**Site fade.** `F_site` = 0 inside every site's outer influence (emitter sites and the weather lung, where `SiteWeight > 0` or `WeatherLungWeight > 0` can occur), rising to 1 over a 200 m margin. A new read-only `EmitterSiteTerrainShaper.MesoMask(around, across)` gives it from the registry's existing spatial lookup (it changes no shaper output). S9 then holds exactly: the site operator sees the carve height wherever it acts.

**Combined fade.** `F = F_river * F_site * E`. Every term except the cove cut is multiplied by `F`; the cove never meets `F < 1` because of its keep-out.

**Floor rules**, in this order after the terms:
1. S7 (no land pushed to 0 or below): if `h_c > 0`, `h = max(h_c + delta, min(h_c, 0.5))`; if `h_c <= 0`, `h = h_c` (no term acts on sea or on ground already under 0). Continuous in `h_c` and `delta`.
2. S8: if `nearLevel` is finite, `h = max(h, min(h_c, nearLevel + 0.3))`: T7 never lowers ground below the adjacent water plus 0.3 m, and never lowers ground that already stands below that line.
3. Coves: `h = min(h, P)` (D3). The only operation that pushes ground under 0, and only inside accepted water outlines.
4. Sites (existing operator).

### D5. Hollows and ponds

- **Pond-ready cell** (a hollow cell of D3) when, at its centre: `w_h >= 0.5`, `F = 1` over the cell's disc (corridor, site mask) and `d_s >= 400 m` (so never in the beach profile), and its base tilt is small: `|grad h_rest| R <= 0.5 D_hollow`, where `h_rest` is the height of everything but the hollow, from the rim probes below.
- **Level by construction.** Probe the rest height on 32 rim points at `q = 1.05`; `level = min(probes) - 0.3 - 0.5 max_k |probe_{k+1} - probe_k|` (the last term bounds a dip between probes for a field whose slope is bounded over one probe gap). The cell is pond-ready only if `level >= 0.5` (water above sea level by construction) and the hollow floor (centre height) is at most `level - 0.3`.
- Water is drawn only where `q < 1` and the height is below `level`, so the pond lies inside the hollow; the rim outside `q = 1` is the rest field, at least `level + 0.3` at the probes and above the level between them by the bound. T7-5's 64-ray test is the check; if it fails anywhere, escalation to `claude-deep` (as written in T7-1).
- Cache: the level (or "none") per cell, in the hummock per-worker cache; one cell costs 33 rest-height samples (est. 33 x 3 us = 0.1 ms), paid once per worker per cell; a 180 m cell holds about 32,000 samples at 1 m, so the amortised cost is about 3 ns per sample.
- Proto (2 km window, defaults of v1, hollow share 0.20, depth 0.6 H): 0.8 ponds per km2 at L = 180 (median 3,800 m2), 2.8 per km2 at L = 120. v2 raises the hollow share to 0.35 and the depth to 0.8 H; T7-5 calibrates against S2 (2-10 per km2).

### D6. Footprints

- Fade function: D3 (`fade(f, L)`), per term with its mean. Coves shrink their radii; buttes and cliffs widen their faces; nothing else is footprint-dependent.
- **What each caller passes** in worlds with bit 10 (otherwise exactly today's call):
  - CPU proxy sectors: `FootprintFor(spacing)` (`RingTerrainProxySector.cs:795`) returns the spacing in meso worlds as in inland-water worlds (0 at 2 m and finer, the spacing up to 256 m).
  - The F16b callers (global LOD, analytic global LOD, far viewport geometry and tiles, the pyramid): their own sample spacing instead of 0.
  - GPU K1 and K2: the level spacing, passed to `hm_meso` (D7).
- **Morph target.** A level's morph target (the parent's surface it morphs onto before hand-off) is sampled at the **parent's** footprint, so the morph ends exactly on what the parent draws.
- **Pyramid.** Max bounds padded by the largest positive meso delta possible in the cell: `4 A_s + 4 A_r + H_max` (hummock), `+ H_c max` (cliff, coastal cells only), and buttes (cells within a butte lattice cell's reach take `T - B` from the cell cache). Min bounds padded by `D_max` of coves (cells with `|c| <= 0.06`) and by `0.8 H_max + 4 A_s` (hollows and swell troughs). T7-9 reports the bound growth.
- **Lodgap metric (C3).** As `LandformTerrainV7Test.LodGap` (`tools/LandformTerrainV7Test.cs:225`): for each spacing `s`, `|delta(mid) - (delta(a) + delta(b)) / 2|` along 24 lines per tile, with `delta` the T7 contribution only (height with the stage minus height without), each sample at footprint `s`. p99 under 2 m per C3. Tiles: the four v7 tiles plus a hummock, a cliff and a butte tile found by the T7 pose finder.

### D7. GLSL

- **Block from 320** (no existing index moves; names start `HM_P_MESO_` so `ParseLayout` accepts them, F13):
  - 320-337 the 18 D2 fields in table order (as float bits);
  - 338 hummock threshold `t_h`, 339 cliff threshold `t_c` (derived on the host from the D2 CDF tables);
  - 340-348 the nine `MesoNoise` RMS normalisers (ripple, swell, style, coast style, cliff height, cliff notch, retreat, dune crest, dune wind);
  - 349-357 their nine per-band seeds (int bits); lattice periods are derived in the shader from the fields;
  - 358-359 reserved;
  - `HM_P_MESO_END` 360, the new total for the buffer size. The base noise table is full (F12); `MesoNoise` needs no table, only seeds, which is why the block is small.
- Feature bits come from `RC_P_WORLD_FEATURES` (317); `hm_meso` is a no-op without bit 10.
- File `materials/terrain_gpu/ring_meso_relief.glslinc`, included by `ring_proxy_height_k1.glsl` and `ring_proxy_sector_k2.glsl` only. Signature: `float hm_meso(vec2 around_df, float across, HmSample s, float footprint)` returning the meso delta before floors; K1 and K2 apply the S7 floor (S8 cannot arise in a GPU-routable sector, P3).
- **Shore distance on the GPU.** `hm_sample` is not edited (T7-10 constraint), so `hm_meso` recomputes the warped coordinates (two warp samples) only when `0 < c <= 0.06`, then four continental samples. No cache on the GPU; this is the costliest path and runs on coastal sectors only (est. +40 % on those threads; C4 is measured on plains and hill sectors).
- **Precision.** Cell indices from df64 `around` (the ring is 31.4 Mm; float32 resolves 2 m there): `cell = floor(df64 / period)` in df64, local offset `around - pivot` in df64 then float. Gradients of `MesoNoise` from integer hashes, so CPU and GPU agree bit-for-bit up to the float32 kernel arithmetic (parity tolerance F13).
- **Cove code is CPU-only** (P10); buttes, hummocks, cliffs, the cliff retreat, dunes and the base bands are ported. The retreat's `F_ret` is 1 in every GPU-routable sector (no channel within 1 km, F15); the T7-10 routing proof asserts it with `CorridorWeight`.
- **Butte acceptance without the graph.** The GPU evaluates only the stateless part of a butte cell's acceptance (hash, `w_b`, `LandformGate`). A cell that passes it but fails the keep-out (rivers, sites) is "vetoed" on the CPU, and `GpuSectorRoutable` (`RingTerrainProxySector.GpuHeights.cs:75-85`) sends every sector within a vetoed cell's reach to the CPU (a stateless per-cell test, cached per worker, beside the cove test). In GPU-routed sectors the stateless test therefore equals the CPU's acceptance. Vetoes are rare (est. under 10 % of butte cells; T7-10 reports the count).
- **Drainage contract:** `ring_drainage_sample.glsl` never includes the meso file (T7-10 test greps the include graph) and the GPU bake hash equals the rivers generation's (S10).

### D8. Pond water (T7-11)

- **Sampler fields.** The stage sets `InlandWater = Lake` and `WaterLevel = level` (D5) where the river operator reported none and the point lies inside a pond-ready cell's `q < 1` with height below the level. `RiverReach = -1`, `DryBasin = -1`. The sampler's classification at `RingTerrainSampler.SurfaceGeneration.cs:200-206` then makes it Lake water with no change (F19: kind and level suffice).
- **River-bed mask guard.** `CreateRiverBedMaskLocked` calls `graph.LakeAt` for Lake samples (F19, 135-138); a pond has no graph lake, so the guard is "Lake kind and `graph.LakeAt` returns -1 means a pond: skip it". A new diagnostics flag `MesoPond` makes this explicit rather than inferred.
- **Routing.** `GpuSectorRoutable` gains `touchesPond`: a stateless per-cell test (any pond-ready cell within the sector bounds). Ponds are built on the CPU like lakes (F15).
- **Draw distance.** Ponds are drawn on levels up to 32 m spacing; from 64 m spacing the hollow fades with its footprint (D3) and the pond is not drawn (a 3,800 m2 pond is about 70 m across). The minimap takes the lake tint from the sampler (`RingTerrainVisuals.cs:209`) with no change.
- **Trigger for a GPU lake class** (C8): if pond routing leaves under 50 % of the no-pond GPU share at a hummock pose with ponds, P7's fallback question goes to the user (dry hollows freeze, ponds follow as their own feature).

### D9. Sand carrier

- **C#.** `RingTerrainSampler.GetNaturalBiomeTextureWeights` (the beach weight at `RingTerrainSampler.cs:839-841`) becomes, behind bit 11: `beach' = max(beach * (1 - cliffSand), duneW, coveBeachW)` where `cliffSand = w_cl * S(0, W_face, d_s) * (1 - S(400, 1600, d_s))` (no sand on cliff faces and tops), `duneW` the dune weight, `coveBeachW = S(-0.5, 0, e) * (1 - S(W_b - 5, W_b + 5, e))` on the cove's beach ring (0 on the wall). The three weights come from the stage's diagnostics (`DuneWeight`, `CoveBeachWeight`, `CliffWeight`, appended fields); the sample already carries the shaped diagnostics.
- **GLSL.** `rc_biome_weights` (`ring_regional_climate.glslinc`, beach band at 217) takes `max(beach * (1 - cliffSand), duneW)` behind `ring_world_has(11)`; cove beaches need no GLSL term because cove sectors are CPU-built (P10). The cliff and dune weights are recomputed in the shader from the packed fields (same functions as `hm_meso`), so **no new per-vertex channel** is needed.
- **Scree and rock suppression** (`materials/ring_ground_surface.gdshaderinc:44-106` and its mirror `RingGroundSurfaceModel.cs`): behind `ring_world_has(11)`, the scree and rock weights are multiplied by `1 - S(0.4, 0.7, sandWeight)` where the sand weight is the carrier's, so a 32 degree slip face is drawn as sand, not scree. Cliff faces keep rock (their sand weight is 0 by `cliffSand`).
- Grass and the habitat grass share follow the carrier through `Desert` (D8a).

## Risks

- **Ponds touch the most systems** (sampler, proxies, routing, minimap, swim, sound sources, near fog). That is why they are last and separable (P7).
- **Coves put sea where the natural fields still say land.** Anything that reads continentalness instead of height (beach sand today; T7-1's table D8a looks for more) will disagree with the water until it is handled. The keep-out also means coves are missing wherever rivers, lakes or sites crowd the coast.
- **The sand carrier may not fit the existing biome weights** on every tier. T7-8's escalation removes dunes rather than adding a mesh format.
- **Lowland LOD mismatch is new.** Plains are smooth today, so coarse and fine levels agree. With metres of relief they will not, until the cell is resident. T7-9 measures it; a visible transient at streaming time may remain.
- **Height cost reaches everything.** Tree, grass and scatter builds are dominated by height samples (9.5-22 µs each). C1's 1.25x can become 1.25x on fills, which is why C6 and C7 exist.
- **The rivers freeze may slip.** T7-1 and T7-2 do not wait for it; everything else does.
- **Calibration against slope.** R2 and R3 pull against each other (see the ceiling table). If the user wants more relief than the ceiling allows, the ceiling is the thing to change, not the measurement.
