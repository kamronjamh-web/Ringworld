### Relief and habitat inputs

- P12. Relief (T0 8.1, T4 R1 and R2):
  - curvature at 8 m is centimetre noise on lowland until T7, while 64 m curvature carries 0.7-3.9 m RMS relief;
  - slope p95 is 7.7 degrees on plains, 29.2 hill country, 38.8 breaks, 42.3 mountain.
  - So boulders in hollows and at slope feet will mostly follow 64 m curvature and slope breaks before T7, and outcrops will concentrate in mountains and breaks.
  - T4 provides the C# mirror: `RockExposure` (48), `ScreeWeight` (55), `Relief` (64), `NodeConvexity` and `Convexity64` (86-101, 32 m lattice, 64 m taps); `SlopeBiasPerOctaveDeg` was calibrated to 1.0 (16). The proxy builder's C64 (`RingTerrainProxySector.Relief.cs:46-104`) takes node heights from `GetHeightOnly` and `GetTerrainRenderHeight`.
- P13. `TerrainSample.Moisture` and `Temperature` are the climate inputs (wetness without rivers). `ForestRegionSampler.Sample` (`scripts/Vegetation/ForestRegionSampler.cs:48-112`) gives canopy density, habitat and clearing; it returns nothing at 32 degrees or steeper (59). "Height above nearest channel" needs the T2 graph.
  - A height-only sample (`RingLodSurfaceEditSnapshot.GetPresentationHeightOnlyTerrainSampleAndHeight`, 302-318, down to `RingTerrainSampler.GetRuntimeHeightOnlyTerrainSampleAndPresentationHeight`, `SurfaceGeneration.cs:239-264`) gives the height, water and site mask for one model evaluation and no climate.
- P13b. Ring dimensions (`scripts/Voxel/Core/CertifiedRingScale.cs`): circumference 981,748 x 32 m = 31,415,936 m = 2^7 x 245,437, so 8, 16, 32, 64 and 128 m lattices wrap exactly around the ring. The width is 318,000 m: 39,750 cells of 8 m, but 9,937.5 cells of 32 m, so the across edge clamps. The runtime sampler clamps across to width - 2 m (`SurfaceGeneration.cs:317`).

### Cost, re-checked against T0 8.6 and the perf pass

- C1. Plan gates (A1000, Medium): 0.5 ms GPU; 24 draws; at most 1,500 boulders, 150 outcrops and 6,000 cobbles resident; tile build at most 1.3 times the tree-only tile; under 8 MB of mesh data.
- C2. Tile build. P5b replaces the 11-10 medians: on the 4070 the 1.3x gate allows +2.9 ms (plains, hills) to +5.1-7.2 ms (steep, cliff, breaks, mountain) per tile.
  - A naive 8 m habitat grid over a 240 m tile, with a halo wide enough for 64 m curvature, is about 47 x 47 = 2,209 height samples. At 1.15-2.8 us per sample (plan 1.2, v7 cost; est.) that is 2.5-6 ms, about the whole gate before any placement.

- C3. Draws. T0 draw deltas include shadow-pass draws (trees: 151-749 at forest poses; at the save 694 of 757 tree draws are shadow draws, perf pass 0.13). Frames at the user's poses carry 1,284-1,678 draws in all (`docs/perf-bench.md` reference). Batching rocks per mesh and LOD across tiles gives up to 30 meshes x 3 LODs = 90 draws before shadows, against a gate of 24. Design B3 meets the gate, shadows included.
- C4. **How T5 measures cost.**
  - GPU: a new hide class `scatter_hidden` in `tools/landscape_baseline_common.gd` (`CASES` line 22, `HIDE_CLASSES` 23): every scatter renderer and its shadow casters, same-boot A-B-A in a review world. Draw and primitive deltas are exact. The harness counts every geometry node under `ctx["trees"]`, the decoration layer, as tree geometry (`capture_original_state`, 408-460), so scatter renderers must not be its descendants (design B2).
  - CPU tile build: "Scatter build" cannot nest inside "Trees build" without editing `BuildVegetationTile`. Design A3 defines separate worker scopes, added to `FILL_SCOPES` (line 25), and how the 1.3x gate is computed from them.
  - Memory: the prepared rock resource size plus resident instance bytes.
  - The A1000 is authoritative; the 4070 is indicative (T4 C3).

## T5 design A: habitat field grid, class table, records and cache

T5-1, claude-lead, 2026-11-18. Written against `9c1bfbc` plus the working tree, with the Phase facts re-verified the same day. For the primary's review. No GPU or CPU measurement was made for this design: every millisecond below is an estimate from P5b and plan 1.2 unless it cites a measurement. The questions for the user (Q1-Q3) are at the end of design B.

### Summary A

- **Two lattices.** Fields live on a canonical 8 m lattice, with the 32 m T4 C64 lattice under it. Every node value is a pure function of the node and the world, so any tile, layer or worker gets the same bits. Around wraps exactly, because both lattices divide the circumference (P13b); across clamps.
- **Fine where it matters.** The 8 m nodes are evaluated only in 32 m cells where the coarse slope reaches 12 degrees or that touch water. A plains tile costs about 0.7 ms, a mountain tile 4-5 ms.
- **Stages by reach.** Scatter records are built in their own worker jobs once a tree tile is resident: outcrops for tiles within about 2.8 km, boulders within about 0.9 km, cobbles per 60 m block within about 100 m. `BuildVegetationTile`, `CachedTree`, `VegetationTile` and the tree cache are not edited.
- **One table.** Classes are append-only rows with stable ids. They are built in order of reach and may avoid only classes built before them. T6's rows fit without a schema change.
- **Records** are 44 bytes, in their own file `s_<around>_<across>.bin` under `lod_cache/scatter/<identity>/`, magic `SCT1`. The disk cache is used only once the feature is frozen, and the key carries `ScatterAlgorithmVersion`.
- **Fingerprint:** from the rocks generation on, the `scatter` slot hashes the eight T0 tiles plus `steep_eye` and `scree_eye`.

### A1. Grid definition

**Names.** `scripts/Vegetation/Scatter/RingHabitatFieldGrid.cs` (class `RingHabitatFieldGrid`), the struct `RingHabitatSample` (A2), and node keys `RingHabitatNode(long I, int J)` per level.

**Lattices** (canonical metres):

| level | node (i, j) at | around index | across index | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fine F | around 8i, across 8j | i mod 3,926,992 | 0..39,749 | 39,749 x 8 = 317,992 m |
| coarse C | around 32I, across 32J | I mod 981,748 | 0..9,937 | the T4 D5 lattice |

- Every coarse node is a fine node (i = 4I), and every node of either level is a vertex of the 2 m presentation lattice, so a node height is one lattice vertex, never an interpolation.
- **Ring seam:** around indices wrap modulo the counts above (31,415,936 m divides by 8 and by 32), so node 0 and node N-1 are neighbours and nothing special happens at the seam.
- **Rim:** across is not wrapped (as `ForestRegionSampler.cs:54-55`). An index below 0 or above the last node is clamped to the edge node before evaluation, so a clamped query returns exactly the edge node's value. Placement keeps the tree rule and rejects candidates within 4 m of either rim (`TreeStreaming.cs:1181`).

**Purity.** A node's value depends on the node and the world only: the sampler (recipe, seed, feature mask) and the edit snapshot. No tile, layer, worker count, camera, setting or build order enters. Each job evaluates nodes through its own memo, so a node evaluated twice gives the same bits. Edits are part of the world: edited tiles are rebuilt and skip the disk cache (A6).

**Between nodes.** Placement reads a point by bilinear interpolation over the four nodes of its cell, in double, with the around wrap. Which level a point reads is fixed per coarse cell: if the cell is *fine-flagged* (A3), the point reads the fine nodes; otherwise it reads the coarse nodes. The flag is a pure function of the cell's four coarse corners, so the field at any point is canonical, whatever tile asks.

**Arithmetic.** Heights are the floats the sampler returns. Derived values are computed in double from those floats in a fixed order, then stored as float. Use the same library functions as the tree builder and the T4 mirror (`Math.Atan`, `Math.Acos`, `RingGroundSurfaceModel.SmoothStep`).

**Proof:** `habitat_field_grid_test` (T5-3): the same node from two tiles, both layers (a 240 m tree tile and a 32 m grass patch), 1 and 4 workers, and both sides of the ring seam gives identical bits; a clamped across query equals the edge node; the bilinear value at a node equals the node.

    public float Relief8 => C8M / (MathF.Abs(C8M) + 0.25f);

}

| field | range | level | formula and source |
|---|---|---|---|

"Point" fields are computed from the interpolated inputs at the queried point, not interpolated themselves, so a boulder at a point sees the same rock exposure the T4 rule gives for those inputs.

**What 8 m curvature contributes before T7 (P12).** On lowland, C8 is centimetre noise: |C8| of about 0.04 m gives Relief8 of about 0.14, and with its weight of 0.3 it moves wetness by about 0.04, which nothing can see. In mountains and breaks it resolves gully floors and ledge lips (0.5-2 m, Relief8 0.7-0.9). Boulders and outcrops are therefore driven by slope, rock exposure, scree and C64 before T7, and C8 gains weight by itself once T7 adds 2-30 m relief, with no table change.

**Agreement with the ground.** The terrain applies the same T4 functions on a 4 m baseline (Terrain3D) or twice the level spacing (proxies); the grid uses 16 m plus T4's bias. The two agree statistically, not per pixel. `scatter_rock_placement_test` (T5-5) adds: at least 80 % of outcrop roots have terrain rock exposure of at least 0.3 on the 4 m baseline (`ProbeSlopeRise` slope at the root).

**Proof:** `habitat_field_grid_test`: a plane gives C8 = C64 = 0 and the analytic slope; a paraboloid gives the right sign and magnitude for both curvatures; a 50 degree plane gives rock exposure 1; C64 at 50 coarse nodes equals `RingGroundSurfaceModel.Convexity64` and the proxy Lod1 builder's node values bit for bit (away from rivers); rock exposure and scree equal the mirror's functions at 64 sampled points.

### A3. Sampling cost

**Sample counts.**

| unit | fine nodes | coarse heights (incl. C64 taps) | climate samples | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 240 m tree tile, every cell flagged | 35 x 35 = 1,225 (tile plus 16 m each side) | 14 x 14 = 196, of which about 100 lie outside the fine window | about 100 (the coarse nodes over the window) | about 1,325 model evaluations and 100 climate samples |
| 240 m tree tile, plains (about 5 % of cells flagged) | about 60-120 | 196 | about 100 | about 300 model evaluations |
| 32 m grass tile (aligned to the coarse lattice), every cell flagged | 7 x 7 = 49 | 4 corners plus 16 taps | 4 | 65 model evaluations, 4 climate samples |
| 32 m grass tile, plains | 0 | 20 | 4 | |

**How the design keeps the count down** (C2's three levers, and one more):
1. *Alignment to the 2 m lattice.* Every node is a lattice vertex (A1), so a node costs one model evaluation. Placement's exact root height (A5) goes through the job sampler's lattice memo (`EnablePresentationLatticeCache`), so neighbouring candidates share vertices.
2. *A coarser sub-grid for 64 m curvature.* C64 comes from the 32 m lattice, 196 heights per tile instead of the naive 47 x 47.
3. *Halo.* The dense window is the tile plus 16 m: one node ring for the gradients and one for spacing peers of boulders and cobbles. Anything farther (outcrop peers, halo trees) is evaluated lazily through the job's memo.
4. *Fine only where it can matter* (new). A coarse cell is **fine-flagged** when the largest coarse slope of its four corners is at least `HabitatFineSlopeDeg` = 12, or any corner has `Water`. Scree starts at 26 degrees and rock at 32 at the earliest (T4 D2, D4). For a cell to need fine values with all four corners under 12 degrees on a 64 m baseline, a scarp under 32 m wide would have to reach 26 degrees on a 16 m baseline; R2 and R3 say that is rare outside mountains. Water flags the shore cells so shorelines use 8 m water flags.
   - Proof: `habitat_field_grid_test` builds the 14 test tiles (eight T0, six dressing) once with every cell flagged and once with the rule, and requires identical records for every rock class (with T5-5's rows; before T5-5, identical rock exposure and scree above 0.01 at every fine node of flagged and unflagged cells). If anything differs, the threshold drops by 2 degrees until it passes, and the cost line is re-measured.

**Per-stage work.** The stages are defined in A6.
- Stage O (outcrops, tiles from about 0.9 to 2.8 km): the coarse window, and climate and fine nodes only around the 16-25 outcrop candidates per tile that pass a coarse pre-test (A5). On lowland nothing passes.
- Stage B (boulders, tiles within about 0.9 km): the dense window, every candidate.
- Stage C (cobbles, 60 m blocks within about 100 m): it reuses the tile's fine window, which stage B keeps in memory (A4); otherwise it evaluates the block's own window (about 12 x 12 nodes).

**Estimate per tile** (4070 under fill load; 1.2-1.5 us per model evaluation on lowland and 2.5-3.0 us in mountains, inferred from P5b and plan 1.2; about 2 us per climate sample; 0.3 us of arithmetic per fine node; placement from A5's candidate counts with about 6 us per accepted root):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| plains, coast | 9.6-10.0 | about 5 % | 0.3 | 0.6 | 0.1 | 1.07-1.10 |
| hills | 9.7 | about 60 % | 0.4 | 1.4-1.7 | 0.3-0.6 | 1.21-1.28 |
| forest interior, edge, clearing, lowland | 14.0-19.3 | 20-60 % | 0.3-0.4 | 0.7-1.7 | 0.2-0.4 | 1.06-1.18 |
| steep, scree | 18.4-20.2 | about 100 % | 1.0-1.5 | 3.7-4.4 | 0.8-1.5 | 1.27-1.40 |
| cliff, breaks | 17.0-17.3 | about 100 % | 1.0-1.5 | 3.7-4.4 | 0.8-1.5 | 1.32-1.43 |
| mountain_2km | 24.0 | about 100 % | 1.0-1.5 | 3.7-4.4 | 0.8-1.5 | 1.23-1.31 |

A tile only gets stage O and stage B both when it comes within stage B's reach, so the last column is the worst case: the tile built for trees, then outcrops, then boulders. Cobble blocks have their own line: at most about 2 ms per 60 m block on full scree (about 180 clusters, each about 2.5 new lattice vertices for its exact height, plus spacing).

**Read the gate honestly.** Lowland, hill and forest tiles meet 1.3x. **Steep, scree, cliff and breaks tiles are estimated at 1.27-1.43x, at or over the gate**, because their fine window is fully evaluated. Per pose the share is small: stage B reaches only about 45 tiles against about 1,800 tree tiles in a fill, so the fill total rises by about 1-3 % (mountain pose: 430 x 1.2 + 45 x 5.5 ms, about 0.8 s, against about 30 s of tree builds; plains about 0.2 s against 18 s). Per the order, the gate is not relaxed here: T5-10 measures it per tile (below) and reports. The mitigations, in order, each measured before the next:
1. Reuse the tree build's lattice memo: hoist the sampler's disposal out of `BuildVegetationTile` and run stage B in the same task when the tile is built inside stage B's reach (load and teleports). Output-neutral (the fingerprint proves it), but it edits the generation 1 function, which is an escalation condition, so the primary decides. Estimate: 20-30 % fewer fine evaluations on forested steep tiles, less on bare alpine ones.
2. In cells whose four coarse corners are dry and unmasked, take fine heights from the plain cached height call instead of the height-only diagnostics sample (the flags come from the corners). Saves whatever the diagnostics cost over a plain height; measure in T5-3.
3. Raise `HabitatFineSlopeDeg` in 2 degree steps while the identity check above still passes.

The gate question goes to the user as Q1.

**Measurement (C4).** New worker scopes: "Scatter build O", "Scatter build B", "Scatter build C" (per stage job) and "Habitat grid" nested in each, added to `FILL_SCOPES`. "Trees build" is unchanged by construction. The gate, per pose in the review world against the same pose in generation 1:
- per tile, for tiles that got stage B: median and p95 of (Trees build + Scatter build O + Scatter build B) / Trees build of the same tile, at most 1.3;
- per pose fill totals (sum ms of all scopes), reported;
- cobble blocks: "Scatter build C" p95 at most 3 ms per block, and zero coverage waits in the travel benchmarks (Q1).

**Proof:** `habitat_field_grid_test` prints the cost per 240 m tile against this table (escalate at more than 50 % over, T5-3); the build-time rows of T5-10.

### A4. Who computes the grid

- **Tree tiles**, inside their scatter jobs, for their own classes. Stage B keeps its fine window with the tile's scatter state (1,225 nodes x 48 bytes = 59 KB; at most about 45 tiles, 2.6 MB), so stage C, and later T6's near classes, reuse it. The window is dropped when the tile leaves stage B.
- **The grass builder (T6)** recomputes its own 32 m patch with the same `RingHabitatFieldGrid` on its own worker sampler (the pooled one or `ownedSampler`, `RingStandingGrassLayer.cs:653-660`). Cost: 65 model evaluations and 4 climate samples on rough ground, 20 and 4 on plains, about 0.03-0.2 ms, which is +1-5 % on today's 2.2-4.6 ms grass tiles. Recomputing is chosen over a shared cache: grass tiles reach 400 m and build on their own workers, so reading the tree tile's window would make a grass tile wait for a tree tile's stage B and couple the two streamers. Identity of the values is by construction (A1).
- **Proof:** `habitat_field_grid_test` compares a grass patch's nodes with the covering tree tile's window nodes, bit for bit.

**Build order and interactions.**
- Rows are built in the order of `ReachM` descending, ties by `Id`. A row may list in `Avoid` only rows that come earlier in that order. Larger, longer-reach objects are placed first and smaller ones fit around them, and a frozen row can never come to depend on a later one: a T6 row may avoid rocks, rocks never avoid T6 rows. `scatter_tile_determinism_test` validates the rule, along with unique ids, cell sizes, enum ranges and the frozen rows' hashes.
- Every row avoids trees by `TreeClearance`. Trees are never moved by T5 (A7).
- Every row rejects a candidate when any of the four nodes around it has `Water` or `SiteBlocked`, and within 4 m of a rim.
- Frozen rows are listed with their hashes in `tools/fixtures/scatter_class_table.json` (append-only). The table hash `RingScatterClassTable.Hash` covers every column of every row plus `RingGroundSurfaceModel`'s constants (A6).

**Placement per class** (pure, per stage job):

```
for each class cell (cx, cz) whose square overlaps the tile (or the 60 m block):
  for k in 0 .. CandidatesPerCell - 1:
    salt = 101 + 37 * Id + 13 * k
    around = (cx + .06 + .88 * Hash01(cx, cz, seed, salt)) * CellM      -> rounded to cm
    across = (cz + .06 + .88 * Hash01(cx, cz, seed, salt + 1)) * CellM  -> rounded to cm
    skip unless OwnerTile(around, across) == this tile (A8), and 4 m inside the rims
    f = grid.Sample(around, across); skip if any of its four nodes has Water or SiteBlocked
    p = min(1, Density(f) * CellM^2 / 10,000 / CandidatesPerCell)
    skip if Hash01(cx, cz, seed, salt + 2) >= p
    id = StableScatterId(Id, cx, cz, k, seed); size, yaw, tilt, mesh from Hash01(.., salt + 3 .. salt + 7)
    skip unless WinsScatterSpacing(...)    // A8: same class, Avoid rows, trees
    surface = PresentationSurfaceHeight(edits, sampler, around, across)   // double coordinates, lattice memo
    up = Alignment(f gradient); add the record
```

For stage O the coarse pre-test is the same density with every response evaluated at its upper bound over the candidate's coarse cell (coarse slope plus 14 degrees); a candidate whose bound is 0 is rejected without fine nodes.

### A6. Records and cache

**Record** (`scripts/Vegetation/Scatter/ScatterRecord.cs`), 44 bytes on disk:

```csharp
internal readonly record struct ScatterRecord(
    ulong Id,              // StableScatterId (A8); the rank is derived from it like TreeRankFromId
    long AroundCm,         // canonical around, centimetres (0 .. 3,141,593,599)
    int AcrossCm,          // canonical across, centimetres
    float SurfaceHeight,   // presentation height at the root (m), exact through the lattice memo
    ushort ClassId,
    ushort Mesh,           // index inside the class's MeshFirst .. MeshFirst + MeshCount - 1
    float Size,            // m: boulder height, outcrop length, cluster footprint
    float Yaw,             // radians
    uint UpOct,            // the record's up vector, octahedral, 2 x snorm16, in the local ring frame (around, up, across)
    uint BaseTint);        // RGB8 of the ground colour at the root (B4), from RingTerrainVisuals.ResolveMacroTextureColor (RingTerrainVisuals.cs:81) at the coarse sample; top byte 0
```

Positions are integer centimetres, so they are deterministic and survive a round trip unchanged; trees use whole metres, which would put boulder fields on a visible 1 m grid.

**Per-tile scatter state**, in the decoration layer partial `RingBiomeDecorationLayer.Scatter.cs`, in its own dictionary keyed by `VegetationTileKey` (not a field of `VegetationTile`, which stays as it is):

```
ScatterTileState { int BuiltRows; ushort CobbleBlockMask; List<ScatterRecord> Records /* build order, then id */;
                   RingHabitatFieldGrid? FineWindow; ulong EditVersion; long Bytes; }
```

**Stages.** `BuiltRows` is the number of rows built, in build order (A5). A tile is due row r when its centre is within `ReachM(r) x ScatterDistanceScale + margin` of the predicted focus (margin 300 m, the tree prediction look-ahead applies). Cobble blocks are due within 50 m x scale + 50 m, only while the camera is less than 60 m above the ground.
- The scatter streamer (`RingBiomeDecorationLayer.ScatterJobs.cs`) queues jobs for resident tree tiles by distance, at most `MaximumScatterJobsInFlight` = 2 at once.
- A job builds the missing rows or blocks on a worker: it creates its sampler with `template.CreateIndependentCopy()` on the worker (the template is a copy the streamer holds; T5-4 confirms the call is safe from several workers and escalates if not), enables the lattice memo, and reads the tile's tree records (immutable).
- The main thread installs the result if the tile and edit version still match.
- When a tile falls out of a stage by more than 300 m, its rows and window beyond that stage are dropped. Rebuilding gives the same records, because rows and blocks are pure functions (A8).

**Memory.** Outcrop records for about 430 tiles (a few each), boulders for about 45 tiles (up to about 400 each), cobbles for at most about 12 blocks (up to about 180 each), plus the fine windows: under 1 MB typical, about 5 MB at worst. This is counted as `ScatterMemoryBytes`, not in `MaximumVegetationCacheMemoryMiB`, so tree eviction does not change.

**File.**
- Path: `lod_cache/scatter/<identity>/r_<shardAround>_<shardAcross>/s_<around>_<across>.bin`, with the same shards (64 tiles) and budget root as the tree files, so `RingGeneratedLodCacheBudget` covers it.
- Header, 48 bytes: magic `0x31544353` (`SCT1`), `ScatterCacheVersion` (1), key around, key across, `ScatterAlgorithmVersion`, the class table hash, `BuiltRows`, `CobbleBlockMask`, record count (at most 65,536). Then the records, then an FNV-1a 64 trailer over header and records.
- Written to a temporary file and renamed, through `RingGeneratedLodCacheBudget.CanWrite`, like `TryWriteVegetationTile`.
- A load fails on a wrong magic, version, key or trailer, or out-of-range values (class id, mesh, a non-finite float). A file with fewer rows or blocks than due is loaded and topped up by a job, then rewritten.

**Identity**: SHA-256 of `sv{ScatterCacheVersion}|a{ScatterAlgorithmVersion}|c{ClassTable.Hash:X16}|m{RockCatalog.CatalogVersion}|g{generator}|seed{seed}|h{TerrainDefinitionHash:X16}|t{tree settings hash:X16}` plus the `|es1|cm1|wl1` site flags, as for trees. The tree settings hash is in it because trunk avoidance reads tree placement. The class table hash covers `RingGroundSurfaceModel`'s constants, because T4-5 already changed one (the slope bias) once.

**Open features never touch the disk.** Scatter files are read and written only when every scatter feature in the world's mask is frozen (`FrozenIn > 0`). Review worlds always regenerate, so an open-feature code change can never load a stale tile, even if someone forgets to bump `ScatterAlgorithmVersion`. `--disable-lod-disk-cache` is honoured. A test hook (`ScatterDiskCacheForTests`) turns the cache on for `scatter_tile_determinism_test`. After a freeze, `ScatterAlgorithmVersion` belongs to that generation and never changes (a look change is a new feature, plan 2.2).

**Edits** follow the tree rules exactly: tiles near edited columns skip the scatter disk cache (`TileTouchesEditedColumns`); dirty columns drop the scatter state of the 3 x 3 tiles around them (the `InvalidateVegetationTiles` hook, A7); a job result whose edit version is stale is discarded.

**Feature off:** no streamer, no jobs, no files, no state (`_scatter` is null).

**Proof:** `scatter_tile_determinism_test`: a written tile reloads byte-identically; a corrupt header, trailer or record is rejected; a bumped `ScatterAlgorithmVersion` changes the path; an open feature writes no file; the feature off writes nothing; a stale edit version is discarded.

### A7. Generation 1 path

- **Not edited:** `BuildVegetationTile`, `StartVegetationTileBuilds`, `CachedTree`, `VegetationTile`, the tree file format, `ResolveVegetationDiskCachePath` and `VegetationCacheSettingsHash`.
- **The only edits in `RingBiomeDecorationLayer.TreeStreaming.cs`** are three one-line hooks, each `_scatter?.On…(key)`:
  - after `_vegetationTiles[key] = result.Tile;` (588): `OnTreeTileResident`;
  - in `EvictVegetationCache` (782): `OnTreeTileEvicted`;
  - in `InvalidateVegetationTiles` (708 onward): `OnTreeTilesInvalidated`.
  `_scatter` is created once at world load only when the sampler's mask has bit 4; in every other world the hooks are null checks.
- Scatter jobs read tree records and never write them. Trees in a `scatter.rocks` world are therefore identical to generation 1; only their cache directory differs, because the review list is in the definition hash (P4).
- **The fingerprint tree rows cannot move:** they hash `BuildImportedForestReviewTile`, which calls `BuildVegetationTile`, which is not edited and has no input from the scatter feature. The scatter slot stays `none` for every generation without `scatter.rocks` (A9).
- The order said "called from the same worker task after the trees". Separate jobs after the tree tile is resident keep that order (trees first, never on the main thread) without touching the generation 1 task. The one cost is that a scatter job cannot reuse the tree build's lattice memo; that is mitigation 1 in A3, held back because it would edit the generation 1 function.
- **Proof:** `world_generation_fingerprint_test`; `tree_streaming_contract_test`; `scatter_tile_determinism_test` adds: the tree records of the 14 test tiles are identical in a generation 1 world and a scatter review world of the same seed.

### A8. Determinism and seams

- **Candidates** come from the class cell grid of A5. Cell sizes divide 128 m and so the circumference: the around cell index wraps exactly, cx mod (31,415,936 / CellM). Positions are centimetre integers.
- **Ids:** `StableScatterId(classId, cx, cz, k, seed)` is `StableTreeId`'s mixer (1585-1596) over cx, cz and seed, with the candidate word replaced by `classId x 0x10000 + k`, so rock ids cannot collide with tree ids of the same cell.
- **Ownership:** a record belongs to the tile whose production extent contains its position: tile around = floor(floor(aroundM / aroundCellMeters) / 8), with `aroundCellMeters` from `CaptureVegetationLayout` in double, the same arithmetic as `AroundTileForCanonical` (1600-1606); tile across = floor(acrossM / 240). Every candidate has exactly one owner, including at the ring seam and in the short last tile. A cobble block is a 60 m square of the tile's own extent.
- **Spacing** is Matern II, as `WinsForestSpacingCompetition`:
  - Same class: a candidate loses to any *viable* peer (it passes the density test) within the required distance with a smaller id. Peers are regenerated from canonical cells within ceil(max distance / CellM) cells, memoised per job.
  - Earlier classes (`Avoid`): a candidate is rejected within the clearance of any *accepted* record of an earlier class. In the tile and the halo those come from the tile's state, or are recomputed by the earlier class's own pure accept function (memoised), so a neighbouring tile that is not resident or not yet at that stage changes nothing.
  - Trees: in-tile trees come from the tile's records. For candidates within clearance of a tile edge, halo trees are recomputed with `EvaluateSpacingCandidate` and `WinsForestSpacingCompetition` (both pure, P1) on the job's own memo.
  - Ties: by id; equal ids (probability about 2^-64) by (classId, cx, cz, k).
- **Worker independence:** each job owns its sampler copy, lattice memo and node memos, and shares no mutable state. Records are sorted (build order, id) before install.
- **Stage and block independence:** a row's records are the same whether stages run together or apart, and a cobble block's records do not depend on whether its neighbours were built first.
- **Proof:** `scatter_tile_determinism_test`: identical records across two runs, two build orders (tile and stage order reversed, cobble blocks shuffled), 1 against 4 workers, and the tiles on both sides of the ring seam and of an across seam; each seam record is owned once. The order's "can it fail" step (tie by candidate index instead of id) must fail the build-order check.

### A9. Fingerprint

- From the generation that freezes `scatter.rocks` (N), `row["scatter"]` holds one entry per tile `{pose, tile_around, tile_across, count, hash}`, plus `scatter_params`: the class table hash, `ScatterAlgorithmVersion`, the rock catalog manifest's artifact SHA-256, and the layout used.
- **Tiles:** the eight T0 tile poses (P10) plus `steep_eye` and `scree_eye` from `tools/fixtures/landscape_poses_dressing_s1337.json`. Two dressing poses rather than one, because the T0 poses are lowland and forest (few rocks), and cobbles exist only on scree.

The tree records used for trunk avoidance are built with the same layout.
- **Hash** per record, in (build order, id) order: Id, AroundCm, AcrossCm, the SurfaceHeight bits, ClassId, Mesh, the Size and Yaw bits, UpOct, BaseTint.
- Generations without `scatter.rocks` keep `{count 0, hash none}`; the generation-keyed computer asks the ledger. T6's rows join the same slot from their own generation on.

## T5 design B: rock meshes, render path, draws, colliders, far speckle and settings

### Summary B

- **Meshes.** 30 procedural rocks (16 boulders, 8 outcrops, 6 cobble clusters) x 3 LODs from a seeded C# tool, about 0.8 MB. All variants of a group share one topology per LOD, so each (group, LOD) is one indexed carrier mesh, with per-variant positions and normals in data textures: the impostor's vertex-fetch technique (P8), without its padding waste.

The 24 gate is met without relaxing it, so user decision 10 is answered.
- **Shader.** `ring_scatter_rock.gdshader` is a follower of the terrain lighting contract, like the grass cards: per-fragment haze, cloud and macro shadow, height shadow, wetness and storm light are the terrain's own functions. Rock texture and strata come from T4, with a CPU-exact root height for the strata.
- **Shadows:** boulders from 2 m and every outcrop, from fixed low LODs, within the sun's near map.
- **Colliders:** a pool within 110 m, as for trunks.
- **Far:** a boulder speckle in the ground proxy variant from rock exposure and scree, hashed on canonical cells.
- **Settings:** "Rock density" (a rank prefix) and "Scatter distance scale" in the Vegetation tab; `PresetScatter`.
- **Cost:** about 0.04-0.08 ms of GPU at High at a rocky pose on the 4070 (est.), about 0.1-0.2 ms at Medium on the A1000 (est.), against the 0.5 ms gate.

### B1. Mesh set

**Tool.** `tools/PrepareRockAssets.cs` with the wrapper `tools/prepare_rock_assets.ps1`, styled after `tools/prepare_tree_assets.ps1` (headless; `-Check` regenerates and compares with the manifest).
- Determinism: integer-hash value noise (no FastNoiseLite), double arithmetic, and vertex positions quantised to 1/4096 of the unit size before normals and hashes are computed. The per-mesh hashes are over the quantised arrays, so they are engine-independent; the artifact SHA-256 is engine-dependent, as in the tree manifest (`engine_identity`).
- Meshes are built at unit size; the instance scale is the record's `Size`.

**Topology and budgets** (all variants of a group share the LOD's topology):

| group | variants | LOD0 tris / verts | LOD1 | LOD2 | base mesh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| boulders | 16 | 1,280 / 642 | 320 / 162 | 80 / 42 | icosphere, subdivision 3 / 2 / 1 |
| cobble clusters | 6 | 480 / 252 | 120 / 72 | 48 / 36 | six stones, each an icosphere 1 / 0 / octahedron |
| outcrops | 8 | about 1,700 / 900 | about 440 / 250 | about 190 / 110 | 7 stacked beds, rings of 48 / 24 / 12 vertices |

- **Boulders.** A direction function: an ellipsoid (axes 1 : 0.6-0.9 : 0.45-0.75 by hash), times (1 + three octaves of noise at amplitude 0.12), then 3-6 planar fracture cuts (p minus max(0, p.n - c) n), so they read as broken rock rather than pebbles. A base plane sits at the embed depth. LOD1 and LOD2 sample the same function at their vertices, which are a subset of LOD0's, so their vertices lie on LOD0's surface.
- **Cobble clusters.** Six stones of 0.15-0.5 m in a footprint of unit size, lowest stone embedded 30 %. A cluster that wants fewer stones collapses the spare ones to a point, so the topology stays shared.
- **Outcrops.** Seven beds, each a ring with its own inset or outset noise and a flat top, so the ledges are horizontal (the bedding). The ring rotates with yaw only, never tilts (`BeddingHorizontal`), so the shader's strata bands run parallel to the ledges. The block extends below its origin to -0.75 x length, so it stays rooted on slopes up to 55 degrees: on a slope the downhill face shows and the uphill side is buried.
- **Base embed** per class, from A5's rows. Each mesh stores, per vertex, its height above the base plane as a fraction (data texture `w`) for the base blend (B4).
- **Size.** Positions as RGBA32F (xyz, w = base height) and normals as RGBA16F, about 27,000 vertices in all: about 0.65 MB, plus the carrier index buffers (about 0.1 MB) and the collision data (boulder hulls are the LOD2 vertices, outcrop collision is the LOD1 triangles). About 0.8 MB against the 8 MB gate.
- **Prepared resource:** `resources/rocks/prepared/rock_catalog.res` with `resources/rocks/prepared/rock_catalog.manifest.json` (schema version, generator version, seed, engine identity, implementation identity, per-mesh content hashes, artifact SHA-256, sizes), like `resources/trees/prepared/lolipop_catalog.manifest.json`. Loader: `scripts/Vegetation/Scatter/RockCatalog.cs` (`RockCatalog.Load()`, `CatalogVersion`, per group: carriers, data textures, collision data, unit AABBs).
- **Proof:** `scatter_rock_mesh_validation_test`: counts 16 / 8 / 6 x 3; one topology per (group, LOD); the budgets above, within 10 %, and the LOD ratios; closed meshes for boulders and every cluster stone, with no degenerate triangles except collapsed spare stones; the base at the designed embed; boulder LOD1 and LOD2 vertices on LOD0's surface within 1e-4; regeneration matches the manifest hashes; total under 8 MB.

### B2. Registration

**Decision: a separate compute instance.**
1. In the tree instance every non-grass batch claims one of the five tree slots (P6), and the tree caps bind at the user's poses (P7). Rocks in a tree slot would take budget from trees there and change tree output. Widening `used[5]` would change the shader and the parameter layout.
2. Rocks need their own draws in any case (B3), so sharing the instance saves no draws.
3. Rocks do not use the vegetation haze or shadow atlases at all: B4 shades them like terrain, per fragment. With `HazeChannel = None` there is no new channel and no compositor change, and the escalation condition about a new haze channel does not arise.

**The layer.** `scripts/Vegetation/Scatter/RingScatterLayer.cs` (`Node3D` named `ScatterLayer`), a child of the game root next to the decoration layer, created at world load only when the mask has bit 4. It is not under the decoration layer, because the harness counts everything there as trees (C4).
- It owns `new RingGpuVegetationCompute { HazeChannel = None, DiagnosticCategory = RingPerformanceGpu.ScatterCulling }`. `DiagnosticCategory` is a new nullable property; null keeps today's choice (1339-1341, 1857), so tree markers stay clean. It is the only change in `RingGpuVegetationCompute.cs`.
- **Sources**, one per render group (16-float rows, no expansion):
  - the transform in the anchor frame (below);
  - `INSTANCE_CUSTOM.x` = mesh index + 32 x the base tint as RGB 6:6:6 (an exact integer under 2^24);
  - `INSTANCE_CUSTOM.z` = the root's canonical height in metres (B4);
  - metadata = (rank, 0, around offset, across offset).
  Each group with casters has a second source, the caster source: boulders of at least 2 m, and all outcrops.
- **Anchor.** Rows are built on a worker in double, relative to an anchor: the camera's canonical position snapped to 512 m. They are rebuilt when the scatter tile set changes or the camera is more than 512 m from the anchor (every 2 s at 240 m/s). The new source replaces the old one through a fresh registration, as the merged tree groups are replaced. Offsets stay under about 3 km, so float32 holds them to 0.25 mm, and the curvature is exact because each row comes from the canonical mapping in double. `LocalToWorld` is the anchor's frame from the geometry snapshot each frame, so floating-origin rebases need nothing more.
- **Batches.** Per (group, LOD), a `BatchRegistration`:
  - identity `ScatterRock_<group>_L<n>`;
  - the full-list `MultiMesh` is hidden (layers 0); the camera draws the phase 6 `CameraTarget`;
  - the group's carrier mesh; `Grass` false, `TreeCustomData` true, `TreeDitherData` true;
  - per group, one caster batch `ScatterRock_<group>_Shadow`: the caster source, the caster LOD mesh (B6), a ShadowsOnly full-list renderer, no camera list, no dither.
- **Frame.** One `FrameRequest` per frame with `SkipUnchanged`:
  - `LodBudgets` = [boulder cap, outcrop cap, cobble cap, caster cap, 0] x the preset's cap scale (B9). Batches are dispatched near LOD first, so when a cap binds the far rows drop first;
  - per batch: `LodPass` = its group's slot, the distance band and fades of B5 x the distance scale, `TreeRankGate` = Rock density;
  - the camera planes through `RequestCameraCull`, the same padded planes the tree layer builds (`Gpu.cs` 1826-1851, moved to a shared helper); no shadow cull state, since casters are not mirrors.
- **Empty groups.** A group with no rows in its source hides its renderers, so plains poses issue only the boulder draws.

**Tree output unchanged.** A separate instance; no edit to the tree layer, the compute shader or the parameter layout; in worlds without the feature the layer does not exist.
- **Proof:** `gpu_tree_integration_test`, `gpu_vegetation_parameter_layout_test`, `vegetation_haze_gpu_test`, `vegetation_shadow_gpu_test` and `tree_frustum_identity_gpu_test` pass unchanged; `scatter_haze_shadow_gpu_test` checks that no scatter batch is registered with the feature off.

### B4. Shader

`materials/ring_scatter_rock.gdshader`, one shader for the three groups. Material uniforms, set per group and never a menu switch: `rock_group` (0 boulder, 1 outcrop, 2 cobble) and the group's data textures.
- **Includes:** `ring_terrain_lighting.gdshaderinc` (which brings atmosphere, cloud shadow, macro shadow and shadow compose), `ring_ground_surface.gdshaderinc` (T4: rock tint, strata, detail normal), `ring_wetness.gdshaderinc`.
- **Parameters:** registered exactly like the grass-card material (P8b): `RingTerrain3DSurface.RegisterSurfaceFollower(material, names)` with the lighting, atmosphere, `biome_texture_scale` and `biome_alpine_albedo` names, plus `RingCloudShadowProjector.Register` and `RingTerrainMacroShadowProjector.Register`. A T8 rock texture that replaces the alpine binding therefore reaches the rocks with no rock change.

**`vertex()`:**
- variant = int(INSTANCE_CUSTOM.x) & 31; base tint = the upper bits; VERTEX, NORMAL and the base weight from `texelFetch(rock_vertex_data / rock_normal_data, ivec2(VERTEX_ID, variant), 0)`;
- the dither words from INSTANCE_CUSTOM.y and .w, the tree protocol (`imported_tree.gdshader:63-75`, w within 0.25 of 2);
- **absolute height for the strata:** rock_height = INSTANCE_CUSTOM.z + dot(world_vertex - root_world, root_up), with root_up the ring up at the root (`-normalize(radial)`, as the tree shader computes it). The root height comes exact from the CPU (float32 resolves about 0.5 mm under 8 km), and the offset is at most 40 m in a precise local direction; the curvature term over 40 m is under 0.2 mm and is dropped. This avoids T4 S3's trap: |world - ring_center| has only 0.5 m resolution in float32.

**`fragment()`:**
- `apply_tree_dither`;
- triplanar `biome_alpine_albedo` at `world_position x biome_texture_scale`, with the proxy's projections, so rock and terrain rock share scale and phase; x `GROUND_ROCK_TINT`;
- outcrops: x `ring_ground_strata_factor(rock_height, fwidth(rock_height))`, with `fwidth` taken in uniform control flow (T4 D3), so their bands sit at the same absolute heights as the cliff's;
- boulders: x (1 + contrast x `Tone(bed)`) for one bed hashed from the root, a flat bed tone with no bands, because a fallen block's bands would not be level;
- cobbles: rock tint only;
- base blend: mix(rock, base tint, 0.7 (1 - smoothstep(0, 0.15, base weight))) x mix(0.85, 1, smoothstep(0, 0.15, base weight)), so the base blends to the ground's macro colour and darkens slightly (plan 5);
- T4 D6's detail normal within 40-60 m;
- wetness: `ring_wet_sample` and `ring_wet_apply` with the rock darkening factor 0.5 (T4 D9), with gloss and no puddles;
- `ring_terrain_night_tint`, then `ring_terrain_apply_atmosphere` per fragment, the same call Terrain3D makes, so haze and storm path darkening are the terrain's at the same world position.

**`light()`:** the terrain-lit `light()` of the grass-card material (`ring_grass_cards.gdshader:213`, on `ring_terrain_lighting.gdshaderinc`): sun visibility with shadow map, cloud and macro shadow, the height-shadow clipmap through shadow compose, night factor and storm light. No per-class switch.
- **Proof:** `scatter_haze_shadow_gpu_test` (the order's checks: haze within the `vegetation_haze_gpu_test` tolerance of the adjacent terrain at 50 m, 500 m and 2 km; darkening in cloud and sun shadow; wetness), plus an outcrop strata check: the band index on an outcrop face equals the terrain's at matched heights, and a half-band offset must fail.

### B6. Shadows

- Casters: boulders of at least 2 m and every outcrop (plan 8), each group through its caster batch (B2). Cobbles and smaller boulders never cast.
- Caster meshes: boulders use LOD2 (80 triangles), outcrops LOD1 (about 440). The lower LODs' vertices lie on LOD0's surface (B1), so on these mostly convex shapes the caster stays inside the drawn rock and does not cause self-shadow acne. Caster shadows also cannot pop at the visible LOD switches.
- Distance band: 0 to the sun's near-map distance (300 m at Low and Medium, 400 at High and Very High, 500 at Ultra; P14).
- **The tree cap:** the plan says "within the existing 128-batch cap". Rock casters are 2 renderers in their own layer and are *not* counted against `MaximumTreeSunShadowBatches`: counting them there would let rocks displace tree shadow batches when that cap binds, which would change tree output. This is a deliberate departure from the plan's wording.
- Per-instance vegetation shadow atlases (S7b) are not used; rocks receive shadows per fragment like terrain.
- **Proof:** `scatter_haze_shadow_gpu_test`: a 3 m boulder casts into the sun map (a ground probe behind it darkens) and a 1.5 m one does not; the T5-10 shadow draw count.

### B7. Colliders

`RingScatterLayer.Colliders.cs`, a pool like the trunks' (P9):
- radius `ScatterCollisionRadiusMeters` = 110 (export, the trunk value) and cap `MaximumScatterColliders` = 192, outcrops first, then boulders by distance;
- shapes: boulders from 0.8 m get a `ConvexPolygonShape3D` from the LOD2 vertices scaled by size (the scaled points are built per body and cached per mesh and centimetre size; GodotPhysics3D does not like scaled shapes); outcrops get a `ConcavePolygonShape3D` from the LOD1 triangles scaled by size, so their ledges can be climbed; cobbles get none;
- tile-driven: re-evaluated when the scatter record epoch changes or the focus moves 4 m (the trunks' quantisation), with the trunks' altitude reach logic and a 50 m height allowance (outcrops stand up to about 30 m);
- at most 8 bodies installed per frame; a body is keyed by record id and only its pose is rewritten when it changes, like `ImportedForestColliderPose`; collision layer and mask as for trunks;
- feature off: no pool.
- **Proof:** `scatter_collision_test` (the order's checks: a body per boulder within the radius up to the cap; a ray hits; release when moving away; none with the feature off); escalation at 0.05 ms per frame on the main thread while walking.

### B8. Far speckle

- **Where:** `materials/ring_terrain_proxy_ground.gdshader` only, inside `#ifdef RING_GROUND_SURFACE`, after rock and scree are computed (about lines 271-273), behind `ring_world_has(4)`. The shipped proxy shader is frozen generation 1 code and is never touched (T4 D7). If "ground" is frozen before "rocks" (Q2), the block goes into a new variant file selected by bit 4 instead of the frozen ground variant, following D7's rule.
- **Weight:** w = max(scree, 0.6 x rock) x RockDensity x smoothstep(540 k, 600 k, d), with k the distance scale: the boulder and cobble rows' main terms, starting where boulder meshes end. It needs no new vertex data.
- **Pattern:** canonical 4 m cells. A cell is a boulder with probability 0.25 w (integer PCG of the cell indices and the seed, like the T4 strata tone), and a boulder cell is darkened by 0.25: lit tops and shadowed sides at 1-3 px average to darker mottling. Both terms are exact means, so the mean darkening is 0.0625 w (at most about 6 %): a boulder field is a little darker than bare scree, as it is.
- **Footprint filter**, as T4 D3: with F = the footprint in cells (`fwidth` of the cell coordinate), the cell term's contrast fades by 1 - smoothstep(0.35, 0.9, F), and the mean term carries on, so the field converges to its mean tone as cells shrink below a pixel. The pattern has no time input, so it cannot shimmer.
- **Coordinates and precision.**
  - A new instance uniform `scatter_sector_origin` (vec2: the sector origin's canonical around modulo 65,536 m and across), appended after every existing instance uniform, set by `RingTerrainProxySector` only in scatter worlds. The ocean and shadow shaders never read it.
  - Per vertex, the model-space offsets along around (`ground_around`, already computed) and along the ring axis, passed as a varying.
  - Model space resolves about 1 mm and the origin is an exact integer under 2^16, so a cell boundary is placed to about 8 mm. The pattern repeats every 65.5 km and has one invisible discontinuity at the ring seam.
  - Floating-origin rebases cannot move it, because nothing uses world position.
- **A global** `ringworld_scatter_look` (vec4: x distance scale, y rock density) in `project.godot` `[shader_globals]`, set by the settings apply (B9).
- **Cost:** about 15 ALU on rock and scree pixels between 540 m and about 3 km, at most about 0.01 ms; zero in any world without the bit.
- **Proof:** `terrain_rock_material_gpu_test` gains a feature-off byte-identity check (the T4 pre-capture) for a world with ground and without rocks; the T5-9 fly-out from 300 m to 5 km over `breaks_300m`, with `sheet` and `flicker`.
