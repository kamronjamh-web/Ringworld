### Near terrain shaders

- S1. `materials/ring_terrain_surface_albedo.gdshaderinc` is unchanged since 11-10 (38 lines): sampler declarations at 10-16, grass weight 18-20, grass tint 23-25, biome mix 29-38. The **grass-cards material includes it too** (header 1-6), so anything added here also compiles into `materials/ring_grass_cards.gdshader`. The card material's follower list repeats the biome names (`scripts/Voxel/LOD/RingGrassCardMaterialConfig.cs:71-72`).
- S2. Terrain3D surface, `materials/terrain3d_ring_surface.gdshader` (413 lines):
  - The normal comes from 12 height fetches at a 4 m baseline (central differences of 2 m cells, 186-224). `slope = 1 - planar_normal.y` is at 242.
  - The dirt blend is `ground_facing = smoothstep(.45, .78, planar_normal.y)` (255): dirt from 38.7 degrees, full dirt at 63.3 degrees. Dirt is sampled only when `ground_facing < 1` (256). **All five biome maps, including `biome_alpine_albedo`, are always sampled** (257-262). The mix is at 263, and `ground_facing` is recomputed at 265 for the wet and grass-coverage terms.
  - Texture position (251-254): anchor-relative around and across, absolute height `surface_height + ring_anchor_height`, scaled by `biome_texture_scale` 0.20, so the textures repeat every 5 m.
  - Wet pass (273-299): rock darkening is `mix(0.5, 1.0, ground_facing)` (275); the cavity takes four `SKIP_PASS` height taps at plus or minus 4 cells, 8 m (283-286).
  - Grass coverage uses `ground_facing` as its `upward` input (300-304). The result goes through `ring_terrain_albedo_scaled` (305).
  - NT-1 (`1503700`): `vertex()` writes NaN for every vertex farther than handoff + 3 cells + 32 m (160-163), and Terrain3D builds a single clipmap level, about 80,000 triangles per camera pass.
  - `ring_anchor_height` is the frame's anchor height (`scripts/Terrain/Surface/RingTerrain3DSurface.cs:421`). The window stores `h - anchor` as float32. `ring_texture_anchor_*_meters` are the anchor **modulo 1000** (338-339), so they cannot give the phase of a 32 m lattice.
- S3. Proxy sectors, `materials/ring_terrain_proxy.gdshader` (347 lines):
  - Biome samplers are declared inline (34-39), not through S1.
  - `ground_facing` compares the interpolated vertex normal with the radial up (181). The texture position is `world_position * biome_texture_scale` (183).
  - Samples are branched by weight: dirt only if `ground_facing < 1` (188-189), **alpine only if `packed.a > 0` (207-208)**, and dirt alone in the else branch (210-212). Then come grass coverage (214-219), the T2R-12b river line tint (220), the albedo scale (221, skipped for `structural_surface`) and wetness (226-236; rock factor at 231).
  - `world_position` is floating-origin world space and `ring_center` is about 5,000 km away, so `length(world_position - ring_center)` has about 0.5 m resolution in float32 (the `shader-debug` skill covers ring-scale precision).
  - **Each sector's model origin is at canonical height 0.** `ResolveSectorLocalOrigin` places the node at `CanonicalToWorld(centre around, 0, centre across)` (`scripts/Voxel/LOD/RingTerrainProxySector.cs:1534-1538`), and `BuildRawSector` builds vertices relative to that point (4013-4021; `scripts/Voxel/Phase2/RingLodGeometrySnapshot.cs:25-88`). Model-space `VERTEX` therefore carries the absolute height along the sector's up axis. The ring axis is world Y (the shader's `radial.y = 0.0`).
  - Slot path (`RingTerrainProxySector.VertexTable.cs` `PackVertexTableSlot`, 378-417): `attr.z` of a lattice texel uses only bit 0 (morph flag) and bit 1 (skirt flag); **bits 16-31 are free on lattice texels** (skirt texels use them for the UV). `attr.w` carries the T2R-12c river line coverage.
  - Mesh path: `CUSTOM0` is `RgbFloat` (the parent chord), present only on runtime-chord sectors (`RingTerrainProxySector.cs:6194-6198`; `VertexTable.cs:960-961`).
  - Who builds what: 2 and 4 m levels on the CPU (slot-installed), 8, 16 and 32 m on the GPU (`materials/terrain_gpu/ring_proxy_sector_k2.glsl`, slot-installed), 64 m and coarser on the CPU (mesh-installed). River sectors are built on the CPU at any level (T2R-11). Vertex biome weights are the nearest 16 m column centre (`ResolveBiomeTextureWeight`, 4742-4763; GPU `PASS_B`).
  - High preset (`resources/voxel/ring_lod_high.tres`), out ranges: Lod1 2 m 320-450 m, Lod2 4 m 0.8-1.1 km, Lod3 8 m 1.8-2.4 km, Lod4 16 m 4-5.5 km, Lod5 32 m 9-12 km, Lod6 64 m 20-27 km, Lod7 128 m 40-55 km. Sector size is 64 cells.
  - Slot-installed sectors carry their whole lattice in `proxy_vertex_table_pos` (`materials/ring_terrain_proxy_vertex_fetch.gdshaderinc:16-24`, `proxy_vertex_table_apply` 46-88); mesh-installed sectors do not.
- S4. Texture bindings: `RingTerrain3DSurface.cs:343-348`, `RingTerrainProxySector.cs:6748-6752` (working tree), `RingStandingGrassLayer.CardStyle.cs:136-140`. The legacy voxel and smooth-terrain paths (`VoxelChunkGridManager.cs:3653`, `SmoothTerrainGrid.cs:2631`) are out of scope.

### Relief that the surface has to work with (T0 spec 8.1)

- R1. Slopes above 39 degrees (today's dirt threshold) are rare outside mountains and breaks. The 2D 4 m slope p95 is 7.7 degrees on plains, 21.9 rolling, 29.2 hill country, 38.8 breaks and 42.3 mountain; mountain is 33.1 % of the lattice. Rock will therefore appear mainly in mountains and breaks, and 28-38 degree ground mostly in hill country.
- R2. The 2-30 m decade is 0.04-0.24 m RMS on lowland, so **curvature at 8 m is centimetre noise until T7**. The 30-300 m decade is 0.7-3.9 m RMS (plains to hill country). Colour variation must be driven by curvature at 64 m or coarser to show anything before T7.
- R3. Slopes shrink as the baseline grows. Slope p99 from a 2 m to a 32 m baseline: mountain 53.8 to 51.0 degrees, breaks 50.9 to 42.1; p90: mountain 24.7 to 23.4, breaks 23.2 to 21.7. Coarse proxy normals therefore show less rock unless the rule compensates (D2).

### Cost, re-checked against T0 8.6 and perf pass 0.15

- C1. T0 (4070, 1080p, Medium, non-authoritative): near terrain 2.3-4.2 ms at every pose, 55-60 % of root GPU on open ground; noise floor 0.00-0.03 ms. Perf pass 0.15 (4070, slot 3, **High**, 1600 x 900, the preset the user now plays):
  - frame GPU 9.2-10.3 ms at the save, plains and forest after NT-1, 6.8 ms at the slope pose; the user's target is 11.1 ms (90 fps) at 1920 x 1080;
  - Terrain3D costs 0.19-0.43 ms after NT-1, proxies 1.33-2.0 ms; both are geometry-bound;
  - fragment cost still matters where terrain fills the screen: a flat Terrain3D fragment saves 0.49-0.74 ms on the plains and the slope;
  - biome texture work costs 0.00-0.20 ms on Terrain3D and 0.00-0.05 ms on the proxies. The Terrain3D fragment does 12 region lookups and 12 height fetches per pixel.
  So T4's fragment additions must be branch-gated to the pixels that need them, and the cheapest data path is per vertex.
- C2. The plan gate (0.4 ms on the A1000 at Medium, 0 new draws) stands. Rock reuses `biome_alpine_albedo`, which Terrain3D already samples for every fragment. The added samples are the detail normal (near only) and the proxy's alpine taps on steep pixels. **Re-scoped gate:** measure at every pose, not only the hill pose. The T4 budget on the 4070 is in D10.
- C3. **How T4 measures cost.** Raster passes have no GPU markers. T4-2's same-boot A-B-A case `--feature-ab=ground.surface` (feature on, off, on) gives the delta. The A1000 numbers are authoritative, but **the A1000 baseline still does not exist** (no `.godot/audit_baseline/a1000/`; T0W-3). Until it does, report 4070 deltas as indicative and flag any 4070 delta above 0.1 ms at any pose to the primary. That threshold assumes the A1000 is at least four times slower on this pass, which is **unverified**.

### Summary

- **Rock** replaces dirt on steep ground: from 38 degrees, full at 46, on a 4 m slope baseline. Convex ground exposes rock up to 6 degrees earlier, hollows up to 6 later, and forest holds soil 4 degrees longer. Coarse proxy levels get a slope bias so they keep the same rock share.
- **Strata**: 40 m beds from the recipe's `breaks_step_m`, at absolute heights shared ring-wide, with a 5 m sub-band. Each bed has its own tone. An exact box filter of the band function over the pixel footprint fades the contrast to the mean as the footprint nears the band. The proxy gets a precise absolute height from its model space: sector origins are at canonical height 0. No format change.
- **Scree** on 26-40 degree ground that is not convex at 64 m: full in alpine, tundra and desert, 40 % in grass and forest.
- **Textures:** rock and scree both use the existing `biome_alpine_albedo`, which turns out to be a gravel texture. Rock is darker, cooler and banded; scree lighter. Dirt is no longer sampled on steep ground. **Provisional** (Q1).
- **Colour variation** follows one field, C64: 64 m convexity on a global 32 m lattice. Paler on convex ground, darker and greener in hollows: about plus or minus 5 % typical, 12 % at most. Terrain3D computes C64 in its vertex stage. The proxy builders store it per vertex in a free slot-table field and in `CUSTOM0.w`. Both tiers interpolate the same lattice the same way, so they match at the hand-off by construction. It fades out between 16 and 24 km. T5 reuses the field through the C# mirror.
- **Detail normal** on rock and scree within 60 m, Terrain3D only, from a normal map generated in the repo from the alpine texture (1.4 MiB VRAM).
- **Far:** rock tone from the far tile normal in `ring_terrain_global`. No strata or colour variation there, and no CPU colour or tile-cache change.
- **Identity:** everything sits behind bit 2 in a new terrain-only include. The shared include S1 and the grass cards are untouched. Generation 1 is guarded by T1f, pre-captured receivers and a sector-data golden.
- **Budget:** 0.10 ms GPU for the whole feature on the 4070 at 1080p High, at every pose (D10). Zero draws.

### D1. Textures

**Choice.** No new imports.
- Rock = `biome_alpine_albedo` x `GROUND_ROCK_TINT` x the strata factor (D3).
- Scree = `biome_alpine_albedo` x `GROUND_SCREE_TINT`, with no strata.
- Both tiers already bind the texture, and Terrain3D already samples it for every fragment.
- In feature worlds dirt is not used on steep ground: rock replaces dirt sample for sample (plan 5). Both shaders skip their dirt taps there (D7 rule 2a).

**Change from the WO's suggestion.** The WO suggested scree = the dirt map greyed toward the rock colour. But the "alpine rock" texture is itself loose gravel and slate chips (finding S5), so it is the better scree, and the dirt tap is saved. The cost is that rock and scree differ only by tint, strata and the detail normal.

**Detail normal: option (a).**
- Output: `materials/biomes/alpine_rock_detail_normal.png`, generated by a deterministic headless tool, `tools/GroundRockDetailNormal.cs` with wrapper `tools/ground_rock_detail_normal.gd`.
- Steps: linear luminance of `alpine_rock_albedo.png`; Lanczos resample to 1024 x 1024; high-pass (subtract a 32-texel wrap-around box blur); wrap-around Sobel at strength 2.0; xy into RG8 (z rebuilt in the shader).
- Import: normal map, VRAM compressed (BC5/RGTC), mipmaps. About 1 MiB plus mips, 1.4 MiB of VRAM; under 2 MB on disk. The plan allows 24 MB of new textures (plan 8).
- Provenance: the source's origin is unrecorded (S5). `THIRD_PARTY_ASSETS.md` records the output as a derivative of `alpine_rock_albedo.png`, with both SHA-256 hashes and the origin the user gives in Q1 (or "unrecorded").
- (b) A procedural normal is rejected: its bumps do not line up with the stones in the albedo, and it costs more ALU than three taps. (c) Dropping it is rejected: the plan adopts it, and near rock under a low sun reads flat without it.

**Swap path.** The design is texture-agnostic. Before the freeze, a swap (for example a CC0 rock face, Q1) means: the bindings at the S4 sites and the detail-normal global, a new `GROUND_ROCK_MEAN_LINEAR`, and possibly new tints. Nothing else changes. After the freeze it is a new ledger feature in a later step (for example `ground.rock_texture`): the new texture sits behind its own bit, and generation N keeps the old one.

**Guards.** T1f (feature off); `terrain_rock_material_gpu_test` (rock and scree weights); `ground_surface_model_test` (the mean-colour constant equals the texture mean within 0.01).

### D2. Rock exposure rule

```glsl
float ring_ground_slope_bias(float baseline_m) {
    return min(GROUND_SLOPE_BIAS_PER_OCTAVE_DEG * max(log2(baseline_m / GROUND_SLOPE_REF_BASELINE_M), 0.0), GROUND_SLOPE_BIAS_MAX_DEG);
}
float ring_ground_rock_exposure(float slope_deg, float baseline_m, float relief, float forest_weight) {
    float s = slope_deg + ring_ground_slope_bias(baseline_m)
            + GROUND_CONVEX_SHIFT_DEG * relief - GROUND_FOREST_SHIFT_DEG * forest_weight;
    float t0 = ringworld_ground_look.x;
    return smoothstep(t0, t0 + GROUND_ROCK_RAMP_DEG, s);
}
```

The C# mirror is `RingGroundSurfaceModel.RockExposure(double slopeDeg, double baselineM, double relief, double forestWeight, double rockStartDeg = DefaultRockStartDeg /* 38 */)`. `relief` is D5's value in (-1, 1); it is 0 until T4-4b. `forest_weight` is the forest biome weight.

**Defaults.** Rock starts at 38 degrees and is full at 46, on a 4 m baseline. On a convex crest (relief +1) it starts at 32; in a hollow (relief -1) at 44; in dense forest 4 degrees later.

**Inputs per tier:**
- Terrain3D: `planar_normal` (4 m baseline), relief from the D5 varying, forest = `packed.g`.
- Proxy: the interpolated vertex normal against `surface_up_world`; the baseline from a new level material uniform `ground_slope_baseline_meters` (default 4), which `RingTerrainProxySector` sets to 2 x `SampleSpacingMeters` when it creates the level material; relief from D5's vertex value; forest = `COLOR.g`.
- Far (`ring_terrain_global`): the surface tile normal (128 m baseline), relief 0, forest 0, and only where `surface_tile_enabled`. Without tiles the vertex normals are kilometre-scale and give no rock, which is correct at that scale.

**Reasons.**
- The start sits on today's dirt onset (38.7 degrees) and on R1 (breaks p95 38.8, mountain p95 42.3), so rock appears mainly in mountains and breaks.
- Plan 5 makes convexity the second input. Forest is the WO's biome input, kept to one physical effect (roots hold soil).
- The slope bias keeps the rock share of the coarse levels close to Lod1's (R3). T4-5 calibrates its one constant headlessly (20 % rule).

**Wetness.** In feature worlds the wet darkening uses `mix(0.5, 1.0, 1.0 - rock)` instead of `ground_facing` (D9), so the new rock darkens by 0.5 as rock does today.

**T5.** T5 places boulders and outcrops with `RockExposure` at the default start of 38 degrees, not the live menu value, so tile caches stay valid. The menu value is a surface-look tunable only.

**Tests.** `terrain_rock_material_gpu_test`: at least 0.9 at 50 degrees and 0 at 10, on both tiers; GLSL equals C# at 64 pairs within 1e-4. `ground_surface_model_test`: the shape checks, and T4-5's calibration.

### D3. Strata

**Band function and filter** (h = absolute canonical height in metres, P = `ringworld_ground_world.x`):

```glsl
// tone(n): integer hash (PCG) of int(n) and the seed, mapped to [-1, 1]
float ring_ground_band(float h, float fw, float P, uint seed) {   // fw = fwidth(h), taken in uniform control flow
    float u = h / P;
    float w = max(fw / P, 1e-5);                   // footprint in bands
    if (w >= 1.0) return 0.0;                      // band mean
    float n0 = floor(u - 0.5 * w);
    float f = clamp((u + 0.5 * w - (n0 + 1.0)) / w, 0.0, 1.0);
    float b = mix(tone(n0, seed), tone(n0 + 1.0, seed), f);   // exact box filter: a box under one band wide crosses at most one boundary
    return b * (1.0 - smoothstep(GROUND_STRATA_FADE_W.x, GROUND_STRATA_FADE_W.y, w));
}
float ring_ground_strata_factor(float h, float fw) {
    float P = ringworld_ground_world.x; uint seed = uint(ringworld_ground_world.y);
    float c = ringworld_ground_look.y;
    float bed = ring_ground_band(h, fw, P, seed);
    float sub = ring_ground_band(h, fw, P / GROUND_STRATA_SUB_DIV, seed ^ 0x9E37u);
    return (1.0 + c * bed) * (1.0 + c * GROUND_STRATA_SUB_RATIO * sub);
}
// rock colour = alpine * GROUND_ROCK_TINT * ring_ground_strata_factor(h, fwidth(h))
```

- Each bed has its own flat tone with crisp contacts. The mean is preserved, because `tone` averages to 0 and the filter tends to it.
- At the default contrast of 0.12, beds differ by up to plus or minus 12 %, and sub-beds by plus or minus 6 %.
- A vertical face at 1080p and a 70 degree field of view has about 1.1 m per pixel at 1 km. So 40 m beds stay visible to about 15 km and fade beyond, while 5 m sub-beds fade between about 1.5 and 4 km. The plan's "fine sub-band near the camera" therefore comes from the filter, with no distance constant.
- **Derivatives.** `fwidth(h)` must be evaluated in uniform control flow: at the top of the feature block, which branches only on the global bit, and passed into `ring_ground_band`, never inside a per-pixel `if (rock > 0)`. Texture taps inside weight branches follow the shaders' existing pattern (the proxy already samples alpine and dirt inside weight branches). D6 branches on distance, not weight.
- Strata act only through the rock weight. Flat alpine ground, which also shows the alpine texture, gets no contour stripes.
- Bed contacts are exactly horizontal at fixed heights, as decided by the user (plan 13.1, decision 5). If they read too regular, an undulation is a later question, not part of this step.

**Absolute height source per tier:**
- **Terrain3D:** `h = surface_height + ring_anchor_height`. It is a bilinear surface; in float32 it resolves about 1 mm at 8 km.
- **Proxy:** computed in `vertex()` after the geomorph (line 111), passed as `varying float ground_height`:

  ```glsl
  vec3 o = MODEL_MATRIX[3].xyz;                      // sector origin = canonical height 0 (S3)
  vec3 r = o - ring_center; r.y = 0.0;
  float R = length(r);
  mat3 to_model = inverse(mat3(MODEL_MATRIX));
  vec3 up_m = normalize(to_model * (-r / R));
  vec3 around_m = normalize(cross(normalize(to_model * vec3(0.0, 1.0, 0.0)), up_m));
  float a = dot(VERTEX, around_m);
  ground_height = dot(VERTEX, up_m) - a * a / (2.0 * R);
  ```

  - Precision: the 0.5 m float32 error in `r` only tilts `up_m` by about 1e-7 rad. Model-space values resolve about 1 mm. The dropped terms are of order d^3/R^2: under 1 mm for d up to 2 km, which covers every level where strata can show.
  - Slot and mesh sectors behave identically. No new vertex data, no cache or kernel change. Only `ring_terrain_proxy.gdshader` changes; the shadow and ocean shaders do not.
- **Far:** none. Wherever the far tiers draw, the footprint is at least one band, so the filter returns the mean.
- **T5 rock meshes:** the same method, from the instance origin's canonical height (T5-2 B4 decides).

**Phase at the 112 m hand-off.** Both tiers compute the canonical height of the same 2 m lattice surface. They differ only by Terrain3D's bilinear surface against the proxy's planar triangles, at most about 0.1 m on steep 2 m cells: 0.3 % of a 40 m band and 2 % of a 5 m sub-band. T4-3 tests band-index equality at matched heights, including near a boundary, and a half-band offset must fail.

**Aliasing review** (plan 11):
1. The filter is analytic and exact below one band of footprint.
2. `fwidth` per 2 x 2 quad is the standard footprint, and it grows on grazing faces, so the fade comes earlier there, which is correct.
3. There is no time input.
4. The hash uses an integer band index, so it cannot drift with precision.
5. T4-6 runs the flicker check on slow pans at `steep_eye` and `cliff_eye`.

**Publication.** `RingGroundSurfaceGlobals.PublishWorld` reads `breaks_step_m` and the seed from the recipe at world load. It is the same owner and moment as the feature mask.

### D4. Scree

```glsl
float ring_ground_scree(float slope_deg, float baseline_m, float relief, float vegetated) {
    float s = slope_deg + ring_ground_slope_bias(baseline_m);
    float band = smoothstep(GROUND_SCREE_IN.x, GROUND_SCREE_IN.y, s) * (1.0 - smoothstep(GROUND_SCREE_OUT.x, GROUND_SCREE_OUT.y, s));
    return band * (1.0 - smoothstep(GROUND_SCREE_CONVEX_FADE.x, GROUND_SCREE_CONVEX_FADE.y, relief))
                * mix(1.0, GROUND_SCREE_VEGETATED, clamp(vegetated, 0.0, 1.0));   // vegetated = grass weight + forest weight
}
// composition: ground = mix(ground, alpine * GROUND_SCREE_TINT, scree); ground = mix(ground, rock_colour, rock);
```

**Why slope plus concavity, not "below rock".** A pixel cannot see what lies above it. But 28-38 degree ground that is not convex at 64 m is the lower, concave part of a slope profile: talus and the slope foot. Convex ground in the same band is a shoulder, and gets no scree.

**Vegetated biomes get 40 %.** Grassy hill country at 30 degrees is mostly soil, and hill country is where most 28-38 degree ground is (R1). Full-strength scree there would put grey bands on every steep hill.

Until T4-4b, relief = 0 and the rule reduces to the slope band and the biome factor. The C# mirror is `RingGroundSurfaceModel.ScreeWeight(...)`; T5 uses it for cobbles.

### D5. Colour variation

**The field, C64**, in metres:
- Nodes: the canonical 32 m lattice (around = 32i, across = 32j, in canonical metres; across clamps to the ring width).
- At a node: `C64 = h(node) - 0.25 * (h(node + 64 m around) + h(node - 64 m around) + h(node + 64 m across) + h(node - 64 m across))`.
- At any other point: the bilinear interpolation of the four nodes of its 32 m cell.
- h is the presentation height the level uses for its vertices (the carved height in river worlds).
- `relief = C64 / (abs(C64) + GROUND_RELIEF_REF_M)`, in (-1, 1).

The C# mirror is `RingGroundSurfaceModel.NodeConvexity` and `Convexity64(Func<double, double, double> heightAt, double around, double across)`. T5-1's habitat grid takes its 64 m curvature from it (T5 P12, C2).

**Colour** (applied to the dry albedo before wetness, grass coverage and the albedo scale):

```glsl
float s = ringworld_ground_look.z * (1.0 - smoothstep(GROUND_RELIEF_FADE_M.x, GROUND_RELIEF_FADE_M.y, d)) * (1.0 - rock);
float lum = dot(col, vec3(0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722));
col = mix(col, mix(col, vec3(lum), 0.25) * 1.12, s * max(relief, 0.0));   // convex: paler, a little less saturated
col = mix(col, col * vec3(0.88, 0.94, 0.86), s * max(-relief, 0.0));      // hollow: darker and greener
```

At the default strength of 1 the shift is plus or minus 12 % at saturation. Typical hill country has a 64-128 m band RMS of 1.3 m (T0 8.1), which gives about plus or minus 4-6 %. That is a realistic soil-and-moisture contrast, not an exaggeration. The menu can go higher, but only as the user's own choice.

**Terrain3D: computed in the vertex stage**, which is cheaper than per fragment (C1):
- For every vertex the NT-1 drop keeps, take the 4 nodes of its 32 m cell x 5 taps: 20 `texelFetch`es with `VERTEX_PASS` region lookups at plus or minus 32 cells.
- Nodes are placed through a new material uniform `ring_ground_lattice_offset` (vec2: anchor around mod 32 and anchor across mod 32, canonical metres, across direction applied). `RingTerrain3DSurface` sets it next to line 338. The existing texture anchors are modulo 1000 (S2) and cannot serve.
- Output: `varying float v_ground_c64`.
- A tap outside the window falls back to its node's height, which makes that tap contribute 0.
- Reach: handoff 112 m + cell 32 m + tap 64 m = 208 m, within the 384 m half-window. T4-4b checks the largest camera-to-anchor offset.

**Proxies: option (b), a per-vertex value.**
- (a) is rejected: Lod1's 64 m tap is half of its 128 m sector, so neighbour fetches within one slot fail near every edge and leave seams.
- (c) is rejected: it leaves 112 m to 24 km bare, which is most of what the player sees.
- Reading the height-shadow clipmap is rejected: its texel size depends on the preset and the altitude band (`RingHeightShadowLattice.TexelForBand`), so the look would change with the preset.
- Building (T4-4a):
  - CPU builders (2 and 4 m slot levels, river sectors, 64 m mesh levels) compute the apron node heights at build time; nothing is cached, and the sample-cache format is unchanged. Extra height samples per sector, (S/32 + 5)^2 - (S/32 + 1)^2: Lod1 56 (+1.3 %), Lod2 88 (+2.1 %). 64 m levels need none, because their plus or minus 64 m taps are the existing one-vertex halo.
  - GPU k2 (8, 16 and 32 m): `PASS_S` evaluates the apron nodes into an extra region of the heights buffer: 152 (+3.4 %), 280 (+6.2 %) and 272 (+6.1 %) heights per sector. `PASS_A` computes node C64, takes the bilinear value per vertex, and packs it.
  - Levels of 128 m and coarser store nothing; the fade ends at 24 km, inside Lod6's range at High. At Medium, where Lod6 ends sooner, the variation ends in that level's fade band, softly.
- Packing:
  - Slot: lattice texels `attr.z` bits 16-31 = `snorm16(C64 / 32 m)` (1 mm steps). Skirt and water texels are unchanged. A new include function `proxy_vertex_table_relief(int vertex_id)` re-reads the cache-hot attr texel, so `proxy_vertex_table_apply` stays unchanged.
  - Mesh: in feature worlds `CUSTOM0` becomes `RgbaFloat` with `w = C64` (`xyz = 0` on sectors without a runtime chord). Generation 1 keeps today's formats.

- No seams by construction, since the nodes are on a global lattice.
- This is the format change the WO foresaw for option (b), in the slot payload and k2. It is not a disk-cache change and not a strata change, so no escalation condition is met.

- Terrain3D only; the proxies start at 112 m, beyond the radius.

### D11. River beds, banks and dry basin floors (T4-7 addendum)

claude-lead, 2026-11-18, against `9c1bfbc` plus the working tree. For the primary's check before T4-7 starts. Questions R1-R3 at the end of this section.

**Facts this rests on (verified 2026-11-18):**
- F1. Weights reach every tier from one C# function, `RingTerrainSampler.GetBiomeTextureWeights(in TerrainSample)` (`RingTerrainSampler.cs:776`). Terrain3D takes them per 2 m sample (`GetPresentationSurfaceHeightAndBiomeWeights`, fused lattice path `RingTerrainSampler.SurfaceGeneration.cs:43-54`; the gameplay stream uses the same call). The proxies take them per **16 m column**: `ResolveBiomeTextureWeight` (`RingTerrainProxySector.cs:4793`) samples the column centre at footprint 0, for every level. The far tier takes them through `RingTerrainVisuals.ResolveMacroTextureColor`. GPU k2 sectors compute them in GLSL (`rc_biome_weights`).
- F2. Today a wet sample of any kind gives pure Desert (`GetNaturalBiomeTextureWeights`, :793). On the proxies a 16 m column whose centre is wet paints the whole column sand, dry bank included.
- F3. `Diagnostics` already carries what the zones need: `InlandWater`, `WaterLevel` (the owner's level, finite for dry bank points inside a river core and for dry lake-shore points with P > 0), `RiverReach`, `ChannelOffset` and `DryBasin` (`TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs:12`, set at :165). The river core ends at the bank top `L + f` (`f = RiverHydraulics.Freeboard = 0.4`), and the lake plug keeps the ground at `Level + f` or higher where P = 0 (T2 S7, S8). The reach speed is `RiverNetwork.Sections[reach].Speed` (Manning, m/s). Basins: `RiverGraph.Basins[b].FloorLevel/SpillLevel`, `DryBasinAt`, `LakeAt`, `RiverLake.Basin` (-1 for a spilling lake).
- F4. River and lake sectors build on the CPU at every level (T2R-11, `touchesRiver`). **Dry basin floors do not**: they are 5.43 % of land cells (seed 713263263, `graph_report.txt` of 2026-11-13), and a dry cell more than one cell from a lake or reach is not `Near`, so its 8, 16 and 32 m sectors go to k2.
- F5. The proxy disk cache stores samples, and the vertex colours are rebuilt from fresh column samples, not read from the cache. The river record is version 13 (`LodSectorCacheRiverVersion`, PT2-3).
- F6. Textures (`d11_material_swatches.png` in `.godot/audit_baseline/common/t4/t4-7/`): Desert is bright saturated sand (linear luminance 0.475); Tundra is an olive grey-brown ground (0.083) that reads as river mud or silt; Alpine is grey gravel (0.208); Forest is dark leaf litter. Grass cards spawn on `grass * 0.82 + forest * 0.42 + sparse * 0.08` (`RingGrassTileBuilder.cs:243-245`), so Desert, Tundra and Alpine ground stays almost bare and Forest does not.

**Decisions.**
1. **Weights only, no shader change.** Every material below is a target in the existing four channels (Desert D, Forest F, Tundra T, Alpine A; grass is the remainder). The near, proxy, far and grass shaders are not edited. GPU cost is zero by construction, and the D7 variants and the T1f goldens are untouched. Forest is never used: it would grow grass cards on mud.
2. **Permanent bank wetness is albedo.** The wet margin and the bed use darkened "wet" mixes (about 60 to 75 % of the dry luminance, the soak darkening of wet sediment). The sheen of a wet margin is not included: the weight route has no carrier for it, and the wet map (`ring_wetness.gdshaderinc`) is off in clear weather, so routing static wetness through it would keep the map and its cost on permanently. `ring_wetness.gdshaderinc` stays read-only. In rain the wet map darkens the margin further, as it does any soil (known, accepted).
3. **Zones by height above the water, not by distance.** dz = presentation height minus the owner's level. On the operator's profile dz is a function of the distance across the bank, so the bands follow the true waterline at every bend, confluence and lake shore, and they need no new operator output.
4. **Two parts, stored per sample.** `TerrainSample` appends `RiverBedMask RiverBeds = default`, 4 bytes: `RiverCover`, `TargetSand`, `TargetGravel` (target Tundra = 1 minus both) and `PlayaCover`, each a byte in [0, 1]. The sampler fills it in `CreateRuntimeTerrainSampleLocked` (pass the footprint in) only when `HasRiverBeds` (rivers graph present and mask bit 3). Height-only samples, edits and every other world keep the default. Edited columns keep the unedited mask (digging does not move the material).
5. **Application.** `GetBiomeTextureWeights(in TerrainSample)` becomes `RiverBedSurfaceModel.Apply(ApplySiteSurfaceMask(natural, site), sample.RiverBeds)`: first `w = lerp(w, Playa, PlayaCover)`, then `w = lerp(w, RiverTarget, RiverCover)`. With both covers 0 it returns `w` itself (bitwise identity).
6. **Proxies apply the mask per vertex, not per column.** In river-beds worlds `ResolveBiomeTextureWeight` calls a new `GetLandBiomeTextureWeights(around, across)`: natural weights with River and Lake samples treated as land (climate blend; Ocean still sand), site mask applied, no river mask. Each terrain vertex then takes `RiverBedSurfaceModel.Apply(columnWeights, point.Sample.RiverBeds)` from its own cached sample at the level's footprint (both colour sites, :4218 and :4634). This removes the 16 m sand blocks of F2 in feature worlds. Terrain3D and the 2 m proxy level evaluate the mask at the same points with footprint 0, so they match at the hand-off.
7. **Cache.** River-beds worlds write record version **14** = version 13 plus the packed mask (`uint`). Version 13 (rivers without bit 3) and version 11 (generation 1) keep their bytes. `BuildLodSectorCacheDefinition` mixes `RiverBedSurfaceModel.Version` (start at 1) into river-beds worlds only, so a change to any constant below invalidates their caches.
8. **Dry basins route to the CPU.** `RingTerrainSampler.IntersectsClosedBasin(rect)`: true when any 1 km cell overlapping the rectangle is a dry cell, or a wet cell of a lake with `Basin >= 0`. It is false without bit 3. `touchesBasin` sits beside `touchesRiver` in `RouteMissingToGpu`, with the same envelope and a new counter `RingProxyBuildCounters.AddGpuBasinToCpu(spacing)`. There is no GLSL port. The playa never reaches outside member cells (rule P below), so a k2 neighbour always shows natural ground at a shared edge.
9. **Far tier: playa only.** `WithoutFarRiverOwner` also sets `RiverCover = 0` on every sample of a river-beds world, so a far vertex that lands on a bank or bed paints no cell. Wet far lake samples stay sand as today. Dry basin floors are kilometre-scale and show in the far colours through the same weights, as a dry lakebed seen from orbit should.

**Materials** (targets; linear luminance from the swatch):

| material | target | luminance | used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| WetSand | 0.55 D + 0.45 T | 0.30 | fine bed and bar, lake shore |
| WetGravel | 0.60 A + 0.40 T | 0.16 | coarse bed and bar |
| Mud | T | 0.08 | fine bank, deep lake bed (silt) |
| Gravel | A | 0.21 | coarse bank |
| Playa | 0.45 D + 0.55 A | 0.33 | dry lakebed (pale grey-beige clay) |

**Zones.** h = presentation height; L = `Diagnostics.WaterLevel`; dz = h - L; fp = the sample's footprint (Terrain3D, controller and the 2 m level 0; the 4 m level 4).

| zone | where | material |
|---|---|---|
| bed | River owner, dz < 0 | `mix(WetSand, WetGravel, g)` |
| bar (wet margin) | River owner, 0 <= dz < 0.15 | same as the bed edge, then `mix(.., bank, smoothstep(0.10, 0.20, dz))` |
| bank | River owner, 0.15 <= dz, within the core | `mix(Mud, Gravel, g)`; cover `1 - smoothstep(0.25, 0.38, dz)`, 0 at the bank top |
| lake bed | Lake owner, dz < 0 | `mix(WetSand, Mud, smoothstep(1, 4, depth))` (sandy shallows, silty deep water) |
| lake shore | Lake owner, dz >= 0 | WetSand; cover `1 - smoothstep(0.10, 0.25, dz)` |
| floodplain | outside the core | natural ground, unchanged (floodplains are vegetated; riparian plants are T6-12) |
| dry basin floor | closed-basin member cell, arid | Playa, cover rule P |

- **Coarseness** `g = smoothstep(0.6, 1.4, U)`, U the reach's mean speed in m/s. With Manning at n = 0.035 and 1 m depth, that is sand below a gradient of about 4e-4 and gravel above about 2.5e-3, which is where sand-bed and gravel-bed rivers part on Earth. Torrents and gorges are gravel. Per reach, so the bed changes at a confluence and nowhere else. (Spans would be finer, but `Diagnostics` has no s; T5-11 may refine it.)
- **River cover** is 1 below dz = 0.25 and falls to 0 at dz = 0.38, inside the 0.4 m bank top, so it is continuous where the core (and with it L) ends. The lake fade ends at 0.25, inside the plug's 0.4. The bars are narrow (about 0.6 of the bank width, 1.2 to 7 m); a 40 m beach would be an exaggeration.
- **Margins only where the level resolves them.** For fp > 4 the river and lake cover is multiplied by `1 - smoothstep(-0.10, 0.0, dz)`: bed material under water only, no bar, bank or shore. A 2 to 12 m bank is sub-cell from the 8 m level on (beyond about 1 km), and point-sampling it there would leave dashes along every river. Where a level holds no owner (`w < 2 fp`, S13.2) there is no mask at all, and the T2R-12b/12c line tint is the river.
- **Rule P (playa).** Basin b = the sample cell's `DryBasin`, else the basin of `LakeAt(cell)` when the operator reports a Lake owner; otherwise no playa. With F = FloorLevel, Z = SpillLevel, D = max(Z - F, 0), e = h - F, top = min(8, 0.5 D), full = 0.4 top:
  `PlayaCover = (1 - smoothstep(full, top, e)) * (1 - smoothstep(0.30, 0.45, moisture)) * smoothstep(0, 0.5, P_b)`.
  - The band follows contours: the low, flat part of the basin, up to 3 m above the floor in full and fading out by 8 m, never above half the basin's depth, so it never reaches the rim.
  - Aridity: the Desert biome ends at moisture 0.30. A humid closed basin keeps its natural ground (wet meadow), not a playa.
  - P_b is the bilinear interpolation, between the four surrounding 1 km cell centres, of J = +1 for a member cell of b and -1 otherwise (as S8's P). It is 0 on a cell edge to a non-member, so the 1 km cells never show as squares. Memoise J for the last 2 x 2 block per sampler worker.
  - **Salt flats are not drawn** (question R1): a white crust needs a colour that the four channels do not hold. S8b's split (moisture below 0.35 and within 20 m of the floor) stays the definition for when one exists.
- **Gorge walls and the T4 rock rule.** The mask lives only in dz below 0.38 m and on basin floors, so a gorge wall gets no bed or bank material. In ground-surface worlds D2 (rock) and D4 (scree) are mixed after the biome mix, so on steep ground rock wins over any weight, beds included. The core bank is at most about 28 degrees (a 2 m bank rising 0.7 m), below the rock start: banks keep their material and the wall above them turns to rock. Scree may tint the steepest 2 m banks of the smallest streams a little (sparse weights give full scree). Accepted.
- **River line tint (T2R-12b/12c).** By S11b a level shows a river either as water geometry (an owner) or as line coverage, never both. D11's mask exists only where the level's own samples have an owner (or a basin), so bed materials sit under water geometry and the tint stands alone where there is none. On the far tier the river cover is cleared (decision 9). Nothing is counted twice, and there is no gap between the two.
- **Other consumers.** Grass cards read the same weights (`GridWeights`, cards): bars, banks and playa are Desert, Tundra and Alpine, so they stay nearly bare. Trees follow `Biome`, not weights (unchanged; riparian trees are T6-12). The distant grass-coverage term takes its eligibility from the weights. Wetness, colour variation, rock, scree and the albedo scale act on top, as on any ground. No grave-key row: these are realistic defaults baked at build time, not live tunables.

**C# shape.** New `scripts/Terrain/Surface/RiverBedSurfaceModel.cs` (static, the constants above, `Version`, `Coarseness(speed)`, `RiverPart(kind, dz, g, fp)`, `PlayaCover(...)`, `Pack/Unpack`, `Apply(weights, mask)`). T5-11 (bars) and T6-12 (galleries) read the zones through it. `RingTerrainSampler`: `HasRiverBeds`, `GetLandBiomeTextureWeights`, `IntersectsClosedBasin`. Ledger: `FeatureInfo("ground.river_beds", 3, "T4", 0)` (open); `docs/world-generations.md` row updated. Without `water.rivers` the bit does nothing.

**Cost.**
- GPU: 0 (no shader, vertex-format or draw change). Measured as two boots, not A-B-A: the T4-2 `--feature-ab` hook toggles only the shader global, and nothing reads bit 3 there.
- CPU, per full sample: one branch where there is no owner and no basin. Near water, a few smoothsteps and one `Sections` read. On member cells, one `LakeAt` plus the memoised 2 x 2 block. That is well under 5 % of a model evaluation.
- Routing: dry-floor sectors of 8 to 32 m that are not already river sectors move from k2 to the CPU. Measure the share and the per-level build median (D10 line: at most +5 % proxy build CPU; T2R-11's warning stands at 40 % CPU-routed land sectors at the 2 km level).
- Memory: `TerrainSample` +4 B in every world (values unchanged in generation 1, as T2R-10's precedent); +4 B per cached record in river-beds worlds.

**Identity.** With bit 3 off every path is the old one: the default mask, the old column weights, v13/v11 records, the old routing. Guards: `world_generation_fingerprint_test`, `inland_water_sampler_test` (v11/v12 bytes, as far as its known failure allows), `ground_relief_field_test` (sector-data golden), `terrain_surface_include_regression`, and a new pre-capture: hashes of the vertex colours of four river sectors (2, 4, 64 m and a lake shore) in a rivers world without bit 3, taken before any edit and equal after.
