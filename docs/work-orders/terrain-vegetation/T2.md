## Phase rules

These apply to every work order below, in addition to TEMPLATE.md.

- **Executors.** Claude Code agents from `.claude/agents/`: `implementer` (bounded changes), `claude-lead` (design, investigation, review), `claude-deep` (only with the primary's approval and a question that fits in five lines). Where TEMPLATE.md says `sol_implementer`, `sol_lead` or `sol_deep`, read these.
- **Production first.** Every order lands production code or a test of production code. No study-only tools. A number that decides something is printed by the bake report or by a test.
- **Construct, do not fit.** The operator builds the bed, banks and lake plugs, so water is contained by construction (reset doc 4.1). No order may try to prove that the natural terrain already contains the water.
- **Sampling with tolerances.** An invariant is checked on a fixed, deterministic sample set. It passes when at least 99.5 % of samples pass and none exceeds the stated hard limit. Failing samples are printed with coordinates. Exact equality is used only for determinism hashes and for generation 1.
- **STOP RULE.** Each order has a budget in *periods* (one watchdog period, 55 minutes of agent work) and *attempts* (one attempt is a build plus a test run). When either is used up, or an escalation condition is met, stop and report in at most 15 lines: what works, what fails, the evidence path, one proposed next step. Do not add tools, probes, proofs or acceptance criteria. The primary decides what happens next.
- **No new requirements from inside.** If a criterion seems to be missing, propose it in the report.
- **Testing cadence.** One to three directly relevant tests per change, with `-Tests` and `-DryRun` first. The affected compatibility checks once at the end of the order. `-Suite gate` only at the three commit points (after T2R-6, after T2R-11, at T2R-16). Serialise C# edits and builds.
- **Tolerances are starting values.** If a tolerance in this file is exceeded, that is an escalation with a picture. The primary may change the tolerance, tune a constant, or start a step of the fallback ladder (T2R-15).
- **Pictures.** The user gets pictures at three milestones: M1 the river map (T2R-4, T2R-5), M2 carved ground (T2R-8), M3 first water (T2R-11). Evidence per report: at most five links.

### T2R-7b. River operator follow-up (from the T2R-7 review)

### T2R-8b. Rivers through lakes (from the T2R-8 review)

ROLE: implementer
SIZE: M
DEPENDS ON: T2R-8 and its review (Review notes, "T2R-8 primary review"). Serialise builds with T2R-9. Before T2R-10 and before the M2 pictures go to the user.

OBJECTIVE
A river that meets a lake is one continuous body of water with it. Every stem that flows into a lake reaches the lake's outlet (or its deepest cell, for a closed lake) along a drowned channel at the lake level, and the outlet stem starts inside the lake.

CONTEXT / ROOT CAUSE
- A lake's flooded cells are 1 km cells of the routing surface: the lowest of 16 samples per cell, plus up to 6 m of routing noise, at a whole-metre level. The shoreline is the fine terrain. Two flooded cells that touch on the lattice need not be joined by water on the ground.
- A stem ends at the node of the first flooded cell and an outlet starts at the node of the first dry cell (`RiverGraphBuilder.Build`, steps 1, 2 and 4). A node is the cell's one lowest sample, moved by up to 60 m of jitter. Nothing cuts the ground between that node and the lake's water.
- Measured on the 400 km strip of `river_carve_invariants_test` (seed 1337), T2R-8 state: 78 of 187 inlet and outlet ends stand on natural ground at or above the lake level; on 24 lakes rastered at 16 m, 32 of 48 ends stand apart from the lake's water, by up to 5.9 km. The M2 lake picture shows 1.8 km at an inlet and 0.8 km at the outlet (`.godot/captures/t2r8/m2_4_lake_shoreline_diagnosis.png`).
- Tried in the review and rejected: lengthening each stem only to the first flooded cell whose lowest sample is 2 m under the level (`.godot/captures/t2r8/review_option2_lake_connectors.patch`). Dry ends fell to 3 of 187, but 14 of 48 rastered ends still stood apart (up to 5.7 km), because a 2 m pocket is often not joined to the rest of the lake. The lead rule of item 2 and the jitter rule of item 4 come from that patch and worked. The patch also made two existing checks fail ("containment did not fail with the core lower bound off on a fabricated dip" and "the lake operator makes a step or a waterline difference worse than the river-only state"); expect them here and see validation 3.

### T2R-12b. Area-weighted river coverage and glint on the proxy levels (from the T2R-12 escalation)

ROLE: implementer
SIZE: M
DEPENDS ON: T2R-12 (`fabb558`) and its lead review (Review notes, "T2R-12 lead review and user look decision"; Spec S11 as amended, S11b). Serialise builds with T2R-13.

OBJECTIVE
The proxy levels, which draw everything the player sees out to 8,000 km, carry the true area fraction of river water per vertex and show it as a tint and a sun glint of the same strength. No minimum coverage anywhere: a thin river dims with distance, a wide river stays as wide as it is. The 0.2 floors of T2R-12 are removed from the far tier, and far-tier samples no longer carry a river owner.

CONTEXT / ROOT CAUSE
- **User look decision (2026-11-12): realistic, not map-like.** Coverage is area-weighted. Glint strength follows real coverage. Big rivers and lakes stay visible as their real size allows.
- T2R-12 put the coverage into `ring_terrain_global` (global LOD, far viewport, tiles). In the fly-out that shader draws no pixel; `ring_terrain_proxy` covers the screen (emission probe). The proxy levels run from 2 m to 32,768 m spacing and fade out at 6,500 to 8,000 km (scenes/VoxelRingIntegrationTest.tscn).
- Levels of 512 m and coarser sample at `RiverFootprint.Far` (valley only, S10), and at 256 m a river narrower than 16 m has no strip (`RiverCarveOperator.ValleyOnly`, :236). So from 110 km a river of any width has no water signal at all: a 1 km river disappears, and no glint is possible because the shader does not know where water is.
- Carrier. Proxy `COLOR` is four biome weights. `UV2.y` of a terrain lattice vertex is a flag that is always 0 (RingTerrainProxySector.cs:3952); water and skirt vertices hold 1 (:3955, :5043). Every reader only tests `< .5` or `>= 0.5` (ring_terrain_proxy.gdshader:99, ring_terrain_proxy_shadow.gdshader:33, RingTerrainProxySector.cs:4141, RingTerrainProxySector.VertexTable.cs:408 and :1033). The coverage rides there as `0.49 * c`: no new vertex stream, no memory, a float (no 8-bit quantum).
- Levels of 64 m and coarser install as meshes (`SpacingUsesVertexTableInstall` is 2 to 32 m), so the slot tables and the K2 kernel are not involved. The sector disk cache stores samples, not vertex payloads (:523), so no cache or record version changes.
- A splat that deposits each piece of channel area onto the lattice conserves area by construction. A river cannot fall between samples, so no floor is needed for continuity (Spec S11b).
- Far consumers sample with footprint 0 (`GetPresentationTerrainSample`), so a far vertex that lands in a channel is a River sample and paints a whole far cell as water. S10 wanted no river owner there.

FILES/SYMBOLS
- scripts/Terrain/Hydrology/RiverGraph.cs (`LineCoverage`, new `SplatLineArea`); RiverFootprint.cs (new `UsesProxyLine`); RiverCarveOperator.cs (`ValleyOnly` becomes internal, no other change).

2. **`RiverGraph.SplatLineArea(double aroundOrigin, double acrossOrigin, double spacing, int columns, int rows, double footprintMeters, Span<float> coverage)`**, returning true when any value is above 0 (Spec S11b). Vertex (x, z) is at `origin + index * spacing`; around is unwrapped metres. It clears `coverage` (row-major), then:
   - collects pieces once with `Index.CollectPieces` for the lattice rectangle grown by `spacing + RiverSpatialIndex.ReachRadiusMeters`;
   - walks each piece in `CoverageSegmentsPerPiece` segments when `spacing <= 1024`, and as one segment (the end points, length `curve.Len[k]`) above;
   - per segment reads `Network.ChannelAt` at the middle, and skips the segment unless `RiverCarveOperator.ValleyOnly(footprintMeters, channel.HalfWidth)`: the coverage stands in exactly where the level has no river owner;
   - for `spacing <= 1024` moves the segment by `Network.Meander(stem, s, in channel)` along the normal that `RiverCarveOperator.LocatePiece` uses, so the coverage lies in the carved valley;
   - splits the segment into `n = max(1, ceil(len / (0.5 * spacing)))` steps along and `m = max(1, ceil(w / (0.5 * spacing)))` samples across the width `w = 2 * HalfWidth`; each sample adds `(len / n) * (w / m) / spacing^2` to its four surrounding vertices with bilinear weights (vertices outside the lattice are skipped);

## Design

The reasons are in docs/t2-river-reset.md section 4. This section records the decisions so T3 and later phases can cite them.

### D1. Grid and sampling
**Decision.** Cells of about 1 km, `round(circumference / 1000)` columns of equal spacing, exact wrap. 4 x 4 natural-height samples per cell on a 250 m lattice at integer metres. Cell value: the lowest sample. Node position: that sample. Moisture at the centre. Sources: GPU in the game, CPU as the reference. No smoothing. Checked by `drainage_grid_test` and `drainage_height_source_gpu_test`.

### D2. Barriers, outlets, seam, walls and moisture
**Decision.** Analytic footprints from the existing registries (emitter discs, the Wall Engine stadium, the Weather Lung footprint), expanded by the corridor radius (300 m), the lateral cap (350 m) and half a cell diagonal. Rim walls on both across edges. Wrap at the seam. Diagonals cannot pass a blocked orthogonal neighbour. The sea (any sample below 0) and the Weather Lung basin are outlets; the Lung is an isolated receiving body with no synthetic inflow. Runoff uses the recipe's moisture. Checked by `drainage_graph_test`.

### D3. Depression conditioning
**Decision.** Priority-flood on the valley-floor grid. Least-cost breaches of at most 40 m and 32 cells, ranked by cut volume; the cheapest half is cut (`BreachShare`); `lake_abundance` acts through the water balance (S8b). A depression at least 2 m deep is a lake; a shallower one is routing fill. A lake's level is the lowest unperturbed value among the cells bordering its flooded set, barrier cells included. No partial drawdown. The lake share is a measured result, shown to the user at M1.

### D4. Routing and path shape
**Decision.** Topology: steepest descent, flood parents in filled cells, a 6 m routing noise, crossing repair. Shape: the centreline of D7. The user's criterion (F36) is measured on production centrelines by `RiverPathMetrics`, validated on the frozen baseline. Barnes flats, near-tie, facet, valley-corridor and global routing are not used.

### D5. Channels, reaches, index and memory
**Decision.** Threshold `100 km^2 / river_density`. Reaches between sources, confluences, lake contacts and the sea. Profile knots per reach. Index: a bit per cell and 4 km buckets. Gate 32 MB. Details: Spec S5.

### D6. Interim hydraulics
**Decision.** Plan 3.2 relations with a runoff scale that makes the smallest mapped river swimmable: pool depth at least 1.2 m, width at least 8 m. The carve uses the same width and depth. T3-3 recalibrates; tests check relations. Details: Spec S6.

### D7. River operator
**Decision.** Reset doc 4.3. Quadratic B-spline centreline with wander and meander. Level: the running minimum of the sampled ground along the path, in whole-metre pools with chutes. Section: bed, bank, then the natural ground clamped between a levee bound and a valley bound, fading to zero by 300 m. Overlaps: lowest upper bound, highest lower bound, channel wins. Footprint: the channel is at least 1.5 footprints wide and proportionally shallower; the level does not change. Bank fill is allowed and reported. Details: Spec S7.

### D8. Lakes
**Decision.** Reset doc 4.4. Level from the valley-floor flood. Region: the flooded cells. Free shoreline where the ground is below the level and the membership weight is above zero. Plugs on the dry ring only. The outlet holds the lake level to the ring; inlets drown. Details: Spec S8.

### D9. Sample fields and consumers
**Decision.** An additive float level with a validity flag; the int is its floor. Classifier, only with the feature: operator water, then the sea rule, then none; the river wins inside its channel where the natural ground is at or above 0. Edits reclassify on the interpolated bed. Feature worlds use a v12 proxy cache record with the footprint in the key. Generation 1 is byte-identical. Details: Spec S9.

### D10. Proxy routing and near water
**Decision.** River and lake sectors build on the CPU, selected by the union of consumer sample envelopes. Proxy levels pass their spacing as the footprint. Fallbacks: T2R-15. Details: Spec S10.

### D11. Far water and minimap
**Decision.** Rivers: line coverage from the graph with a minimum, glint, a whitewater input at 0. Lakes: point classification. Minimap: polylines. No wet-area mips. Details: Spec S11.
**Amended 2026-11-12 (user look decision, T2R-12b).** No minimum: coverage is the true area fraction, and the glint scales with it. The proxy levels carry the same coverage per vertex (Spec S11b). The row D11 in docs/t2-river-reset.md ("with a minimum so a river stays a line") is superseded by this.

### D12. Bake lifecycle
**Decision.** A feature-only wait before the world is attached; a worker lease with cancellation; inputs captured on the main thread; no sampler needed to bake. GPU source with CPU fallback. The graph file is world data under `<world>/hydrology/` and is never rebaked while valid. The CPU bake is the reference for goldens and the generation test world. Details: Spec S12.

### D13. Recipe fields
**Decision.** As T2R-2. Defaults and ranges are provisional until the freeze. `river_ferocity` is read-only at 1 until T3-1.

### D14. Hash, file and versions
**Decision.** A pre-graph input identity (recipe fields that affect the bake, natural-height identity, barrier registries, moisture inputs, algorithm version) finds and validates the file. The graph identity hash is appended to the runtime definition hash. `DrainageAlgorithm` changes with bake output, `CarveOperator` with operator output, `GraphFormat` with the file layout. After the freeze a bump is a new feature on a later step.

## Spec

Written by T2R-1 on 2026-11-11 against `0cce3a6` plus the working tree. Line numbers are from that read; re-check before editing. Values marked **(tune at review)** are the reset doc's defaults or a starting value, not a measurement. Notation: C circumference, `smoothstep(e0, e1, x)` as in GLSL, `fp` footprint in metres, `ms` = `meander_strength`, `vw` = `river_valley_width`.

### S5. Graph records, index and bytes

**Decision.** One immutable, resident `RiverGraph` of flat arrays (no per-query allocation). Curves are built per **stem**: a maximal chain that, at each confluence, continues up the incoming branch with the larger contributing area (tie: lower cell index). Reaches are stem intervals between sources, confluences, lake contacts and the sea. The main river therefore stays C1 through confluences, and a tributary stem ends on the receiving stem's curve.
- Grid: `W = round(C / 1000)` columns (31,416), `H = 318` rows, cell `c = row * W + col`, cell size `C / W` by 1,000 m.
- **Node** (stored, 8 B): `AroundDm`, `AcrossDm` (int32 decimetres, around wrapped). Includes jitter and wander (S7). A tributary's last node is the junction point on the receiving stem's displaced curve.
- **Stem** (stored, 32 B): `NodeStart`, `NodeCount`, `KnotStart`, `KnotCount`, `ReachStart`, `ReachCount` (int32); `Terminal` (byte: Sea, Lake, Confluence, Lung); `TerminalId` (lake id or receiving stem id); `JunctionS` (float, receiving stem arc length).
- **Reach** (stored, 32 B): `Stem`, `SStart`, `SEnd` (float stem arc length), `AreaKm2` (unweighted contributing area at its last cell), `Q` (float), `S` (float gradient, S6), `MeanderPhase` (float, phase at `SStart`), `UpstreamLake` (int, -1). Width, depth, speed, class, bank, floodplain and slope are derived at load by `RiverHydraulics` (S6), never stored.
- **Knot** (stored, 8 B): `S`, `W` (float). Piecewise linear, non-increasing in S (S7).
- **Lake** (stored, 40 B): `Level` (float, whole metres, S8), `RawLevel` (float, report only), `CellCount`, `OutletStem`, `OutletS`, `InletStart`, `InletCount`, bounds (4 x int cells). **Inlet** (16 B): `Stem`, `S`, `AroundDm`, `AcrossDm`; direction is the stem tangent at `S`.
- **Lake cells** (stored, 12 B per run): `Row` (u16), `Col` (u16), `Length` (u16), pad, `LakeId` (int32), sorted by row then column, with `RowStart` (int32[H + 1]). Lake lookup by cell = binary search in the row.
- **Pieces** (derived at load, 8 B): `Len` (5-point Gauss-Legendre arc length of the quadratic piece, S7) and `S0` (stem arc length at its start). Same code, same doubles, so bake and load agree.
- **Bitsets** (derived at load, 1 bit per cell, 1.25 MB each): `Flooded`; `Ring` (not flooded, 8-adjacent to a flooded cell; barrier cells included); `Near` (some piece within 650 m of some point of the cell, or the cell is Flooded, Ring, or 8-adjacent to Ring). `Near == 0` is the operator's early exit.
- **Buckets** (derived at load): 4 x 4 km, `ceil(W / 4) x ceil(H / 4)` (7,854 x 80), CSR `int32` offsets and piece ids. A piece is listed in every bucket that its control-point box, expanded by the reach radius **650 m** (300 m fade + 350 m lateral cap), overlaps. `ReachesNear(around, across)` returns the bucket's span.
- **Stable ids** are array indices: stems sorted by (last node cell, head cell); reaches by (stem, `SStart`); lakes by lowest flooded cell; pieces by (stem, k). Independent of worker count.
- **File** (`RiverGraphFormat`, T2R-6): magic `RRGF`, `GraphFormat` (int32), `DrainageAlgorithm` (int32), input identity (u64, S12), `Source` (byte: 0 CPU, 1 GPU; not part of the identity), payload length (i64), content hash (u64, FNV-1a 64 over the payload bytes; this is the **graph identity hash**). Payload: nodes, stems, reaches, knots, lakes, inlets, runs, each prefixed by its count, little-endian.
- **Bytes** (estimate from T0's 170,000 nodes at 100 km^2, +/- 40 % because the lowest-sample grid should give longer rivers): stored about 10 MB (nodes 1.5, stems 1.1, reaches 2.2, knots at 3 per km 4.2, lakes and runs 1.0); resident adds bitsets 3.75, bucket offsets 2.5, bucket entries about 2.4, pieces 1.5, so about **20 MB** against the 32 MB gate. First saving if needed: knots as int16 centimetre deltas **(tune at review)**.
- Symbols: `RiverGraph.cs`, `RiverSpatialIndex.cs`, `RiverLake.cs`, `RiverGraphFormat.cs` (T2R-6); `RiverGraphBuilder.cs` (T2R-5).
- **Checked by** `drainage_graph_test` (T2R-6 part): the index against brute force at 10,000 points (every piece within 650 m is listed; `Near == 0` implies none); byte-equal round trip; identity hash stable over two bakes; the bake report prints bytes by part.

### S6. Hydraulics and recipe scaling

**Decision.** Plan 3.2 relations with the T0 constants, a runoff scale of 20x and two floors. Pure statics in `RiverHydraulics`; the bake stores Q and S, everything else is derived.
- Runoff per cell: `r = A_cell(km^2) * max(0.1, m / 0.5)`, m the cell-centre moisture (T0 rule). Accumulated in T2R-4 as R.
- `Q = q_s * R(last cell of the reach)`, **q_s = 0.40 m^3/s per km^2** (20 x the T0 0.02). Chosen so a threshold river (100 km^2, m 0.5, Q 40 m^3/s) at the T0 median gradient 0.016 has d about 1.2 m and w about 8.5 m before the floors **(tune at review; T3-3 recalibrates)**.
- `S = max((F(c_mid) - F(c_5km)) / pathlen, 1e-4)`: the T0 `S_f` on the unperturbed filled surface F (no routing noise; flooded cells at their raw level), over 5 km downstream along the flow path from the reach's middle cell. `DrainageField` (T2R-4) must therefore expose F, R, the per-cell runoff r and the down array. Taken from the grid, not from the level profile, so hydraulics exist before the centreline and the level (S7 needs w for meander and bank lines).
- `d = max(1.2, channel_depth * 0.27 * Q^0.4)`.
- `n = 0.035 / river_ferocity`; `U_M = d^(2/3) * S^(1/2) / n`; `U_0 = min(U_M, 10 * river_ferocity)`.
- `w = max(8, Q / (U_0 * d))`; then `U = Q / (w * d)`, so `Q = U * w * d` holds exactly and `U <= U_0`.
- Interim class from the centre pool speed `1.5 U`: calm < 1.5, swift < 4, fierce < 7, torrent otherwise (T0 bands). Confinement is not modelled (T3-3).
- Geometry used by S7: half width `a = w / 2`; bank width `b = clamp(0.25 w, 2, 12)`; edge depth `d_e = min(0.3, 0.25 d)`; freeboard `f = 0.4`; level incision `0.6`; floodplain width beyond the bank `F = vw * clamp(2 w * sqrt(0.002 / S), 10, 120)`, then `F <= 220 - a - b`; valley side slope `tan_v = clamp(0.3 + 10 S, 0.3, 2.0) / vw`; meander amplitude `A_m = min(2 w * ms * (1 - smoothstep(0.005, 0.03, S)), 100) * clamp((w - 8) / 24, 0.25, 1)` (tuned in T2R-5c: small streams get a quarter of the amplitude), wavelength `lambda = max(12 w, 300)` (tuned in T2R-5c: floor was 60); wander amplitude `A_w = 250 * ms * (1 - 0.7 * smoothstep(0.001, 0.02, S))`. All **(tune at review)**.
- Continuity along a stem: a, b, d, d_e, F, tan_v and A_m blend from the upstream reach's value over the first 200 m of a reach: `x(s) = lerp(x_up, x, smoothstep(SStart, SStart + 200, s))`. The first reach of a stem does not blend. Q and the class do not blend.
- Recipe fields: `river_density` sets the channel threshold `100 / river_density` km^2 of unweighted area; `lake_abundance` acts in the bake only, through the water balance (S8b); `river_valley_width` scales F and divides tan_v; `channel_depth` scales d above the 1.2 m floor; `meander_strength` scales A_m and A_w (0 gives a smooth, unwandering curve); `river_ferocity` scales n and the cap (fixed at 1).
- **Checked by** `drainage_graph_test` (T2R-5 part), relations only: `Q = U w d` within 1e-6 relative; d >= 1.2 and w >= 8; d non-decreasing in Q at fixed S; `U <= U_M` and `U <= cap`; at every confluence `R(confluence cell) = sum R(incoming last cells) + r(confluence cell)` within 1e-6 relative. The report prints p10/p50/p90 of Q, S, d, w, U and class shares by length.

### S7. River operator

**Centreline** (T2R-5).
- Node: the cell's lowest sample plus jitter `60 m * (h1, h2)`, h uniform in [-1, 1] from `hash(seed, cell)`. **Smoothing and valley-floor snap** (tuned in T2R-5c, after the hairpin easing and before the wander): on reaches under slope 0.01 (faded in between 0.006 and 0.01) each interior node moves to a Gaussian-weighted average of the nodes within +-3 (sigma 1.5 nodes, window cut at the stem ends, which never move), which removes the old D8 45 and 90 degree runs; after the wander, a node whose own ground is more than 8 m above the lowest of its cell's 16 samples moves to the sample nearest to it among those within 2 m of that lowest and within 350 m of the eased node (barrier, sea and lake cells are left alone); every node ends within 350 m of its eased position (smoothing, wander and snap together). **Hairpin easing** (tuned in T2R-5b): an interior node where the node chain turns by more than 65 degrees moves towards the midpoint of its two neighbours by 0.75 of the distance, at most 250 m (one pass on the unmoved nodes; end nodes never move). It keeps sharp bends near the baseline; the lowest-sample nodes alone gave 0.20 % against 0.12 %. Then **wander** (baked into the stored nodes, a function of arc length evaluated at the nodes): interior node k moves along the node-chain normal `perp(P(k+1) - P(k-1))` by `A_w * taper * N(sigma_k)`, `N(sigma) = tanh(1.5 n) / tanh(1.5)`, `n = 0.45 sin(2 pi sigma / 7000 + phi1) + 0.35 sin(2 pi sigma / 4300 + phi2) + 0.20 sin(2 pi sigma / 2600 + phi3)` (tuned in T2R-5b: three incommensurate wavelengths so the wander is irregular, and a soft saturation that raises its typical size with `|N| <= 1`; was `0.7 sin(.. / 6000) + 0.3 sin(.. / 2500)`), phi from `hash(seed, stem)`, sigma the cumulative chord length of the jittered nodes, `taper = smoothstep(0, 1500, sigma) * smoothstep(0, 1500, sigma_end - sigma)`. End nodes never move.
- Curve: uniform quadratic B-spline. Piece k has Bezier controls `(M_k, P_k, M_k+1)` with `M_k = (P_k-1 + P_k) / 2`, `M_0 = P_0`, `M_n = P_n`. Closest point per piece: solve the cubic `(B(u) - p) . B'(u) = 0` in closed form, clamp u to [0, 1], also test both ends. Arc length `s(u) = S0_k + Len_k * u` (linear in u, continuous at joints).
- **Meander** (analytic): `D(s) = A_m(s) * E(theta) * sin(theta + 0.6 sin(theta / 2.7 + psi1) + 0.3 sin(theta / 4.6 + psi2)) * tau(s)` with `theta = MeanderPhase_r + 2 pi (s - SStart_r) / lambda_r` and the envelope `E = smoothstep(-0.7, 0.7, 0.6 sin(theta / 3.3 + psi3) + 0.4 sin(theta / 7.7 + psi4))`, psi from `hash(seed, stem)` (tuned in T2R-5b: amplitude and local wavelength, 0.78 to 1.4 lambda, vary along the river instead of a constant sine; was `A_m sin(theta) tau`), `tau = smoothstep(0, 2 lambda, s - s_stemStart)`, times `smoothstep(0, 2 lambda, s_stemEnd - s)` on tributary stems. Phases chain so D is continuous: `phase_r+1 = phase_r + 2 pi (SEnd_r - SStart_r) / lambda_r`. Lateral coordinate `t = (n - D(s)) / sqrt(1 + D'(s)^2)`, n the signed distance to the piece (left positive). Cap: `|wander| + |meander| <= 250 + 100 = 350 m` by construction.
- `RiverPathMetrics` scores the displaced centreline sampled every 25 m.

**Level** (`RiverLevel`, T2R-5).
- Stations every 50 m of s (25 m where the ground slope between stations exceeds 0.03) on the displaced centreline and both bank lines `t = +/-(a + b)`, rounded to integer metres, through `SamplePoints`. `g` = lowest of the three. Stems are processed tributaries first. `W_rm(s)` = running minimum of `g - 0.6` from the stem head; at a confluence the receiving stem's minimum also takes the incoming stem's end value.
- Lake outlet stems: `W = Level` up to the first station with `P <= 0` (S8), then the running minimum continues from `Level`. Terminal stems: `W = max(W, L_recv)`, `L_recv` = lake `Level`, 0 for the sea.
- Knots: greedy from the head; extend a segment while every station in it satisfies `W_rm - 0.75 <= segment <= W_rm`. The T2R-5 envelope check exempts stations where the stored profile equals an outlet hold or `L_recv` (these are reported as fill).
- Displayed level: `L(s) = min(floor(W(s)), min over integer crossings x_c in [s, s + 10] of (k_c - 1) + (x_c - s) / 10)`, where W crosses integer k_c at x_c. Whole-metre pools, chutes of 10 m, continuous, never above W. Several crossings within 10 m make a cascade.
- Tail (every terminal kind): `L(s) <- max(L_recv, L(s) - max(0, Delta - max(0, s_end - T - s) / 10))`, `Delta = max(0, L_pre(s_end - T) - L_recv)` with `L_pre` the displayed level before the tail (tuned in T2R-5b: the residual drop is tapered off the profile; the former `min(L(s), L_recv + max(0, s_end - T - s) / 10)` capped every stem at a 1:10 ramp from its mouth and cut up to 2,490 m into steep stems; the two agree where the profile is flat near the mouth), with `L_recv` = the receiving stem's `L(JunctionS)` and `T = a + b + F + 50` of the receiving reach for a confluence, the lake `Level` and `T = 50` for a lake, 0 and `T = 50` for the sea. Lung terminals have no tail.

**Section** (T2R-7), per piece within reach of the point, with G the natural height:
- Footprint: `a' = max(a, 0.75 fp)`, `b' = max(b, 0.5 fp)`, `d' = max(d * a / a', 0.05)`, `d_e' = min(d_e, 0.5 d')`. The level never changes. A piece contributes **valley only** (no core, no owner) when `fp > 256`, or when `fp > 64` and `w < fp / 16`.
- Core `|t| < a' + b'`: `C = L - d_e' - (d' - d_e') * (1 - (t / a')^2)` for `|t| < a'`, else `C = L - d_e' + (d_e' + f) * smoothstep(a', a' + b', |t|)`. Upper and lower bound are both C.
- Outside, `u = |t| - a' - b'`: levee (lower) `lo = L + f - 0.25 u`; valley (upper) `V = tan_v * max(0, u - F)`, `V' = lerp(V, Terrace(V, 40, 0.14), smoothstep(40, 60, V))` (the breaks terrace function, F4), `hi = L + f + V'`.
- Fade: `phi = 1 - smoothstep(R_c, 300, |t|)`, `R_c = min(a' + b' + F + 20, 250)`; `hi_f = hi + (1 - phi) * max(0, G - hi)`, `lo_f = lo - (1 - phi) * max(0, lo - G)`. Sea guard on every lower bound (bank, levee, plug): `lo <- G + smoothstep(-1, 0, G) * (lo - G)`, so nothing is built out into the sea.
- **Combination** over all pieces and the S8 plug: `HI = min hi_f`, `LO = max lo_f`, `H = min(HI, max(LO, G))`. The upper bound wins a conflict, so a channel always beats another reach's levee or a lake plug; overlapping cores give the deeper bed. This is the confluence rule.
- **Owner**: the core piece with the smallest `|t| / (a' + b')` (tie: lower stem id), only where `G >= 0`: River, its `L(s)`, reach, `t`. Otherwise a lake (S8). Otherwise None, `WaterLevel = NaN`. Wetness is decided by the sampler (S9).
- `RiverCorridorWeight = max(max_i (1 - smoothstep(a' + b' + 10, a' + b' + 60, |t_i|)), omega_lake)`. `RiverGroundDelta = H - G`.
- Signatures: `enum InlandWaterKind : byte { None, River, Lake }`; `readonly record struct RiverOperatorResult(double Height, InlandWaterKind Kind, double WaterLevel, int Reach, double ChannelOffset, double CorridorWeight, double GroundDelta)`; `RiverCarveOperator(RiverGraph graph)`; `RiverOperatorResult Evaluate(double around, double across, double naturalHeight, double footprintMeters)`; `double CorridorWeight(double around, double across, double footprintMeters)`. `Near == 0` returns `(G, None, NaN, -1, 0, 0, 0)` before any piece work.
- Model (TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs, :41, :109, :133): natural, then `Evaluate`, then `_sites.ShapeHeight` on the result. New `CalculateHeight(around, across, fp)` and `SampleDiagnostics(around, across, fp)`; the two-argument forms mean fp 0. `Diagnostics` (:11) appends, after `WeatherLungWeight`: `double WaterLevel = double.NaN, InlandWaterKind InlandWater = InlandWaterKind.None, int RiverReach = -1, double ChannelOffset = 0d, double RiverCorridorWeight = 0d, double RiverGroundDelta = 0d`.
- **Checked by** `river_carve_invariants_test` as T2R-7 lists. Its "levee bound off" control is read as **core lower bound off**: with the core as upper bound only, a fabricated dip below L in the bank zone becomes wet next to a dry sample below L, and containment must fail. (Containment comes from the core shape and the owner region; the levee is a slope bound.)
- **Containment as checked since the T3R-3 escalation (2026-11-12; T3.md Review notes, decision 3).** A wet-dry pair counts for containment when the dry sample lies outside every channel (limits unchanged: 0.05 m at 99.5 %, hard 0.5 m). Where the dry sample is a channel's own bank, the two levels must differ by at most 0.6 m (99.5 %, hard 1 m): the difference there is the fall of the water surface over 2 m, not a leak. T2R-8's lake checks use the same split.
- **Tuned in T2R-7 review (2026-11-11).** These replace the bullets above where they differ. Code: `RiverCarveOperator.cs`.
  - **Combination** (kept from the T2R-7 implementation): start from G; every valley bound clamps from above; the highest lower bound (bank, levee) then lifts, capped at `L_j + f + 2 * max(0, |t_j| - a'_j - b'_j)` by every other channel j; the lowest core caps the result. A levee therefore beats another reach's valley floor, and a core beats everything.
  - **Draw-down** (new; this is the confluence and neck rule): the pieces at a point are sorted by level. A piece i is drawn towards each lower piece j: within `a_j + b_j + 2.5 m` of j's centreline (plain widths, no footprint) i takes j's level; beyond that the level returns to its own with `smoothstep` over `clamp(10 * (L_i - L_j), 10, 120)` m. Lower pieces are resolved first, so the pull is transitive. Every point that two cores cover therefore has one level, and a higher channel meets a lower one through a chute, wet to wet. The rule reads the point and the plain geometry only, so the level is still the same at every footprint. "Owner = deepest core" was not adopted: it leaves the higher channel's water against the lower channel's bank. Known cost: along a path past a neck the level dips and returns (23 of 295,245 path pairs rise downstream).
  - **Owner**: unchanged, the core with the smallest `|t| / (a' + b')` (tie: lower stem, then lower piece); its `WaterLevel` is the drawn-down level.
  - **Valley bound**: `hi = L + f + rise * smoothstep(0, max(8, b'), u) + V'(side) + relief`.
    - `rise` = how far the stored profile W stands above the displayed pools and chutes (0 to 1 m, from `RiverLevel.AtStem(.., out storedAbove)`). The floodplain follows the smooth profile; the pools and chutes stay in the channel. This removes the 1 m steps across the valley floor.
    - `side = tan_v * x^2 / (x + e)`, `x = max(0, u - F)`, toe `e = clamp(1.5 w, 12, 90)`: slope 0 at the floodplain edge, `tan_v` far out. V' is the same terrace function.
    - `relief = r * 3 / (r + 3) * smoothstep(0, clamp(0.5 w, 10, 40), u)`, `r = 0.2 * max(0, G - hi)`: ground above the bound keeps a fifth of its height above it, saturating at 3 m, so the floor is not a flat shelf.
    - The clamp is a smooth minimum, `min(G, hi)` lowered by at most `k / 4` where the two are closer than `k = min(4, 0.1 u) * phi`: the line where the valley side meets natural ground is rounded.
    - Levee, fade, sea guard, terrace and footprint rules are unchanged. All four widths above scale with w (T3R-3 will triple the median width).
  - **Interfaces for T3**: `bool RiverCarveOperator.Locate(around, across, footprintMeters, out RiverLocation)` with `RiverLocation(Piece, Stem, Reach, S, T, TangentAround, TangentAcross, HalfWidth, BankWidth, Depth, EdgeDepth, Level)` (the tangent is the displaced centreline's, downstream; `Evaluate` uses the same pieces, draw-down and owner, and additionally requires `G >= 0`); `static double RiverCarveOperator.CoreProfile(absT, level, halfWidth, bankWidth, depth, edgeDepth)` is the bed and bank of the Core bullet; `RiverNetwork.ChannelAt(stem, s, out RiverChannel)` is the only reader of channel and valley values (`Geometry` and `Meander` go through it; `RiverNetwork.LateralReach(reach, limit)` bounds how far a piece can act).
  - **Test bars** (`river_carve_invariants_test`): cut over 40 m on at most 5.5 % of path length (was 3 %; the state accepted in the T2R-5 review until M2); "a coarser footprint is never deeper" holds at 99.5 % on reach sections and at 95 % on confluence sections, printed apart; the fabricated-dip control uses reach sections; the cost near rivers is a 3.0 us tripwire with a target of 1.0 us after T2R-7b (was 0.5).

### S8. Lake operator

**Decision.** A lake is a level plus its flooded cells; the shoreline is the fine terrain; a plug closes sub-lattice gaps on the dry ring only.
- **Level**: `Level = floor(raw spill level)` in whole metres (raw = the T2R-4 rule, over the neighbours water can flow to only: tuned in T2R-5c, see the Barrier cap). Lower is conservative, and river pools are whole metres, so inlets meet the lake exactly.
- **Indicator**: `J(c) = +1` if c is flooded, `0` if Ring, `-1` otherwise. `P(p)` is the bilinear interpolation of J between the four surrounding cell centres. Across the rim edges the stencil replicates the edge row. A stencil cannot hold +1 and -1 together, so `P = 0` exactly on the ring-centre lines.
- **Lake id**: the lake of the stencil's flooded corners. If corners of two lakes meet (only possible across a blocked diagonal), the lake with the larger bilinear share wins (tie: lower id).
- **Owner and wet test**: Lake where `P > 0` and no river core owns the point (S7). `WaterLevel = Level`. The sampler marks it wet where presentation height < `Level`. Islands stay dry.
- **Plug** (lower bound, joins LO in S7): `psi = 1 - smoothstep(0, p_b, |P|)`, **p_b = 0.25** of a cell (tune at review); `lo_plug = G + psi * max(0, Level + f - G)`. At `P = 0` the ground is at least `Level + 0.4`, so the wet region never reaches the ring line. The band lies inside Ring cells (p_b < 0.5), where all 16 samples are already at or above the level, so it acts only in gaps between samples. The plug does not depend on the footprint.
- **Outlet hold**: S7 (outlet stem level = `Level` until `P <= 0`). The outlet core is an upper bound, so it cuts through the plug at `Level` with banks at `Level + f`.
- **Inlets**: the S7 tail with `L_recv = Level`; the inlet point is the stem end and its direction the stem tangent there.
- **Barrier cap** (changed in T2R-5c): barrier cells, and diagonals blocked by a barrier, do not take part in the level rule (a low barrier cell gave the perched lake 272 a 917 m level against a 2,086 m spill, leaving 456 of 2,356 cells wet); the level is the lowest value over the neighbours routing can flow to. A lake beside a barrier can therefore stand above the barrier ground; the plug-band checks below keep that away from sites. The operator never queries sites. The bake instead checks that no plug band (`P >= -p_b`) and no reach corridor (650 m) reaches a site influence disc, the Wall Engine stadium or the Weather Lung footprint, and fails with the lake or reach id.
- **Rim walls**: a lake may touch row 0 or row 317. The replicated edge row keeps `P > 0` out to the rim, there is no plug along the rim edge, and the rim wall contains the water **(tune at review: if the T2R-8 picture shows a water wall, rows 0 and 317 become non-floodable in T2R-4)**.
- **Corridor weight**: `omega_lake = 1` for `P >= -p_b`, `1 - smoothstep(p_b, 2 p_b, -P)` below. This is 1 on the water, the low shore and the dry ring, and continuous to 0.
- Symbols: in `RiverCarveOperator.cs` (or `RiverLakeOperator.cs` called from it).
- **Checked by** `river_carve_invariants_test` lake part (T2R-8). The report prints the plug count, the p50, p99 and maximum plug height, and the ring share over 5 m.

### S8b. Water-balance lakes

Written on 2026-11-11 for T2R-6b (user decision in "T2R-6 review and user lake decisions"), against the T2R-6 working tree. Evidence for the change: `.godot/audit_baseline/DESKTOP-RAVA9C1/t2r/bake/20261111_202640_t2r6b/graph_report.txt` (16.5 % of land flooded, 68 % of lakes without an outlet, depth p99 479 m, maximum 2,070 m, an 8,203 km^2 rim basin filled 1.5 km deep). This section replaces the `lake_abundance` sentences of D3 and S6, and S8's level rule for lakes that do not reach their spill. Everything else in S8 (indicator, plug, ring, corridor weight, rim walls) applies unchanged.

**Decision.** Every conditioned depression (a final flood component at least 2 m deep; T2R-4 calls it a lake, this section calls it a **basin**) holds water at a steady water balance: inflow equals evaporation from the wet cells. If its inflow exceeds the evaporation of its full surface, the basin **spills**: it is today's lake (spill level, every component cell, the outlet river) and passes the surplus downstream. Otherwise it is **closed**: it holds one or more **balance lakes** below its spill, the rest of the basin is **dry floor**, and nothing leaves it. The bake makes one pass over the basins in the accumulation's topological order, and one bottom-up pass over each basin's merge tree. There is no time stepping and no iteration.

- **Units.** Everything is in S6's runoff units (km^2 at moisture 0.5; discharge = q_s x value). Runoff per cell is `r(c) = A_cell * max(0.1, m / 0.5)`, unchanged. Evaporation of a wet cell is `e(c) = A_cell * (E0 / lambda) * clamp(1 - m(c), 0.05, 1)`, with m the cell-centre moisture. Rain on a wet cell is part of the inflow (its r), so e is gross evaporation and nothing is counted twice. Temperature is not used, because the grid does not carry it **(tune at review)**.
- **E0 = 20** (calibrated in T2R-6b from 10; whole-ring default share 9.65 %, see the T2R-6b sweep) **(tune at review)**, a constant in `DrainageLakeBalance` covered by `DrainageAlgorithm`. The ratios below are for E0 = 10; double them. A closed lake then needs a catchment of `E0 (1 - m) / max(0.1, 2 m)` times its own area: 45 at m 0.1, 20 at m 0.2, 5 at m 0.5, 1.25 at m 0.8. These are Earth-like ratios: arid closed lakes about 20-50, humid basins spill.
- **`lake_abundance`** (0-1, default 0.5; the range and default do not change): `lambda = 4^(2 la - 1)`. That gives 0 → 0.25 (evaporation x 4, mostly dry floors), 0.5 → 1, and 1 → 4 (evaporation / 4, most basins spill, close to today's map). Breaching no longer reads the field: `DrainageConditioning.BreachShare = 0.5` keeps today's default behaviour (the cheapest half of the feasible breaches) **(tune at review)**. `DrainageBakeOptions.LakeAbundance` keeps its name and now sets lambda. A new `EvaporationScale` (default 1) is used only by the report tool's sweep.
- **Target share**: wet cells **8 % of land cells** at the default, in a band of 6-10 % **(tune at review)**. This is not measured yet. Rough estimate: about 40 % of land drains into basins, divided by a catchment-to-lake ratio of about 5 at mean moisture, gives about 8 %. Calibration in T2R-6b: the report's sweep sets E0 to the value from {6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20} whose default share is nearest 8 %. If none lands in 6-10 %, that is an escalation.

**Order** (`DrainageRouting.Route`, accumulation stage). Directions and the crossing repair do not change. Accumulation runs the flood order in reverse, with each basin k as one super-node:
1. A basin cell does not pass its accumulation along `down`; the basin collects it. A cell outside basin k whose `down` is a basin-k cell is a **donor**. Its rank is above the basin's lowest rank, so every donor has been counted before the basin's **first cell** `f_k` (the basin cell earliest in the flood order) comes up.
2. At `f_k` the basin is resolved (below). Its outflow `O_k` (0 when closed) is added to `accumulated[s_k]`, where `s_k = FloodParent[f_k]`. That cell lies outside the basin and earlier in the flood order, so the order stays topological. Area: a spilling basin passes its whole contributing area to `s_k`, which keeps today's outlet network. A closed basin passes none, so channels below it lose its catchment. The routing's area conservation check counts the area that closed basins hold.
3. Inside a closed basin, `down`, `AreaCells` and R are rewritten from the basin's descent (below). Inside a spilling basin they stay as today, but the outflow is reduced by the full lake's evaporation.
So basins are resolved upstream first. An upstream basin at balance sends nothing to the basins below it. One that spills sends its surplus, which may make the next one spill (chains).

**Resolving one basin** (`DrainageLakeBalance.Resolve`). Inputs: the cells of basin k, h = `Effective`, and Z = the basin's raw spill level (today's `DrainageLake.Level`).
- **Key** `(h(c), c)`, a total order. **Descent**: `BasinDown(c)` is the 8-neighbour in the same basin with a smaller key and the steepest drop `(h(c) - h(n)) / step` (tie: lower index), or -1 if there is none; such a cell is a **pit**. Neighbours come from `DrainageDomain.GetNeighbors`, so barriers and blocked diagonals are excluded.
- **Merge tree** of the cells with `h < Z`, taken in key order, with union-find:
  - A cell with no processed neighbour in the basin starts a **leaf**.
  - A cell whose processed neighbours lie in two or more sets merges them pairwise, in ascending node id. Each pair becomes a binary **merge node** with saddle `z = h(cell)`.
  - Otherwise the cell joins its neighbour's set.
  Each cell is an **own cell** of the node that is current when it joins, so a node's own cells are contiguous in key order. Node ids follow creation order, which is ascending saddle. `top(N)` is the parent's saddle, or Z for a root. The tree is a forest when cells at or above Z separate parts of the basin.
- **Inflow to the leaves**: take the basin cells in descending key order. `acc(c) = r(c) + donor inflow(c) + the acc of every basin cell whose BasinDown is c`, passed on to `BasinDown(c)`. At a pit it becomes the leaf's inflow D. A pit at or above Z (outside the forest) passes its inflow to the root that holds the basin's lowest cell; the report counts these. `AreaCells` accumulates the same way.
- **Capacity**: `E_N(L)`, for L in `[z_N, top(N)]`, is the sum of e over the cells of N's subtree with `h < L`. That equals the sum of e over the children's subtrees plus the prefix of N's own cells below L.
- **Fill and spill**: one pass over the nodes in creation order. A **pool** is a node whose subtree holds one body of water; every leaf starts as a pool with `I = D`. At merge node N with children A and B, A is **full** if it is a pool with `I_A >= E_A(z_N)`:
  - Both full: N becomes a pool with `I_N = I_A + I_B`.
  - Only A full: A is pinned at `z_N` with `I_A := E_A(z_N)`. The surplus `I_A - E_A(z_N)` goes to the pool in B that holds the leaf reached by descent from the saddle cell's lowest-key neighbour in B's subtree. Adding water to a pool P re-checks P against `top(P)`. If P is now full and its sibling is full, their parent becomes a pool holding the sum of both, and that pool is re-checked in turn. If the sibling is not full, the surplus moves on the same way. Each step moves up the tree, or across to a sibling below `z_N`, so the process ends. If B ends up full, N becomes a pool as in the first case.
  - Neither full: N holds no pool, and its own cells are dry.
- **Spill**: after the last node, the basin spills if any root pool has `I > E(Z)`. A spilling basin becomes today's lake: one lake at level `floor(Z)`, with every basin cell and an outlet per S8, and `O_k = max(0, I_total - sum of e over the cells with h < floor(Z))`. A spilling basin's evaporation is `I_total - O_k`. Otherwise the basin is closed and `O_k = 0`. If a basin has several roots and one of them spills, the whole basin floods, as today; the report counts these cases.
- **Balance level** of each remaining pool P (closed basins only). Take P's own cells `c_0..c_n` in key order and the cumulative `E_j = E_base + sum over i <= j of e(c_i)`, where `E_base` is the sum over the children's subtrees. Find the first j with `E_j >= I_P`. The raw level interpolates between cell floors: `L* = h(c_j) + (I_P - E_(j-1)) / e(c_j) * (h_next - h(c_j))`, where `h_next` is `h(c_(j+1))`, or `top(P)` after the last own cell. A pinned pool has `L* = top(P)`.
  Then **Level** is the whole metre nearest `L*`, clamped to `[floor(h_min) + 1, floor(top(P))]`, where h_min is the pool's lowest cell. The upper clamp keeps every dry neighbour of the wet set at or above the level, because cells outside the subtree have `h >= top`.
  A leaf with `D < 0.5 e(pit)`, or a pool whose clamp interval is empty, holds no lake. Its water evaporates on a **dry pan**; these are counted.
- **Wet cells** of a balance lake: the cells of P's subtree with `h < Level`. They are the lake's flooded set (J = +1 in S8). Every 8-neighbour that is not wet has `h >= Level`, so all 16 of its samples are at or above the level. Wet cells of two different lakes are never 8-adjacent. S8's indicator, plug, ring and corridor weight therefore apply unchanged. If a pool's rounded level lies below its own saddle, its children's wet cells may form separate patches under one lake id. They share one level; the report counts these.
- **Dry floor**: every cell of a closed basin that is not wet.
- **Filled surface F** (S6): balance-lake cells take `max(Level, h)`, dry floor takes `h`, and spilling lakes are as today.
- **Routing inside a closed basin**: `down = BasinDown`, set after the crossing repair. Crossings among these links descend in h, so they are only counted. A pit gets `down = -1`, and its terminal is a new `DrainageCellKind.Basin` value. Basin is a terminal value only, never a cell's kind. With it, cells upstream of a closed basin are not taken for enclosed cells (`Barrier`) and can still be channels.

**Stems and terminals.** The channel rule (T2R-5) already excludes only cells with `LakeId >= 0`. `LakeId` now marks wet cells only, so channels continue across the dry floor along `BasinDown`; no builder change is expected (verify). A stem ends at its last dry cell before one of these:
- a wet cell: Terminal Lake, TerminalId = the balance lake, and the end node at that wet cell's lowest sample plus jitter, as today;
- a dry pan's pit: Terminal Dead, with no tail, as the level code already treats Dead.

So the terminal moves from the old spill-level edge to the new shoreline. The S7 tail uses `L_recv = Level` and T = 50, as today. Balance lakes have no outlet (`OutletStem = -1`), because no closed-basin cell drains out. `UpstreamLake` and the outlet hold do not change for spilling lakes. `drainage_graph_test`'s check "every channel ends in the sea, a lake or the Lung" adds "or a dry pan".

**Records and file** (`GraphFormat` 3, `DrainageAlgorithm` 2; `CarveOperator` unchanged).
- **`DrainageLake`** (bake) appends `RawLevel` (float), `Basin` (int), `Closed` (bool), and `Inflow` and `Evaporation` (double, runoff units).
  - For a balance lake, `Level` is the whole-metre level and `RawLevel` is `L*`. `RiverGraph.Build`'s `floor(Level)` is then a no-op, and it reads `RawLevel` from the record.
  - For a spilling lake, both are the spill level, as today.
- **`DrainageField`**:
  - `LakeId` and `Lakes` are the final lakes, with ids by lowest wet cell as in S5.
  - New: `BasinId` (int per cell, the conditioned component), `Basins` and `IsDryFloor(cell)`.
  - `IsLakeWater` does not change.
- **`RiverLake`** (stored; 56 → 64 B) appends `Basin` (int, -1 for a spilling lake) and `Inflow` (float).
- **`RiverBasin`** (new, stored, 16 B; closed basins only, with ids by lowest basin cell):
  - `SpillLevel` (float, `floor(Z)`);
  - `FloorLevel` (float: the lowest Level among its lakes, or its lowest cell height for a dry pan);
  - `DryCellCount` and `LakeCount` (int32).
- **Dry runs** (new, stored, 12 B): the `LakeRun` layout with `LakeId` read as the basin id, sorted by row then column, with a derived `DryRowStart` (int32[H + 1]). The query `RiverGraph.DryBasinAt(col, row)` (and `(around, across)`) returns the basin id or -1, by binary search in the row as `LakeAt` does. There is no bitset, to save memory.
- **Payload order**: nodes, stems, reaches, knots, lakes, inlets, runs, basins, dry runs. The reader checks basin ids, run order and run bounds. A v2 file is rejected and rebaked.
- **Bytes**: an estimated +1 to +2.5 MB stored and resident (basins about 0.5, dry runs about 2, lake records about 0.4, minus fewer lake runs), against 29.3 MiB today. T2R-6's 32 MB gate applies. First saving if over: leave `Inflow` out of the resident record and pack dry runs to 8 B (u16 basin id when the basin count fits).

### S10. Proxy routing envelope and footprints

### S11. Far coverage hooks

**U4 answer (read from source).** Far colour travels two ways. (a) Vertex colours of the far viewport meshes (RingScaledFarViewport.Geometry.cs:439-441) and the global LOD (RingTerrainGlobalLod.cs:993-994), read by `ring_terrain_global.gdshader` at :116-117, which uses `COLOR.rgb` only; `COLOR.a` is unused. (b) The surface tiles: `surface_tile_color` and `surface_tile_normal` are RGBA8 with alpha written as 255 (`BuildSurfaceTilePixels`, RingScaledFarViewport.SurfaceTiles.cs:470), uploaded with `GenerateMipmaps` (:352), and mixed at :116-124 by `surface_tile_blend`. Proxy sectors use `ring_terrain_proxy.gdshader`, whose `COLOR` holds four biome weights, so proxies show rivers through the wet strip of S7 and S10, not through coverage.
- **Amended 2026-11-12 (user look decision; T2R-12b carries it out).** Coverage is the true area fraction. The minimum `c_min = 0.2` and the shader mip floor of the first version are removed: both made a river wider than it is. What a floor would have guarded against does not occur: a vertex cell and a tile texel are boxes that tile the surface, so every piece of channel falls in exactly one of them, and box-filtered mips of an area fraction are again area fractions. The one remaining loss is the 8-bit quantum of the far vertex and tile alpha: coverage under about 0.2 % rounds to 0, which is an invisible tint and a glint under a tenth of the lit ground. It is accepted. If a far river shows as dashes in the `--far-only` fly-out, the allowed fix is a floor of one quantum (1/254) for a crossed cell, nothing larger.
- `RiverGraph.LineCoverage(aroundMin, aroundMax, acrossMin, acrossMax)`: `c = min(1, sum over pieces of len(piece inside rect) * w / area)`, using the base curve with each piece sampled at 8 points. No minimum. For a far vertex the rectangle is its spacing cell; for a tile texel it is the texel footprint.
- Encoding, feature worlds only: vertex colour `alpha = 1 - c` in `TerrainColors` (global LOD and far viewport geometry); tile `alpha = 255 - round(254 * c)`. With c = 0 both stay 1 or 255, which is generation 1's value.
- Shader (`ring_terrain_global.gdshader`): `uniform bool river_line_enabled = false`, set by the owners when the sampler has a river graph. `c = max(1 - COLOR.a, surface_tile_enabled ? (1 - tile.a) * surface_tile_blend : 0)`, used as it is. Albedo `mix(albedo, river_far_color, c)` before haze. **Glint**: `ROUGHNESS = mix(surface_roughness, 0.06, c)`, `SPECULAR = mix(surface_specular, 0.5, c)`, and a sun lobe added as emission, `pow(n.h, 140) * (140 + 8) / 8 * 0.3 * c`, times the day factor, the cloud-shadow visibility and the haze transmittance (the shader owns its lighting, so the built-in specular is not used). The glint therefore scales with the real coverage: at full coverage its peak is roughly 10 to 30 times the lit ground (an estimate; calibrate at the T2R-16 review), a plausible sun glint on rippled water at an oblique view; a 30 m river on a 512 m cell gets 6 % of that. **Whitewater**: `uniform float river_whitewater = 0.0` mixes towards foam white by `c * river_whitewater`. ### S11b. Proxy coverage (T2R-12b)

**Why here.** The proxy levels draw the ground out to 6,500 to 8,000 km; the S11 tier is only seen beyond that and before the proxies have streamed in. Without coverage on the proxies a river of any width has no water signal from 110 km (levels of 512 m and coarser are valley only), so a wide river vanishes and no glint is possible.

- **Rule.** A level shows a river either as water geometry (the operator gives it an owner, S7) or as coverage, never both and never neither: a channel segment adds coverage exactly when `RiverCarveOperator.ValleyOnly(footprint of the level, half width)` is true. Today that is every river at 512 m and coarser, and rivers narrower than 16 m at 256 m. `RiverFootprint.UsesProxyLine(spacing) = spacing >= 256`.
- **Value.** `c(v) = min(1, sum over samples of area * bilinear weight of v / spacing^2)`: each segment is cut into steps of at most half a spacing along and across its width, and each sample's water area goes to its four surrounding lattice vertices. The weights of a sample sum to 1, so the lattice holds exactly the channel area: no minimum is needed for continuity, and none is applied. On a straight river through a vertex row `c = w / spacing`; a river wider than a cell fills a band of its own width.
- **Geometry.** `RiverGraph.SplatLineArea` (signature in T2R-12b). Eight segments per piece and the displaced centreline (base curve plus `RiverNetwork.Meander` along the operator's normal) up to 1,024 m spacing, so the coverage lies in the carved valley; one segment per piece on the base curve above (a piece is about 1 km, the displacement at most 350 m). A vertex's value depends only on its position and the spacing, so sector edges match bit for bit.
- **Carrier.** `UV2.y` of terrain lattice vertices, `0.49 * c` (0 today; water and skirt vertices hold 1; all readers threshold at 0.5). Mesh-installed levels only (64 m and coarser); a float, so no quantum. It is a render-time payload: the sample cache and its version are untouched.
- **Shader** (`ring_terrain_proxy.gdshader`, behind `river_line_enabled`, set per level when the sampler has a river graph and `UsesProxyLine`): decode in the vertex stage (`UV2.y < 0.5 ? UV2.y / 0.49 : 0`) into a varying; tint, roughness, specular and the emission glint as in S11 with the plain `c`; the glint normal is the local zenith, since river water is level and the coarse ground normal is not. `river_far_color` is the same uniform as in S11; T2R-13's river colour setting must drive both materials.
- **Interpolation.** Triangle interpolation of a lattice value dips between vertices on a river that runs against the quad diagonal (to about a quarter of the peak in the worst case). That is a reconstruction effect, not lost area. If it shows as dots on a river with coverage above 0.2, the fix is a wider splat kernel (the quadratic B-spline over 3 x 3 vertices, weights still summing to 1), not a minimum.
- **Not included.** No whitewater on the proxies (no room in the payload; `CUSTOM1` as RGBA8, 4 bytes per vertex on river sectors, is the route if T3-9 needs it). No morph of the coverage towards the parent level: area is the same at every level, only the blur width doubles at a handoff.
- **Cost.** No memory. One splat per river-touching sector of the 256 m and coarser levels: one index query and about 0.4 million segment evaluations over a whole load (estimate from 265,000 pieces on the ring, F29), under 0.1 s of worker time; the budget in T2R-12b is 0.2 s.
- **Checked by** `river_far_water_fraction_test` (conservation, seam equality, the owner rule, a wide river) and the glint poses of `river_lod_consistency_gpu_test`.

### S12. Lifecycle, climate inputs and device ownership

- **Pre-graph identity** `HydrologyBakeIdentity.Compute(parameters, sites)` mixes the tag `hydrology-input-v1`, `NaturalSiteIdentity` (TerrainGenerationParameters.cs:278; it excludes extension fields, so T7's relief fields stay out), the 20 world climate values, the six river fields, `sites.Registry.Identity`, `sites.Climate?.Identity`, `sites.WeatherLung?.Identity`, the bits of `CircumferenceM` and `WidthM`, W, H and `HydrologyVersions.DrainageAlgorithm`. It finds and validates the file. The runtime definition hash appends the graph identity hash (S5) after the T2R-2 terms.

In flight: one entry per identity holding the task, a CancellationTokenSource and a waiter count. A cancelled waiter decrements the count; at 0 the build is cancelled; completion removes only the same entry.
- **File**: `<world>/hydrology/river_graph_v<GraphFormat>.bin`, where world = `ringworld/runtime/active_world_directory`, globalised on the main thread. One copy per worker. `drainage_grid_test` checks it bit-equal to a runtime sampler's `Moisture` on 1,000 points. If that fails, the fallback is an internal `bindRivers: false` runtime sampler used only for moisture.
- **GPU device**. Branch A (U2 works): the bake task calls `RenderingServer.CreateLocalRenderingDevice()` on its worker, owns it for the grid stage and the level point lists, and frees it in `finally`. Branch B (U2 fails): the GPU source queues dispatches; the bridge's `_Process` runs them on the main RenderingDevice in slices of at most 2 ms per frame, and the worker waits on each slice. - **Loader**: new `prepare_hydrology() -> bool` in ring_world_loading.gd, called by world_size_launcher.gd right after `loader.prepare_world(game, self)` (:1237) and before the `.32` stage (:1238), in both the interactive and the background path. An error calls `fail(error)`; cancellation or a stale `_generation` calls `CancelHydrology()`, which is also called from `CancelWorld`. Generation 1 returns at once with `required == false`, so its stage fractions are unchanged. The diagnostics launch (:1259) bakes synchronously before attaching, feature worlds only.
- Bake progress weights: grid .25, barriers .05, conditioning .30, routing .20, graph .17, pack .03 **(tune at review from the benchmark)**.

### T2R-10 lead review (2026-11-12)

Reviewed by the temporary lead (Fable) before the commit. Two faults were reported in the first pictures of a river world: all distant terrain and all inland water flat white, and straight black lines across land and lake.

**One cause for both, fixed in the review.** The far-terrain systems never started in a river world, so the whole-ring fallback mesh stayed on screen.
- `_configure_game` builds the grid's sampler before the river graph exists. The build fails with `RiverGraphNotBakedException` and `VoxelChunkGridManager.RebuildTerrainNoises` leaves `_terrainSampler` null (the T2R-10 catch; before it, T2R-9 let the same exception escape with the same result).
- `TerrainSampler` was a plain field read. `RingTerrainGlobalLod`, `RingScaledFarViewport`, `RingTerrainMinimap`, `RingSunshineCloudBridge`, `RingLocalVolumetricCloudRenderer` and `RingLocalWeatherEffects` each test `_grid?.TerrainSampler == null` in `_Ready` and switch themselves off. The log of the old run shows it: "RingTerrainGlobalLod could not resolve the shared sampler or focus" and five more lines of the same kind. Other code paths built the sampler later through `EnsureTerrainSampler`, so the near terrain, the proxies and swimming worked and the tests passed.
- The far viewport is what hides the legacy `AnalyticGlobalRing` (`RingScaledFarViewport.cs`, `analytic.Visible = false`). With the far viewport off, that coarse fallback surface stayed visible: it is the white surface seen in place of distant terrain and over the water (it cuts through the real terrain; land above it shows, everything below it is hidden), and its structural seam lines are the black lines. They are not river or lake geometry and not drowned channels. A fixed build shows the same line lattice only for the first half second after a cold arrival, before the terrain streams in.
- The pictures had no minimap and no local clouds for the same reason.

**Fix (about 20 lines).** `RiverGraphNotBakedException` carries the identity of the missing graph. The grid keeps it, and the `TerrainSampler` property builds the sampler on the first read after `RiverGraphStore` holds that graph. Generation 1 never sets the identity, so its path is unchanged. Files: `scripts/Voxel/Phase2/VoxelChunkGridManager.cs`, `scripts/Voxel/Phase3/RingTerrainSampler.SurfaceGeneration.cs`. `inland_swim_smoke_gpu_test` now fails when the far viewport or the minimap switched itself off at attach.

**The straight river in the first picture is real.** That reach is straight for about 400 m looking down the ring; looking the other way from the same point it meanders (`river_meander_after.png`). The near terrain follows the meandering centreline.

**Tests.** `rivers/20261112-133415` and `rivers/20261112-133926` (after the last rebuild), 5 of 5 pass both times: `inland_water_sampler_test`, `inland_swim_smoke_gpu_test`, `river_graph_cache_test`, `world_generation_fingerprint_test`, `terrain_recipe_freeze_test`.

**Pictures** (`.godot/captures/t2r10/`): `river_channel_banks_after.png`, `lake_from_above_after.png` (the two reported views), `river_meander_after.png`, `lake_open_water_after.png`; evidence for the cause: `lake_from_above_before_repro.png` (the fix switched off: white surface and lines, 8 s after arrival), `lake_cold_arrival_before_repro.png` (the same before any terrain has streamed in: the fallback alone) and `lake_cold_arrival_after.png` (fixed build, night, 150 ms after a cold arrival: the same lattice before the terrain covers it).

### T2R-12 lead review and user look decision (2026-11-12)

Reviewed by the temporary lead (Fable) on the implementer's escalation. Result: work order T2R-12b, Spec S11 amended, Spec S11b added, S10 amended.

**Finding, confirmed in the source.** The far line of T2R-12 lives in `ring_terrain_global`, which the player almost never sees. The proxy levels (15 levels, 2 m to 32,768 m spacing) fade out at 6,500 to 8,000 km and cover the screen in the whole fly-out. Levels of 512 m and coarser sample at `RiverFootprint.Far`, so they have the valley and no water. Sheet `.godot/captures/landscape/t2r/lod_flyout/sheet_rivers_final.png`: blue river to frame 15, the widened strip to frame 58, nothing from frame 59 (95 km).

**User look decision (2026-11-12): realistic, not map-like.**
- A distant river is as visible as it would be to a real observer: area-weighted, so it dims with distance. No floored line.
- Sun glint and the valley shading are what make a thin river catch the eye from height. Glint strength follows real coverage.
- Big rivers and lakes stay visible as their real size allows.

**What changes because of it.**
- The 0.2 minimum of S11 and the shader mip floor are removed (T2R-12b item 1). They existed for readability only.
- **Where a floor is still needed against beading: nowhere.** Box cells (far vertices, tile texels) and the bilinear splat (proxies) both conserve channel area, so a river cannot fall between samples, and averaged mips stay area fractions. Two effects remain and neither is fixed with a floor: the 8-bit quantum of the far tier's alpha (under 0.2 % coverage rounds to 0; accepted, with a one-quantum floor as the only allowed fallback), and the dip of triangle interpolation on diagonal rivers (handled by the splat kernel, S11b).
- The fly-out is no longer expected to show a line at every step. Above about 100 km a 30 m river is a 3 to 6 % tint and shows mainly in the sun's mirror zone. The test gets a glint pose instead of a "line present" share.

**Is proxy coverage still worth its cost? Yes, as a smaller order.**
- Without it a river of any width has no water signal from 110 km. A 1 km river (3 to 6 px wide at 110 to 239 km) would disappear, against the third point of the decision, and no glint can be drawn because the shader does not know where water is.
- Glint-only is not lighter: it needs the same per-vertex coverage. A point-sampled owner or corridor weight in the existing samples would alias on rivers narrower than a cell and would need the floor the user rejected.
- The chosen form is the light one: area fraction folded into an existing channel.

| | `UV2.y` payload (chosen) | New `CUSTOM1` stream | Ring-wide coverage texture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memory | 0 (terrain vertices store 0 there today) | 4 bytes x 9,474 vertices = 38 KB per river sector, about 10 to 25 MB over the 256 m and coarser levels | 512 m texels: 61,359 x 621 = 38 MB as R8, 51 MB with mips, and wider than the 16,384 texture limit; 256 m texels: 152 MB |
| Build cost | One splat per river sector, under 0.1 s of worker time per load (estimate; counter in T2R-12b) | Same | A raster of about 2.1 million segments, mips over 38 million texels and a 38 to 51 MB upload at load (estimate 0.2 to 0.4 s plus a sliced upload), or a new cache file |
| Cache and formats | None: render-time payload, no version change | Mesh format differs per sector | New resource, ring UV precision and seam, layer seams |
| Look | True area, blurred over one cell (1.2 to 4.7 px) | Same | Same at 512 m; sharper beyond, which TAA and FSR erase under about 2 px anyway (vastness plan 1.1) |

`CUSTOM1` remains the route if T3-9 needs whitewater on the proxies. The texture was the alternative for a floored, sharp line; under the area-weighted look it buys nothing.

### User play-test feedback (2026-11-12, new rivers world, working tree with uncommitted T2R-12b and T2R-13)

1. **Performance:** 20 to 30 fps with bad frame times. Lead code reading has started; A/B measurement waits until the PC is free.

### Rivers perf investigation (lead, 2026-11-12)

Measured on seed 876374626 at the user's spot (ARC 23410.5 km, ACROSS 192.4 km, 600 m up): 2560x1440 native, Very High, RTX 4070, debug build. Raw numbers are in `.godot/perf_rivers/*/result.json`.
- **Settled and still:** rivers 30.2 fps against gen-1 32.1 fps (about 6 %); GPU 26.5 against 25.4 ms. Nothing river-dependent repeats on the main thread.
- **Streaming:** rivers 23 to 27 fps against 32. Frame p95 78 to 105 ms against 58 to 61 ms. Settle 76 to 88 s against 65 s.

Confirmed causes and fix order:
1. **Install "ocean publish" on the main thread** costs 1.6 to 2.5 times more with rivers. Add sub-scopes first. Fix under L2-3b, judged on a rivers world.
2. **`RiverCarveOperator` tables are rebuilt on every sampler copy** (`RiverCarveOperator.cs`:73-95). A copy costs 1.27 against 0.12 ms, and tree tiles copy 43 to 63 times a second. Build the tables once on the graph.
3. **River mouths:** the operator lowers flanks below 0 m near the coast, and `ClassifyRuntimeWater` floods them as ocean. That is the user's "dry wide channel". Needs a rivers work order.
4. **Inland water is drawn by every level** (the ocean-only replace in `ring_ocean_proxy.gdshader`:343-352). Plus 1.1 ms GPU here, more where more water is in view. Design it together with T2R-12c.
5. **Switch off fine levels by altitude:** 2 to 3 ms GPU per level, in both worlds.
6. **Live Tuning drawer diagnostics** refresh on any tab: 26.7 MB/s of garbage and a GC about every second while the drawer is open. Refresh only on the Diagnostics tab.
7. **Rivers build throughput:** batches are 15 to 30 % slower. Under L2-3a.

Other notes:
- The entry gate timed out (`streaming_settle_timeout`) in every run in both worlds.
- The T2R-12b splat is cleared: about 0.3 s of worker time per session.
