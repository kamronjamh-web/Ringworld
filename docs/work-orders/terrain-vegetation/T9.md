## Phase facts

Verified on branch `gpu-offload`, 2026-11-10, unless marked. Most T9 items build on T2, T3 and T7 code that does not exist yet, so each design WO re-reads the code it depends on.

- F1. **Rivers gate G1** (plan section 11, T2): "Bake within 20 s and zero coverage waits in the travel benchmark over river country, or the GLSL carve moves from T9 into T2." River and lake sectors build on the CPU (plan 3.1, est. 10-20 % of land sectors at the 2 km level). T9b may already have been absorbed by T2; check T2's phase file first.
- F2. **GLSL height model**:
  - `materials/terrain_gpu/ring_height_model.glslinc` ports the natural path only (header lines 1-9).
  - The host's `ProxyGpuHeightKernel.IsSupported` (`scripts/Terrain/Generation/Gpu/ProxyGpuHeightKernel.cs:50-58`) decides GPU eligibility.
  - The `HM_P_*` defines are the buffer contract parsed by the host (`ParseLayout`, lines 118-124).
  - `RC_P_*` occupies 185-316 (`ring_regional_climate.glslinc:16, 36`). New blocks append after 317 (T7 D7) or after whatever T7 appended.
  - K2 builds 8/16/32 m sectors (`materials/terrain_gpu/ring_proxy_sector_k2.glsl`).
- F3. **Drainage**:
  - T0's prototype found channel density 0.034-0.081 km per km² at 25-100 km² thresholds, 98.6 % of channel cells ending at the ocean, and D8 straight-line artefacts on flats (`docs/landscape-audit-t0-spec.md` 8.2).
  - The production graph is T2's (unverified until T2 lands): resident, about 16 MB, with reach hydraulics from T3.
- F4. **Small streams** (plan section 3, ideas table): "Small streams (2-20 m) with real water: contingent (T9). Needs a tile-local network; large." Trunk rivers start at the T2 catchment threshold (est. 50-100 km², plan 3.1).
- F5. **Estuaries and chutes** (plan 3.1): estuaries are "the last reach carved below 0 and the sea floods it". Chutes drop 1 m over 8-12 m. Deltas and waterfall meshes are "polish on top of T2 and T3" (plan section 3, ideas table).
- F6. **Stamps** (plan section 4, rejected alternatives): "Heightmap stamps. Strong, but adds an asset pipeline and repetition visible from altitude. Kept as a T9 option." Badlands go beyond today's "breaks" (`TerrainGenerationHeightModel.cs:275-280`, `BreaksStrength` and `BreaksStepM`).
- F7. **No caves in gen-17**:
  - `scripts/Underground/Terrain3DUndergroundProbeSource.cs:5-7`: "Caves are not ported yet, so density is 'air above the surface height, solid below'".
  - Terrain3D holes already exist for emitter apertures (`scripts/Terrain/Surface/RingTerrain3DApertureMask.cs`; collision hole mask `RingTerrain3DCollisionGeometry.cs:22, 60`). That is the precedent for a surface opening.
  - The gen-16 voxel overhang settings (`scripts/Voxel/Phase3/VoxelTerrainSettings.cs:49-51`) are legacy and are not used by gen-17.
- F8. **Placement** (plan section 8): "Placement stays on the CPU, culling on the GPU. This keeps determinism, the disk cache and collision records. GPU placement is a T9 option for ground cover only." Grass placement is `RingGrassTileBuilder.Build` (`scripts/Voxel/LOD/RingGrassTileBuilder.cs:109`), in CPU tiles of 16-48 m (plan 1.2).
- F9. **Headworks** (plan 3.1): "A great river's source is an outfall, not a spring; T2 needs no art for it, and T9 lists headworks and wall-foot spill fans as options."
  - Wall Engines come from `scripts/Terrain/Sites/ClimateMachineRegistry.cs` and are opt-in through the recipe field `climate_machines` (`TerrainGenerationParameters.cs:52`; default 0, line 99). So spill fans exist only in worlds that turn machines on.
  - POIs go through the `poi-asset-import` skill (`assets/poi/`, the shared `ring_poi_architecture` shader, `RingPoiHaze`, streaming registration).
- F10. **Valley mist**:
  - Plan 10.8: "The drainage graph holds exactly that [a basin floor field]. River-valley mist becomes a small follow-up once T2 and V4 exist."
  - `docs/vastness-plan.md` (lines 169-173):
    - V7a is the mist lid, a logistic mist profile in `ring_haze_shared.gdshaderinc`.
    - Pooling in high basins is deferred: "a low-pass 'basin floor' field from the min-max pyramid sampled in the V4 march".
    - Any edit to `ring_haze_shared.gdshaderinc` forces the Sunshine reimport routine (vastness plan line 403).
  - Light shafts (V4) are deferred (user memory, haze revamp status).
- F11. **Budget**: plan section 12 has no T9 rows, so each design WO proposes its own gates. Set them against the 1.5 ms total for all phases, the 48 draws and the 0.2 ms main-thread budget (plan section 0), and the A1000 headroom risk (plan section 14).

## Order and parallelism

Groups are independent of one another, except that T9c and T9g may share river-source and estuary data from T2. Inside a group the order is: design WO, user go on the design, the implementer WOs as the design orders them, and the freeze if visible. GPU WOs of different groups never run at the same time. T9i has its own table and execution order inside the group.

## The freeze pattern for a visible group

Each visible group ends with a freeze WO (`sol_implementer`, S) that does exactly what T7-14 does:
- the ledger row (generation N = N-1 plus the group's features) and the launcher default;
- the fingerprint row appended with `--capture`;
- a recipe-freeze row if heights changed;
- two or three look goldens per machine;
- `world_generation_contract_test`;
- the test world `g<NN>-<step>-s1337` via `tools/new_generation_world.ps1`;
- review worlds deleted;
- `docs/world-generations.md` updated.

Its validation is `world_generation_fingerprint_test`, `world_generation_contract_test` and `terrain_recipe_freeze_test`, each with a `--golden=` can-fail check. Earlier rows must stay byte-identical. The primary runs `-Suite gate` at the commit point. Two groups accepted in the same review may share one step (plan 2.2).

#### Spec E5. `RingWaterFlow`

```
public readonly record struct WaterFlowSample(InlandWaterKind Kind, double Surface, double Bed, double VelocityAround, double VelocityAcross,
    double Speed, int Span, RiverClass Class, double DangerSpeed, double Whitewater, double ChuteHead);
public interface IWaterFlowSource { WaterFlowSample Sample(double around, double across, double height); }
public sealed class RingWaterFlow : IWaterFlowSource   // RingWaterFlow(RiverGraph graph, RiverCarveOperator carve); double CurrentStrength { get; set; }
```
`height` NaN means the surface. Kind None gives NaN levels and zero velocity. Thread-safe, allocation-free. Cost: "Budgets"; the `Near` bit exits first away from water.

**Rewritten on 2026-11-13.** Against the first version: the surface is the located level; source stems use the head factor of T2-rules.md RS4 in place of a 200 m fade-in; water held in a lake is still apart from inflow jets and the draw at the outlet (the first version faded an outlet stem in over its whole held length, which is now up to 10 km of lake, and drew the jet in open lake water); a stem that ends on a lake cell fades out; the class in the sample follows the local speed at heads and in lakes. Overflow outlets, straits and lake-end joins (T2R-22) need no rule of their own.

**Location.** `RiverCarveOperator.Locate(around, across, 0, ...)` gives the owner core with its stem, reach, s, t, tangent, a, b, d, d_e and drawn level. Where no core owns the point, the lake query (T3R-4) gives the lake and its level where the membership P is above 0. The field has no ground: it does not know islands, the dry ring of a lake or the sea. A caller compares `Surface` with its own ground, and the controller samples only where the sampler reports inland water.

**In a core.**
- `Surface` = the located level (`RiverLocation.Level`: the displayed level with the draw-down at confluences), which is what the sampler shows. `Bed = CoreProfile(|t|, level, a, b, d, d_e)`. Direction = the displaced centreline's tangent, downstream.
- Chute head: `sigma(s) = clamp(L(s - 5) - L(s + 5), 0, 1)`, `B(s) = sigma(s) * max(1, L(s - Ld) - L(s))`, L = `RiverLevel.AtStem`, arguments clamped to the stem. Where the profile is steeper than 10 % the level is a ramp (T2R-7c) and B is the fall over the last 30 m. Just above a lake-end join (T2R-22 item 8f) B reaches tens of metres; the cap below holds the speed.
- Centre speed of a river: `vc(s) = H(s) * min(sqrt(Vp^2 + 2 g eta B(s)), Vchute * fer)`, with Vp and eta of the span, blended as E1.
- Head factor: `H(s) = (s / T)^(4/9)` on a source stem for `s < T = min(1,500, stem length)`, else 1. With the tapered width and depth of `ChannelAt` the discharge then grows as `(s / T)^(5/3)` and `Q = U w d` holds along the taper (RS4).
- Across: `vc * (1 - (t / a)^2)` for `|t| < a`, else 0; a is the located half width, so it is tapered at a head. With depth: times `lerp(0.6, 1, clamp((height - Bed) / (Surface - Bed), 0, 1))`.
- End fades: times `smoothstep(0, 500, s_end - s)` on a stem that ends in the sea or Dead; times `smoothstep(0, 3 w, s_end - s)` on a stem that ends on a lake cell with no drowned channel to carry it on (196 stems on seed 1337). No fade before a confluence.

**Water held in a lake.** A stem i that starts in a lake (`OutletLake[i] >= 0`) is that lake's water for `s <= HeldEnd[i]`: a drowned channel, an in-lake branch, a strait, and the outlet stem up to its outlet point (H29). A lake does not flow like a river. Its water is still, apart from where a river runs in and where the outlet draws. In held water the centre speed is, in place of `vc`:
```
v_held(s, t) = max( draw(s) * (1 - (t / a)^2),  max over inflows j of jet_j(s, t) )
draw(s)      = vc_i(HeldEnd) * exp(-(HeldEnd - s) / (3 w_o))        only where i is the lake's outlet stem; vc_i is the river formula above at HeldEnd, w_o the width there
jet_j(s, t)  = vc_j(end of j) * exp(-x / (3 w_j)) * exp(-(t / (a_j + 0.2 x))^2)        x = s - JunctionS_j >= 0
```
- An inflow j of i is a stem that ends as a Confluence on i inside i's held part and is not itself lake water at its end: a river that arrives from outside the lake. `vc_j`, `w_j` and `a_j` are taken at its end. The inflows of each stem are tabled once per graph. A sample uses at most the four nearest inflows upstream of s and ignores `x > 12 w_j`.
- Both terms are 0 outside the core (`|t| >= a`). There is no chute boost; the level is flat. A jet does not pass from one in-lake stem to the next.
- Continuity: at `s = HeldEnd` the draw equals the river's `vc`, and at a junction the jet starts at the inflow's end speed. Where the span at the end of a hold is the lake's wide, slow one (E1), the draw is gentle and the river picks up speed over the 200 m blend into its first span outside the lake.
- An outlet stem that is lake water to its end and joins a passing river there (T2R-22 (c')) draws toward that junction by the same formula; the step to the receiver's speed is a confluence shear line.
- A strait, an in-lake branch and the in-lake stem of a closed lake have no draw. Without an inflow they are still.
- The lengths `3 w` and the spread 0.2 **(tune at review)**. Still water over most of a lake is the realistic case and the default. A slow current along the drowned channel through the whole lake would show more flow than a lake has; it is question 2 of 2026-11-13 and is not built without the user's yes.

**Open lake water** (no core, P above 0): `Surface` = the lake level, `Bed = NaN`, velocity 0.

**Kinds.** River: a core owns the point and the point is not held. Lake: held water in a core, or open lake water. None otherwise. This is a rule on the arc length. The sampler's rule since PT2-2 is on the membership and the level, so the two can differ within about a cell of a lake's edge (the last metres of an inflow, the first of an outlet). The look follows the sampler and the force follows the field; the field is continuous across the difference.

**Derived values.**
- `Whitewater = max(smoothstep(2.5, 7, Speed / CurrentStrength), min(1, B))` in a river; `smoothstep(2.5, 7, Speed / CurrentStrength)` in held water; 0 in open lake water, and 0 when the strength is 0. `ChuteHead = B`, 0 in held water.
- `Span`, `Class` and `DangerSpeed` are the span's. Inside a head taper `DangerSpeed = Vd * H(s)` and `Class` is the class of that speed (the span arrays are not tapered, H25). In held water `DangerSpeed` is the local centre speed at strength 1 and `Class` its class.
- Every velocity is multiplied by `CurrentStrength`.
- **Checked by** `river_flow_field_test`.

#### Spec E6. Coupling

**Rewritten on 2026-11-13** for the swim depths of T2R-20 (entry 1.15 m, exit 0.9 m, float 1.15 m; H28). The swimming rule is unchanged. The wading rule is new. The first one added `6 * (depth / entry)^2 * (waterVelocity - planarVelocity)` per second to the walking step; against a walking acceleration of 45 m/s^2 it could not move a standing player in any river, and its depth scale was the old entry depth of 0.55 m.

- **Sample.** One per physics frame, only when the player's column is inland water and the player is at or under the water level plus 0.5 m. None in noclip, in flight, in the ocean or without `water.current`. Below, `depth` is `CurrentWaterDepthMeters` (the surface minus the feet) and the water velocity is the sample's at the player's height.
- **Swimming** (`ProcessSwimming`; depth above 1.15 m, or above 0.9 m once swimming): target = input velocity + (VelocityAround, VelocityAcross) on canonical X and Z, approached at `SwimAcceleration`. Float target = `Surface - SwimSurfaceFloatDepthMeters - min(0.6, 0.3 * ChuteHead)` **(tune at G2)**. Where a river is shallower than the float depth the swimmer's feet are on the bed, as today.
- **Wading** (the walking block; depth above 0, not swimming): footing `f = smoothstep(0.6, 1.2, depth * |waterVelocity|)`, depth in m and speed in m/s. The walking target becomes `input velocity + f * waterVelocity`, approached with the existing ground or air acceleration.
  - Under a depth times speed of 0.6 m^2/s the player stands and walks as on land. Above 1.2 m^2/s the player is carried at the water's speed and still walks relative to it.
  - The two numbers are where flood-safety studies put the start and the end of an adult's footing in moving water (about 0.6 to 1.3 m^2/s). They are not chosen for effect **(tune at G2, inside that range only)**.
  - Examples: 0.3 m at 2 m/s holds; 0.3 m at 4 m/s sweeps; 1.0 m at 0.5 m/s holds; 1.0 m at 1.2 m/s sweeps; ankle-deep water never does.
- **Between the two.** At the swim entry a wader in water faster than about 1 m/s is already carried at the water's speed, so entering the swim state changes nothing there. In slower water the swimmer picks up the drift on entering, at most about 0.6 m/s.
- **Never pinned.** The flow has no component across the channel, the input always adds, and the speed is 0 at the channel edge.
- **Wading speed** (question 3 of 2026-11-13; not built without the user's yes). Today the player walks at the full 8 m/s in water up to 1.15 m deep. That is faster than swimming (5.5 m/s), so a wader can outwalk a current that a swimmer cannot hold. If wanted: the input velocity of a wader is multiplied by `lerp(1, 0.5, smoothstep(0.3, 1.15, depth))`, in inland water and at the sea shore alike.
- **Checked by** `river_current_swim_test`.

#### Spec E7. Vertex flow and shader

**Rewritten on 2026-11-13**: the carrier (H33), Lake-kind water (H29), the drift that the flow replaces (H32), and nothing in the cache record.

- **Carrier.** `CUSTOM1` as four half floats (8 bytes per vertex). x and y: the velocity along canonical around and across in m/s at strength 1, which are the axes of the water pattern's coordinates. z: whiteness. w: 0, free. Written for River and Lake vertices from `RingWaterFlow.Sample` at the vertex, at the surface, in the mesh path and in the compact path. Where terrain and water share one vertex array, terrain vertices hold 0 (T3R-9 uses z there). The ocean's `WaterGrid` path, sectors without inland water and worlds without `water.current` carry nothing. `CUSTOM0` stays the parent chord.
- **Not cached.** The values are a pure function of position and the graph and are computed when the mesh is built, as the tint coverage is. The sector cache record and `LodSectorCacheRiverVersion` (13) do not change.
- **Shader**, for inland water (kind above 1.5) when the material's flow switch is on; `RingWaterModel` sets it for worlds with `water.current`. Two phases `p_i = fract(TIME / T + i / 2)`, `T = 3 s` **(tune at review)**. The pattern coordinates of the height, gradient and foam lookups are shifted by `-velocity * ringworld_current_strength * p_i * T` metres, and the two results are blended by `1 - abs(2 p_i - 1)`.
  - River kind: the shift replaces the drift along +x. The pattern's slow change in place stays, so a river at current strength 0 shimmers and does not drift. **This is the ripple drift direction the user left to T3**: the ripples go where the water goes, at its speed.
  - Lake kind: the shift is added to the Lake pattern. Where the lake is still it is 0 and both phases are the same picture.
  - Without the switch the existing path runs unchanged, the +x drift included (legacy rivers, and worlds with `water.rivers` alone).
- **Foam.** Coverage = the vertex whiteness times `min(1, ringworld_current_strength)`, through the existing foam patch pattern, added to the existing foam term ahead of the inland composition (H32), so foam hides the bed as it does at sea. Inland water still has no shore foam.
- **Footprint.** The animation fades between pixel footprints of 1 m and 4 m; foam coverage stays. Proxy levels of 16 m and coarser write `smoothstep(2.5, 7, DangerSpeed)` of the sample in place of the local whiteness, so chutes do not alias.
- **What a level can show.** A level holds a river as water only where `w >= 2 * spacing` (T2.md S13.2), and the vertex flow exists only there. A narrower river is a tint on that level (E8).
- **Checked by** `river_flow_visual_parity_test`.

#### Spec E8. Whitewater on the tint

**Rewritten on 2026-11-13**: the whiteness also rides on the proxy tint (H34). The roar and the calm layer that this section also held are replaced by T3.md Spec W1 to W9 (the water sound), which need no flow field.

- **White share of the tint.** Where a level shows a river as a tint, it also carries the white part of that area: `c_w(v) = sum over samples of area * bilinear weight * wbar / spacing^2`, from the same splat as the coverage `c(v)` of T2.md S11b. The shader mixes the tint from the river colour toward foam white (0.92) by `c_w / c`. It is the true white area; there is no minimum.
  - Slot levels (8, 16, 32 m): `attr.w` of terrain lattice texels holds c and c_w as two 16-bit fractions, in place of the float bits of c.
  - Mesh levels (64 m and coarser): c stays in `UV2.y`; `c_w / c` goes into `CUSTOM1.z` of terrain lattice vertices (E7's array, added on sectors with river coverage).
  - Far tier (global LOD, far viewport): per texel in the alpha of the surface tile normal (reserved at 255 by T2R-12), per vertex in the channel V9 finds. The single `river_whitewater` uniform goes.
  - Only with `water.current`. Without it every carrier holds what it holds today.
- **Checked by** `river_far_water_fraction_test`.

#### Spec E9. Current strength

Key `current_strength`, range 0 to 2, default 1, Water tab, owner `GameplayWaterDefaults`. It sets `RingWaterFlow.CurrentStrength` and the shader global `ringworld_current_strength`, which scale the push and the ripple drift together. At 0 the water is still for physics and visuals alike, and the foam is off. It does not change the class, the geometry or the roar. **Checked by** `water_settings_smoke_test`.

#### Spec E10. Chute query

`readonly record struct RiverChute(double Around, double Across, int Span, float Head, float HalfWidth, float TangentAround, float TangentAcross)`. A chute is a whole-metre crossing of the stored profile; its position is the crossing (the chute's downstream end) on the displaced centreline; `Head` is `B` there. Enumerated for the pieces of the buckets that overlap the rectangle, ordered by (stem, s). Added on 2026-11-13: `HalfWidth` is `ChannelAt`'s, so it is tapered on a source stem; the held part of a stem has no chutes; where the profile is steeper than 10 % the crossings lie less than 10 m apart on a ramp and each is still listed, with the head of the last 30 m. **Checked by** `river_chute_hook_test`.

#### Spec F4. Manager and catalog

Radius 300 m; at most 32 awake bodies, nearest first; the rest sleep in place and are hidden beyond 600 m; nothing is saved. Cost gate 0.1 ms at the cap. Catalog row: scene path, spawn rule, density, allowed classes; no rows ship. **Checked by** `floating_item_validation_test`.
